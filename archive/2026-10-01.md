## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Supreme Court will review nationwide no-bond immigration-detention policy

In Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, the government asked the Court to reverse a Second Circuit ruling that a Brazilian man who had lived in the United States for about two decades could seek a bond hearing. The dispute is whether people already living here without formal admission may be treated as “applicants for admission” subject to mandatory detention throughout removal proceedings. The Court granted certiorari, meaning it will decide the merits in the new term; it did not rule that the policy is lawful. The case could settle a circuit split affecting thousands of detainees.

[Official case docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-104.html) · [Associated Press explainer](https://apnews.com/article/f8ee63bcb11f1be3d7d6300022f3bc88)

### Iran says it received Washington’s response to a war-ending proposal

Iranian officials said mediators delivered a formal U.S. response to Tehran’s proposal for ending the seven-month war and reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Neither government published the text, and no ceasefire has been agreed. The exchange is a concrete diplomatic step, but reported gaps over sanctions, frozen assets and the port blockade remain large.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/449f9e378b8c9bc66fe36712efdfa6b5) · [Reuters analysis (potentially limited)](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-readies-harder-retaliation-if-attacked-diplomacy-faces-long-odds-2026-10-01/)

### Russia issues a nuclear warning over any attempt to isolate Kaliningrad

Moscow warned NATO that it could use nuclear weapons if the alliance tried to cut off or attack the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad. NATO said it has no blockade plan and called the rhetoric irresponsible. The development is a serious escalation in signaling, not evidence that an attack or blockade is under way.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/2bd21dbaf9d67f5e2aada61e9ce8bf06) · [Reuters background (potentially limited)](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/why-russias-kaliningrad-is-potential-flashpoint-with-nato-2026-09-30/)

### CMS finalizes a five-year Part B drug-pricing test

The mandatory GLOBE Model will use international price information to recalculate inflation rebates for selected clinician-administered drugs. It starts January 1, 2027 in randomly selected areas covering about 25% of Original Medicare beneficiaries; affected patients could see lower out-of-pocket costs beginning April 1. Biosimilars, orphan-only drugs, plasma products and certain cell and gene therapies are excluded.

[CMS final rule summary](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-finalizes-new-mandatory-drug-payment-model-deliver-lower-drug-prices-beneficiaries-original)

### New federal-match limits for some lawfully present noncitizens take effect today

A 2025 law narrows the noncitizen categories eligible for federally matched full Medicaid and CHIP beginning October 1. Emergency treatment remains federally matchable, and states may still cover lawfully residing children and pregnant people under the existing option. Eligibility varies by status and state funding, so an individual notice—not a headline—controls what happens next.

[CMS implementation guidance](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-issues-guidance-implement-new-limits-federal-medicaid-chip-funding-certain-noncitizens) · [Detailed CMS letter (PDF)](https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/sho26001.pdf)

## Most Useful Development Today

### New SSA ruling explains how disability evidence should be evaluated for young adults

SSR 26-2p takes effect today for adult disability claims, age-18 redeterminations and continuing-disability reviews involving people roughly ages 18–25. It keeps the adult disability standard but emphasizes the full record: medical evidence, school records, IEPs and transition plans, accommodations, vocational-rehabilitation material, and observations from family, teachers and employers. Practical takeaway: preserve and submit records that show how symptoms affect sustained attendance, pace, adaptation, self-care and work-like functioning—not only diagnoses and grades.

[Official SSA ruling](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/rulings/di/01/SSR2026-02-di-01.html) · [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/01/2026-17814/social-security-ruling-ssr-26-2p-titles-ii-and-xvi-documenting-and-evaluating-disability-in-young)

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Certiorari grant puts mandatory detention policy on the merits docket

ICE detained Ricardo Barbosa da Cunha under a July 2025 policy treating people present without formal admission as applicants for admission who cannot seek bond. He argues the ordinary detention statute allows an immigration judge to consider release; the government says detention is mandatory. The Second Circuit sided with him. By granting certiorari, the Supreme Court agreed to review that ruling; it did not stay a judgment here or decide the policy’s legality. Briefing and argument come next, with a merits decision expected later in the term.

[Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-104.html) · [DOJ petition page](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/rhoney-v-barbosa-da-cunha) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/f8ee63bcb11f1be3d7d6300022f3bc88)

### Second judge blocks the $100,000 fee for new H-1B petitions

In Global Nurse Force v. Trump, a Northern District of California judge vacated agency policies implementing the fee and barred enforcement while required notice-and-comment procedures are completed. The ruling rests on Administrative Procedure Act process grounds and follows a separate Massachusetts injunction; the government can appeal.

[Readable case summary](https://immigration-analytics.com/2026/10/01/federal-court-vacates-100k-h-1b-fee-policies-in-global-nurse-force-v-trump/) · [Reuters (potentially limited)](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/second-judge-blocks-trumps-100000-fee-new-h-1b-worker-visas-2026-10-01/)

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### Extended Paxlovid did not improve Long COVID symptoms in RECOVER-VITAL

In a randomized, double-blind phase 2 trial of 959 adults at 69 U.S. sites, 15 or 25 days of nirmatrelvir–ritonavir did not outperform ritonavir plus placebo across cognitive, autonomic or exercise-intolerance symptom groups over six months. Treatment was generally tolerated. The result argues against this antiviral strategy for established Long COVID; it does not show that viral persistence is irrelevant or that other treatments cannot work.

[NIH summary](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/antiviral-drug-did-not-improve-long-covid-symptoms) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42673984/)

### New Free Journal Articles

#### [Smartphone sit-to-stand video shows promise as a Parkinson’s functional biomarker](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-026-02166-5)

Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation · online October 1, 2026 · peer-reviewed open-access observational machine-learning study. Researchers analyzed smartphone video from 32 people with Parkinson’s disease, reconstructed center-of-gravity trajectories and identified three movement clusters associated with age, balance, functional independence and motor severity; the simplified recurrent neural network classified patterns with 81.2% accuracy. Why it matters: ordinary video could support remote functional assessment. Limitation: the sample was small, lacked an external control or validation cohort, and the method is not a clinical diagnostic.

[Free full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-026-02166-5)

#### [Central cord syndrome study maps cortical changes linked to upper-limb impairment](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-026-02154-9)

Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation · online September 30, 2026 · peer-reviewed open-access cross-sectional human study. Functional near-infrared spectroscopy compared 20 people with traumatic central cord syndrome, 20 with asymptomatic cervical cord compression and 20 healthy controls. The traumatic group showed lower network connectivity and altered SMA/DLPFC activation correlated with worse NDI and mJOA scores; asymptomatic compression resembled controls. Limitation: the small cross-sectional design supports association, not causation, and suggested brain-stimulation targets are not treatment evidence.

[Free full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-026-02154-9)
