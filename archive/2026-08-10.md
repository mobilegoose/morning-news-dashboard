## 1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

### 1. A magnitude-7.4 earthquake caused serious damage in western Colombia

The quake struck near San José del Palmar in Chocó at a depth of about 107 kilometers. Officials reported injuries and damaged or collapsed buildings in Quibdó, Cali, Pereira and Manizales; the shaking was felt in Bogotá and Ecuador. **Why it matters:** damage reports were still developing this morning, and the depth and broad felt area mean assessments may change. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/26fd40f93272d834fced47a4a673edc9) · [USGS significant-earthquake list](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/browse/significant.php)

### 2. A Ukrainian drone attack killed 13 people in a Russian oil hub

Russian officials said the strike on Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan killed 13 people, including a child, and wounded dozens; fires were reported at the TANECO refinery and another industrial site. Ukraine did not immediately give a detailed account of the strike. **Why it matters:** it was among the deadliest Ukrainian attacks inside Russia, and it extends Kyiv's campaign against oil infrastructure far from the front. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-drones-oil-d0e7dc74936cf153e8e473444cc09366) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/ukraine-kills-12-drone-strikes-russian-city-nizhnekamsk-state-media-reports-2026-08-10/)

### 3. Tropical Storm Dolphin flooded eastern China after deadly rains in the Philippines

Dolphin weakened after landfall, but authorities warned of flooding and landslides. More than 300,000 people were moved before the storm and Shanghai canceled more than 1,300 flights; storm-enhanced monsoon rains raised the Philippines death toll to 15 and displaced about 98,000 people. **Why it matters:** the same weather system is disrupting a major aviation hub while driving a multi-country disaster response. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/tropical-storm-dolphin-china-taiwan-philippines-8d9e687f8599d12f050daeca261d7fa2)

### 4. WHO said Congo's Ebola outbreak began months before its declaration

WHO's Africa regional director said early cases were mistaken for malaria or typhoid and the Bundibugyo-virus outbreak likely began in February, not May. More than 4,200 cases and 1,900 deaths have now been reported, with no approved vaccine or specific treatment for this species. **Why it matters:** the delayed start date helps explain why contact tracing is behind a fast-moving outbreak and underscores the need for earlier diagnosis. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/c4f179c021836ee2c9319e660010452a) · [WHO outbreak page](https://www.who.int/emergencies/situations/ebola-outbreak---drc-2026)

### 5. Wildfires forced more than 20,000 people from homes in British Columbia

British Columbia declared an emergency as the Bald Range fire drove evacuations around Summerland and Peachland; an 80-year-old woman died while trying to escape. In Washington, three Spokane-area fires had damaged or destroyed about 920 structures, while two helicopter pilots died fighting a Utah fire. **Why it matters:** fire danger spans the western U.S. and Canada, with displacement, smoke exposure and responder deaths continuing even where some perimeters have stabilized. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/western-wildfires-canada-us-946f5599db59517cb88fd3f0df9f5c46)

## 2. Most Useful Development Today

### England issued amber heat-health alerts for most regions

UKHSA's alerts run from Tuesday morning through Friday evening across much of England. Amber means health effects are likely across the population, with greater risk for older adults, people with chronic conditions and those who live alone. **Useful takeaway:** use the official regional dashboard for the current alert, check on vulnerable people, limit strenuous activity during peak heat and plan for warm nights as well as daytime highs. ### New Free Journal Articles

#### [Molecular genetic and pregnancy outcomes of fetuses with increased nuchal translucency](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13450768/)

*PLOS One* · electronically published August 7, 2026 · **Peer-reviewed retrospective single-center cohort.** Researchers studied 180 pregnant women with fetal nuchal translucency of at least 2.5 mm, selected from a 1,678-pregnancy cohort, using karyotyping and SNP-array testing. Karyotyping found 24 chromosomal abnormalities; the array identified additional copy-number variants, including pathogenic, likely pathogenic and uncertain findings. **Why it matters:** increased nuchal translucency can accompany severe genetic conditions, and the study supports microarray as a complement to karyotyping rather than a replacement for counseling. **Material limitations:** retrospective referral sample, one Chinese center, inconsistent cutoff standards and limited postnatal follow-up; uncertain variants must not be treated as diagnoses. [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13450768/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42566411/)

#### [Clonal LIMD1 loss drives PD-L1 immune evasion via ARIH1-dependent ubiquitination in lung cancer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13451150/)

*Life Science Alliance* · electronically published August 7, 2026 · **Peer-reviewed laboratory and translational study.** Cell-line, primary-airway-cell and co-culture experiments found that loss of the tumor suppressor LIMD1 stabilized PD-L1 and suppressed CD8 T-cell activation. In the 421-patient TRACERx non-small-cell lung-cancer cohort, clonal LIMD1 loss appeared in about 40% of lung adenocarcinomas and was associated with higher PD-L1; low expression was enriched among immunotherapy responders in independent cohorts. **Why it matters:** the mechanism may help refine future biomarker research for checkpoint therapy. **Material limitations:** much of the evidence is laboratory-based, patient analyses are observational, and no test or treatment decision is validated for clinical use. [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13451150/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42567684/)
