### FDA orders an eye-risk warning on every GLP-1 drug label

FDA is requiring all GLP-1 receptor-agonist labels—including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound—to warn about a possible increased risk of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, a rare condition that can cause sudden, painless and sometimes permanent vision loss. FDA's pooled trial analysis found 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years among GLP-1 users versus 3.8 among comparators; separate observational comparisons were mixed. Patients with sudden painless vision loss should seek prompt care. FDA did not advise everyone to stop treatment automatically.

[FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential)

The relationship and board review are confirmed; spying or compromise has not been established.

### Justices send three cases back without deciding their merits

The Court granted review, vacated the lower-court judgments and remanded three matters for reconsideration under intervening precedent. In Roybal v. ## Most Useful Development Today

### CMS finalizes telehealth, remote-monitoring and rural-service changes

CMS issued the 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule, effective January 1. It extends the waiver of the in-person requirement for mental-health telehealth through December 31, 2027; adds a shared-medical-appointment code; permits certain diabetes self-management and nutrition services as stand-alone rural-clinic visits; and finalizes remote-monitoring requirements, while delaying a staff-employment restriction until 2029. The projected conversion factor falls 1.83% for qualifying APM clinicians and 2.31% for others, largely because a temporary 2.5% increase expires. Practical next move: confirm that a 2027 clinician, telehealth arrangement and remote-monitoring vendor will remain covered before care begins in January.

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Three GVRs require lower courts to redo their analyses under newer precedent

Roybal v. Griffith concerns a transgender woman who alleged that a Colorado jail housed her in a male unit, denied gender-appropriate items and subjected her to a degrading strip search. The Court vacated the Tenth Circuit judgment and remanded for reconsideration under newer Supreme Court rulings on sex classifications affecting transgender people; it did not resolve the constitutional claims. Harrington v. Martin concerns whether an Indiana prosecutor is immune from damages after allegedly sharing Brady/Giglio material in a way that violated rights. The Court remanded for application of a newer, fact-specific qualified-immunity framework; it did not decide liability. Cid v. United States concerns a $6.55 million criminal forfeiture and an appeal waiver. The Court remanded so the Eleventh Circuit can reconsider whether enforcing the waiver would produce a miscarriage of justice; it did not invalidate the forfeiture. A final same-morning check found no November 9 merits opinion or separate miscellaneous emergency order beyond the order list.

[Official order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/110926zor_ap6c.pdf) · [Roybal docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-442.html) · [Roybal appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca10/23-1135/23-1135-2025-02-19.html) · [Harrington docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1348.html) · [Cid petition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-5123/416699/20260716163643379_CID.PETITION.pdf)

### Judge orders a screened response to ICE's Bronx-shelter subpoena

U.S. District Judge Lewis Kaplan granted the government's petition to enforce an ICE administrative subpoena but required a special master to screen the records. The federal government is not receiving every shelter resident's name directly: the order calls for disclosure only of people matching specified serious-criminal-history criteria, with the parties to propose a special-master process. ### CMS raises aggregate 2027 home-health payments by an estimated 2.4%

CMS finalized a projected $415 million increase in aggregate Medicare home-health payments for 2027, combining the annual update with other adjustments, including a temporary 3% base-rate reduction. The rule also changes provider-enrollment and revocation policies, clarifies replacement rules for certain durable medical equipment and expands coverage rules for some home-infusion pumps and drugs. Aggregate growth does not mean every agency receives a 2.4% increase; case mix, geography and compliance factors affect actual payments.

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### Sudden painless vision loss is the key symptom in FDA's GLP-1 warning

FDA's action covers the entire GLP-1 receptor-agonist class. The agency found a small absolute rate of NAION in trials and inconsistent observational comparisons, so the warning describes a potential association rather than proven causation. Patients should seek urgent evaluation for sudden painless vision loss or rapidly worsening vision; clinicians should arrange prompt ophthalmology evaluation and weigh discontinuation if NAION develops.

[FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential)

### New Free Journal Articles

### [Pilot trial finds immune-microenvironment changes after JAK1 inhibition](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2162402X.2026.2755668)

OncoImmunology published a peer-reviewed prospective pilot study of eight people with leiomyosarcoma treated with itacitinib, a JAK1 inhibitor, with serial tumor biopsies. Six of eight had stable disease; estimated three-month progression-free survival was 75% (95% CI, 50%–100%), falling to 25% at six months. Biopsies showed fewer M2-like macrophages but also less inflammatory infiltration, and no clear inflammatory antitumor response. Why it matters: it provides human tissue evidence about a possible tumor-microenvironment target in a rare cancer. Limitations are decisive: only eight patients, no randomized comparator, very wide confidence intervals and no evidence that this is an established treatment.

[Free full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2162402X.2026.2755668) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42992600/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/2162402X.2026.2755668)
