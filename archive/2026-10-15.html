Top 5 Things to Know Today

Ranked by broad public importance

1 · U.S. INFLATION

Producer prices jump 1.3% in September, the sharpest monthly rise since 2022

The Labor Department reported that final-demand producer prices rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% over 12 months, the largest monthly and annual increases since March and December 2022, respectively. Energy prices rose 10.8%, while an index excluding food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% for the month. The release measures prices received by producers—not household inflation—but it signals renewed cost pressure that can feed into consumer prices and interest-rate decisions.

Official BLS Producer Price Index

2 · SUPREME COURT

Supreme Court freezes an order that would have forced a quick FCC ruling on political-ad discounts

In FCC v. Brown, the FCC asked the Court to pause a Fourth Circuit order requiring it to rule within 48 hours on Democratic candidates' challenge to an FCC policy extending low broadcast-ad rates to party-coordinated ads and joint fundraising committees. The Court granted the stay Wednesday, so the appellate deadline is suspended while the government seeks Supreme Court review; this does not decide whether the FCC policy is lawful. The stay ends automatically if certiorari is denied, or when judgment issues if review is granted. Justices Sotomayor and Jackson would have denied relief, and the delay is likely to keep the dispute unresolved through the November 3 midterms.

Official docket and orderReadable Roll Call report

3 · UKRAINE

Ukraine resumes strikes on Russian refineries as the informal energy pause unravels

Ukraine said it struck Russia's Ryazan refinery after Russian attacks continued despite a loosely described pause on energy infrastructure. Ukrainian officials also said a Russian strike in the Kirovohrad region killed three people and wounded six, and that more than 850 Russian drones were launched overnight. The renewed refinery campaign marks a concrete breakdown in the attempted restraint and comes as Kyiv reports growing strain in its relationship with Washington over intelligence and Starlink access.

Associated PressReuters companion

4 · PAKISTAN

School roof collapse in Peshawar kills 34 children and a teacher

Two classroom roofs collapsed at a private school in Peshawar, killing 35 people—34 children and a teacher—and injuring more than 50, according to Pakistani authorities. Police arrested the school's owner, and provincial officials opened an investigation into possible structural weakness following recent rain. The scale of the deaths makes the collapse one of today's most consequential safety failures.

Associated Press

5 · NORTH KOREA

North Korea tests submarine-launched cruise missiles for the first time in nearly three years

North Korea said it fired two types of strategic cruise missiles from its Hero Kim Kun Ok submarine, while South Korea's military said the launches appeared to use both vertical and torpedo tubes. The tests do not establish that the system is fully operational, but they indicate progress in an undersea launch capability designed to make detection and interception harder.

Associated Press

Most Useful Development Today

A coverage notice that may require action

ACA COVERAGE · ACTIONABLE

CMS is ending Marketplace subsidies for records flagged as duplicate coverage or deceased—appeals remain available

CMS said it identified 189,000 records with subsidized federal Marketplace coverage plus Medicare, Medicaid or CHIP coverage, or a death record. People identified as dually enrolled receive 30 days to dispute the match before subsidies end; even after that window, they may appeal or show that Medicaid or CHIP coverage is about to lapse and regain subsidies. Anyone receiving such a notice should compare the listed programs, correct any data error promptly, and keep proof of an appeal. The agency's projected savings are estimates, and the data match itself is not proof that every flagged record is improper.

U.S. ECONOMY

Energy and services both contribute to September's producer-price surge

Producer prices for goods rose 2.6% in September and services rose 0.8%. The energy index's 10.8% increase was the largest single pressure in the goods category, but the 0.5% rise in the measure excluding food, energy and trade services shows the acceleration was broader than fuel alone. The data arrive one day after the consumer-price report and will be watched for evidence that price pressure is moving through supply chains.

Official BLS release

VOTING · FEDERAL-STATE DISPUTE

Justice Department gives California 48 hours to review a federal list of alleged noncitizen voters

The Justice Department delivered California and Los Angeles County a thumb drive containing names it says are likely noncitizens who received mail ballots, then demanded that officials verify and segregate unresolved ballots within 48 hours or face possible emergency litigation. California officials say only citizens are registered and raised concerns about the deadline and data security. The federal list is an allegation requiring verification—not evidence that the named people voted unlawfully.

Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning court check included

SUPREME COURT · EMERGENCY STAY

FCC v. Brown: the Court pauses the lower court's deadline, not the underlying challenge

Democratic candidates Sherrod Brown, Jon Ossoff, Roy Cooper and Kristen McDonald Rivet asked the FCC to reconsider guidance that lets party-coordinated ads and joint fundraising committees use the lowest broadcast rates ordinarily guaranteed to candidates. After the Fourth Circuit ordered the agency to act quickly, the FCC asked the Supreme Court for a stay. The justices granted that procedural relief on October 14, suspending the appellate mandate while a certiorari petition is filed and resolved; they did not uphold the FCC policy on the merits. If review is denied, the stay ends automatically; if granted, it lasts until the Supreme Court's judgment. The immediate practical effect is delay likely extending beyond the midterms.

FEDERAL APPEALS COURT · CHILDREN ONLINE

Appeals court lets Florida enforce its social-media age restrictions while the lawsuit continues

A unanimous Eleventh Circuit panel reversed a preliminary injunction that had blocked Florida's law barring social-media accounts for children under 14 and requiring parental consent for ages 14 and 15. The panel concluded the state was likely regulating platform design and age access in a content-neutral way. This is an appellate ruling on interim relief, not a final judgment on constitutionality; the underlying case returns to the district court as enforcement proceeds.

Policy & Benefits

Immigration, healthcare access and voting

IMMIGRATION · EMPLOYMENT AUTHORIZATION

DHS finalizes the end of automatic extensions for many timely filed EAD renewals

A final rule published October 14 adopts without change the October 2025 interim rule ending automatic Employment Authorization Document extensions in certain renewal categories. Unless a statute or a separate Federal Register notice provides otherwise, a renewal application filed on or after October 30, 2025 does not automatically extend an expiring EAD; previously granted extensions and certain Temporary Protected Status notices are not canceled. DHS estimates 20,000 to 33,000 EADs could lapse during one modeled 18-month period beginning in May 2027, which may interrupt work authorization while renewals are pending.

HEALTH COVERAGE · PROGRAM INTEGRITY

CMS says it ended or is ending subsidies tied to 189,000 duplicate or deceased-enrollee records

CMS said the 2026 review found about 120,000 Medicaid duplicates, 58,000 Medicare duplicates and roughly 11,000 records matching deaths, with additional actions expected by month-end. The agency projects approximately $738 million in 2026 savings and $1.5 billion over the next year. Those are agency estimates; affected living enrollees can dispute the underlying match within 30 days and appeal afterward.

Official CMS announcementReuters companion

VOTING RIGHTS · DATA REVIEW

California contests DOJ's rapid ballot-screening demand

State officials are reviewing a federal list of people alleged to be potential noncitizens while disputing the Justice Department's 48-hour demand and threat of litigation. The episode matters beyond California because it tests how federal agencies and states handle citizenship data, mail ballots and due process close to an election. No court has yet ruled on the demand, and the names have not been independently verified as ineligible voters.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

New evidence with explicit limits

METABOLIC HEALTH · HUMAN ASSOCIATION PLUS PRECLINICAL WORK

NIH-backed study links active brown fat to cardiometabolic health and tests losartan in cells and mice

Researchers analyzed oncology PET/CT records from more than 100,000 patients and found that detectable brown-fat activity was associated with more favorable cardiometabolic measures and lower recorded anxiety and depression; people taking angiotensin-receptor blockers were more likely to show activity. Losartan increased metabolism in cultured brown-fat cells and healthy mice. The human evidence is observational and the mechanistic work is preclinical, so the study does not show that losartan treats obesity, depression or anxiety and is not a reason to change medication without a clinician.

NIH research summaryNature article

CLIMATE SCIENCE · RAPID ATTRIBUTION

Rapid analysis estimates climate change and El Niño strengthened Hurricane Polo

A World Weather Attribution and Climate Central analysis estimated that human-caused warming added about 6 mph to Polo's peak winds and El Niño about 5 mph, while making the storm's enabling conditions substantially more likely. The analysis is a rapid attribution study rather than a peer-reviewed paper, and its estimates depend on climate models and counterfactual assumptions. It is useful for understanding contributors to intensity, not for assigning all damage to a single cause.

Associated Press report
