1 · AVIATION · NATIONWIDE DISRUPTION

FAA ground stop causes thousands of weekend flight cancellations

A failure in the Federal Aviation Administration's aging air-traffic-control technology triggered a nationwide ground stop Friday lasting more than four hours—the longest such stop in 25 years. More than 2,500 Saturday flights were canceled and hundreds delayed, with international knock-on effects. The FAA said all aircraft already airborne landed safely and no one was endangered, but carriers warned that recovery could take days.

Associated Press

2 · SUPREME COURT · ASYLUM

Supreme Court agrees to review the administration's border-as-invasion asylum ban

In Mullin v. RAICES, the administration asks the Court to reverse lower-court rulings that rejected President Trump's January 2025 proclamation suspending access to asylum and related protections on the theory that unlawful migration constitutes an invasion. The Court granted certiorari Friday on two questions, meaning it will decide the merits—not that it has upheld the policy. The challenged policy remains in force during the litigation; argument is expected this spring, with a decision likely by late June.

3 · MEDICAID · WORK REQUIREMENTS

CMS offers states a six-month transition that can prevent work-rule disenrollments

CMS told states they may use a hold-harmless period through June 30, 2027, as new Medicaid community-engagement requirements begin January 1. Participating states must still assess compliance and exemptions, but may not deny or terminate otherwise eligible people solely for failing the 80-hours-per-month rule during the transition. The option gives states time to test notices and data systems, but it is not an automatic nationwide delay; each state must elect and implement it.

Iran reports three tankers struck mines; Israeli strike kills two in Gaza

Iranian state television said three oil tankers hit naval mines and caught fire near the Strait of Hormuz; it did not identify the vessels' nationalities or report casualties, and independent confirmation was limited at the cutoff. 5 · FEDERAL APPEALS COURT · NEW YORK

Second Circuit restores nearly $74 million in highway funds withheld from New York

In State of New York v. Department of Transportation, the Second Circuit set aside the federal government's decision to withhold nearly $74 million in 2027 highway funds over New York's refusal to revoke about 33,000 non-domiciled commercial driver's licenses after holders lost immigration status. The court held that the rules in force did not impose the continuing cancellation duty the agency asserted, making the funding decision arbitrary and contrary to law. The judgment removes that withholding determination; the agency may still act under lawful rules and procedures.

MEDICAL EQUIPMENT SAFETY · BEDS AND RAILS

Medline expands its repair-and-replacement program for recalled beds and rails

Medline updated customers and patients on a recall covering all Medline-brand beds, rails, mattresses and related accessories because of potential fire and entrapment hazards that can cause serious injury or death. Supreme Court & Legal

The asylum grant, an appellate ruling and the final Court check

CERTIORARI GRANT · MULLIN v. RAICES

What the asylum cert grant means—and what it does not mean

The Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services and other groups sued over a presidential proclamation that treats the southern border situation as an invasion and suspends statutory asylum and related protections. The administration, led on the docket by DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, asked the Supreme Court to reverse the D.C. Circuit rulings favoring the challengers. Friday's grant limited review to the first two questions in the petition; it sets the case for briefing and argument but does not approve the proclamation. The Court will decide whether the President may suspend these statutory protections and whether courts may review that action, with a merits ruling likely by the end of the term.

ADMINISTRATIVE LAW · STATE OF NEW YORK v. DOT

Second Circuit rejects DOT's highway-funding penalty

New York challenged the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's decision to withhold highway money unless the state canceled thousands of commercial licenses issued to people who later lost lawful status. The court granted New York's petition for review and vacated the agency determination because the cited regulations did not create the continuing revocation obligation the agency claimed. Procedurally, the federal withholding decision is set aside; this is a merits ruling on that agency action, not a blanket right to retain a commercial license regardless of current law.

States can postpone adverse action while testing Medicaid work-rule systems

The CMS option matters most at the renewal and notice stages: a participating state continues checking work, volunteer activity and exemptions, but does not close an otherwise eligible case solely for noncompliance before July 1, 2027. EMPLOYER HEALTH PLANS · PROVIDER DIRECTORIES

Labor Department tells plan fiduciaries to address inaccurate provider directories

The Employee Benefits Security Administration issued an enforcement alert on so-called ghost networks—directories listing clinicians who are unavailable, no longer in network or otherwise inaccurate. The department says employers and other plan fiduciaries remain responsible for prudent monitoring even when a vendor maintains the directory. The alert is guidance and an enforcement signal, not a new regulation; it gives participants a useful basis for documenting failed contacts, unexpected out-of-network costs and complaints.

New Free Journal Articles

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · CERVICAL MYELOPATHY · NOVEMBER 12

Expert review finds sufficient uncertainty to justify a laminoplasty-versus-fusion trial

Neurosurgery Practice published a peer-reviewed feasibility study in which 26 cervical-spine experts reviewed 50 trial cases twice, six months apart—2,600 total responses. A majority considered 46 of 50 cases (92%) suitable for randomization between laminoplasty and fusion, supporting the feasibility of a future comparative trial. Why it matters: cervical spondylotic myelopathy can profoundly affect function, yet procedure choice lacks strong randomized evidence. Major limitation: this was expert judgment from imaging and case information, not patient outcomes; it does not show that either operation is safer or more effective. Published online November 12, 2026.
