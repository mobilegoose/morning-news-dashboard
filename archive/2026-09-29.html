Top 5 Things to Know Today

Broad public importance

1 · FEDERAL POWER & ELECTIONS

Judge blocks election conditions attached to counterterrorism grants

U.S. District Judge Amir Ali granted summary judgment to six cities and counties and struck down DHS and FEMA conditions that would have withheld 20% of more than $1 billion in security grants unless jurisdictions changed voter-roll checks, voting equipment and audit practices. He found Congress had not authorized those election rules and that they were insufficiently related to the anti-terror grants. The ruling protects public-safety funding while reinforcing state and local authority over election administration; the government may appeal.

Hurricane Polo makes Category 2 landfall in Baja California Sur

Polo struck just south of Las Barrancas early Tuesday with maximum sustained winds of 110 mph, then weakened while crossing the peninsula. Forecasters still warned of 6–12 inches of rain, flash flooding and dangerous winds, with remnants capable of bringing heavy rain and flooding to parts of the U.S. Southwest later this week.

3 · U.S.–CANADA TRADE

U.S. ban on nearly $1 billion of Canadian imports takes effect

The prohibition began at 12:01 a.m. Tuesday and covers Canadian alcoholic beverages, some dairy products and motorcycles. Its direct economic footprint—about $967 million using 2025 trade data—is modest relative to annual bilateral trade, but it is a fresh escalation after reciprocal tariffs and could complicate renewal of the U.S.–Mexico–Canada Agreement.

4 · YEMEN & RED SEA

U.N. envoy opens talks after Yemen’s ceasefire collapse and Houthi advance

U.N. envoy Hans Grundberg met the Houthis’ chief negotiator in Muscat and Saudi-backed officials in Riyadh to try to prevent further escalation. The Houthis’ seizure of Mokha and Red Sea islands ended the 2022 ceasefire; the renewed fighting has killed at least 838 people, wounded more than 3,640 and displaced over 145,000, while extending risk toward the Bab el-Mandeb shipping lane.

Associated Press

5 · RARE-DISEASE TREATMENT

FDA approves the first treatment for MCT8 deficiency

The agency approved Emcitate (tiratricol) for peripheral thyrotoxicosis in adults and children with MCT8 deficiency, also called Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome. The rare X-linked disorder prevents thyroid hormone from entering the brain while leaving excessive hormone in the bloodstream. In randomized and longer-term studies, treatment lowered excess hormone and improved heart-rate, blood-pressure and metabolic measures; it does not reverse the underlying neurological injury.

Most Useful Development Today

Actionable health information

MOST USEFUL · MCT8 DEFICIENCY

Emcitate can be given by mouth or feeding tube—but has important medication limits

Emcitate is taken once daily as a liquid suspension, orally or through a feeding tube. The FDA lists diarrhea, vomiting, rash and excessive sweating as the most common adverse effects and says it should not be used with another thyroid medication. Practical step: families affected by MCT8 deficiency can ask their endocrinology, neurology or metabolic specialist about eligibility, access and medication reconciliation; the approval treats excess circulating thyroid hormone, not every neurological feature of the disorder.

SUPREME COURT APPLICATION

Administration seeks emergency permission to enforce prison gender-care limits

Three transgender federal prisoners challenged a Bureau of Prisons policy that ends hormone treatment, surgeries and appearance-related accommodations while preserving mental-health services. U.S. District Judge Royce Lamberth blocked the policy for the class after finding it likely arbitrary and capricious, and the D.C. Circuit declined to lift that injunction. The administration has now filed an emergency application asking the Supreme Court to stay the injunction while the lawsuit continues. The Court has not ruled; a stay grant would temporarily allow enforcement, while a denial would leave the lower-court protection in place without deciding the merits.

HEALTHCARE ACCESS

First U.S. therapy arrives for a severe neurological and metabolic disorder

MCT8 deficiency can cause profound motor and communication disability, feeding difficulty and chronic cardiovascular and metabolic strain. Emcitate bypasses the defective transporter to enter cells and lower circulating thyroid hormone. The approval creates a U.S. treatment pathway, but access, insurance coverage and specialist monitoring will determine its practical reach.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

New research and verified access

FDA APPROVAL

Emcitate lowers excess thyroid hormone rather than repairing the MCT8 transporter

Tiratricol enters cells without depending on the defective MCT8 protein. Across a randomized international trial and a longer open-label study, FDA found reductions in excess thyroid hormone and improvements in cardiovascular and metabolic measures. The evidence supports management of peripheral thyrotoxicosis; the announcement does not establish restoration of normal neurodevelopment.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLES · GLIOMA

Multimodal MRI and digital pathology model predicts high-risk adult diffuse-glioma subtypes

CNS Neuroscience & Therapeutics · online September 28, 2026 · peer-reviewed retrospective diagnostic/prognostic modeling study. Researchers studied 456 adults with diffuse glioma and combined MRI “habitat” features with transformer-based pathology features. The combined model reported AUC 0.888 in training and 0.836 in its test set for high-risk molecular subtypes; model-defined risk groups also differed in survival, and exploratory transcriptomic results implicated extracellular-matrix pathways. Why it matters: it links imaging and pathology to molecular risk in a cancer relevant to disability and prognosis. Limitations: retrospective development with internal testing does not show prospective clinical utility or broad external validity; the biological analyses are exploratory and the model is not a treatment.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLES · MULTIPLE SYSTEM ATROPHY

Apathy is associated with lower medial-prefrontal blood flow in MSA

European Journal of Neurology · online September 26, 2026 · peer-reviewed retrospective cross-sectional imaging study. Among 76 cognitively preserved people with clinically established or probable multiple system atrophy, 55.3% met the study’s apathy threshold. Apathy was associated with lower medial-prefrontal perfusion after adjustment for clinical variables, while depression scores and gray-matter measures were similar; exploratory covariance analysis suggested altered medial-prefrontal–posterior-cerebellar networks. Why it matters: apathy can materially affect function, caregiving and participation in rehabilitation. Limitations: this is an association from one selected cohort, cannot establish cause, and does not identify an available treatment.

Nvidia launches an open platform for enforcing agent boundaries

The Open Agent Safety Platform combines the open-source OpenShell runtime, which traces actions and enforces policy outside the model, with a Sentry reference design running on BlueField-4 hardware that can quarantine an agent in milliseconds.
