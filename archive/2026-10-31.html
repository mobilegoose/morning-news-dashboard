1 · ELECTION ADMINISTRATION · FEDERAL COURT

Judge temporarily bars federal threats against state and local election workers

U.S. District Judge Trina Thompson issued a temporary restraining order late Friday blocking the Justice Department and Homeland Security from threatening or intimidating state and local election officials, or demanding that they halt or segregate ballots based only on allegations of noncitizen voting. The order runs through November 16 while the lawsuit proceeds; it is not a final merits ruling. Thompson found the plaintiffs likely to succeed on their Voting Rights Act intimidation claim, making this an immediate guardrail for ballot counting and certification.

Washington PostReuters

2 · GLOBAL HEALTH · EBOLA

Congo's Ebola death toll passes 5,000

Government data reported Friday put the Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola death toll above 5,000, with cases spanning eight of the country's 26 provinces and many deaths occurring outside treatment centers. That makes it Congo's deadliest Ebola outbreak and the world's second-largest on record after West Africa's 2014–16 epidemic. The current government count is newer than WHO's October 23 update, which documented 9,704 confirmed cases and 4,687 deaths through October 20 and spread to Kenya and Nigeria.

3 · UKRAINE · U.S. ARMS SALE

United States approves a proposed $2.1 billion Patriot-interceptor sale to Ukraine

The State Department approved a possible sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 air-defense interceptors to Ukraine, with Congress now notified. Canada, Germany and Norway, together with European Union-backed funds, would finance the package; no U.S. funds are involved. The approval clears a major procedural step but is not the same as a signed contract or immediate delivery, while Ukraine continues to face Russian missile and drone attacks.

4 · GOVERNMENT SPEECH · FEDERAL COURT

Judge orders three taxpayer-funded White House ads removed

U.S. District Judge Jennifer Rochon temporarily ordered the administration to stop airing three government-funded ads that promoted partisan or electoral messages, including one offering $5,000 if Republicans retain Congress. She declined to block many other challenged ads. The preliminary order protects against campaign-style use of public funds while the case continues; it is not a final judgment on the full advertising program.

ABC News / Associated PressWashington Post / Associated Press

5 · G20 · DIPLOMACY

G20 foreign ministers end their Atlanta meeting without consensus

The two-day meeting closed Friday without a negotiated joint communiqué; the United States issued a chair's statement instead. Divisions over Ukraine, Iran, tariffs, China and South Africa remained unresolved, and a planned group photo was canceled after objections to appearing with Russia's foreign minister. The result shows how little common ground the forum found even on a statement of shared priorities.

Associated Press

Use official plan preview before open enrollment begins

ACA MARKETPLACE · OPEN ENROLLMENT

2027 Marketplace plan preview arrives before enrollment opens November 1

CMS released its Plan Year 2027 pricing fact sheet and says Open Enrollment runs November 1 through January 15. It estimates that 75% of HealthCare.gov users will have access to at least one plan costing $50 or less per month after tax credits, and that eligible users can have about 90% of the lowest-cost plan premium covered. Those figures describe access, not a guarantee for every household. Compare the net premium, deductible, drug list and provider network—and update projected 2027 income—before choosing.

Appeals court rejects DOJ's request to set aside California ballots

Transplants, public housing and employment rights

ORGAN TRANSPLANTS · CMS FINAL RULE

CMS opens high-performing organ-procurement areas to competition

CMS finalized a rule allowing high-performing organ procurement organizations to compete for more than one donation service area while clarifying recertification, decertification, unsafe practices, adverse-event reporting and transitions when an organization fails. More than 100,000 people are on the transplant waiting list. The rule changes oversight and competition; it does not itself guarantee more donated organs or shorter waits.

PUBLIC HOUSING · HUD

HUD launches a searchable housing-authority accountability dashboard

HUD's new Public Housing Authority Accountability Dashboard lets users search by housing authority, state or nationally for performance designation, voucher utilization and budget-authority information. It can help residents and advocates spot underused vouchers or troubled performance, although the dashboard's indicators still need local context and do not replace an appeal or complaint process.

RECENT FALLBACK · CIVIL RIGHTS · EMPLOYMENT

EEOC rescinds its arrest-and-conviction-record guidance

On October 29, the EEOC voted to rescind its 2012 Title VII guidance on employers' use of arrest and conviction records, effective immediately. The agency says the rescission does not permit intentional race or national-origin discrimination. The change removes a longstanding federal analytical framework, but employers remain subject to Title VII and applicable state and local fair-chance laws.

MEASLES · CDC

U.S. measles count rises to 4,852 confirmed cases

CDC's October 30 update reports 4,852 confirmed cases through October 29—264 more than the prior update—with 511 hospitalizations, three deaths and 43 new outbreaks. Ninety-six percent of cases were in people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status was unknown. The week-to-week increase and outbreak count show continuing transmission; the national total can still change as jurisdictions verify cases.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · BLADDER CANCER · OCTOBER 29

Cisplatin-resistant bladder-cancer model shows increased sensitivity to enfortumab vedotin

PLOS One published a peer-reviewed preclinical laboratory and mouse study using one cisplatin-resistant subline derived from the HT1376 human bladder-cancer cell line. In three-replicate cell experiments and xenografts across 14 female nude mice, the resistant model was more sensitive to enfortumab vedotin and its MMAE payload; ABCB1 expression appeared to influence sensitivity. This matters because metastatic urothelial cancer and treatment resistance are high-priority cancer concerns, but it is mechanism-generating evidence—not proof that cisplatin-resistant patients respond better and not a new treatment indication. Material limitations include a single selected cell-line model, no human participants, possible culture-driven clonal effects and approximate tumor measurements. Published online October 29, 2026.
