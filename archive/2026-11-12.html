1 · MISSISSIPPI · U.S. SENATE

Scott Colom wins Mississippi's Senate race in a historic upset

Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith after officials finished processing enough absentee and provisional ballots for the race to be called Wednesday. Hyde-Smith conceded. Colom will be Mississippi's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction and the state's first newly elected Democratic senator since 1947, materially strengthening the Democratic majority that takes office in January.

Israeli airstrike hits Nasser Hospital, killing six and halting key services

Gaza health officials said the strike on the surgical wing killed six people, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old patient, and wounded at least 28. Israel said it targeted a Hamas commander and other militants but offered no evidence; the named commander was not among the dead. 3 · INTERIOR · PUBLIC LANDS

Interior adopts a five-year strategy to shrink the federal estate through land sales

The Interior Department's new 2026–2030 strategic plan says the government will offer federal land to states, local governments and Tribes and focus federal resources on undefined 'nationally significant' lands. It identifies no parcels, acreage, prices or schedule. The plan is a policy roadmap, not an executed sale: protected lands and other federal holdings face statutory limits, and major transfers may require congressional cooperation.

Most Useful Development Today

A medication-safety action with a clear practical response

RECENT SAFETY ACTION · GLP-1 MEDICATIONS · NOVEMBER 9

FDA requires every GLP-1 medicine to warn about a rare eye condition

FDA ordered labeling changes for the full GLP-1 class—including semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide products—because of a potential increased risk of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION), which can cause sudden, painless and sometimes permanent vision loss in one eye. FDA's pooled analysis found 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years with GLP-1 drugs versus 3.8 with placebo or non-GLP-1 comparators. Patients should not stop treatment on their own, but sudden vision loss or a marked change in vision requires prompt medical attention.

INDONESIA · CONSTITUTIONAL SUCCESSION

Indonesia's vice president resigns after courts question his eligibility

Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned after the Constitutional Court and Supreme Court found that his overseas education documents did not satisfy election requirements and 120 lawmakers began an impeachment process. President Prabowo Subianto accepted the resignation. Under Indonesia's Constitution, Parliament must choose a replacement from two presidential nominees within 60 days; there will not be a new national election.

PUBLIC LANDS · FEDERAL STRATEGY

Interior's sale policy is now official strategy, but no land is yet identified for sale

The five-year plan directs Interior to reduce the federal estate and return part of sale proceeds to buyers for conservation elsewhere. Federal law bars sale of protected land and limits when other parcels can be sold; the plan itself does not override those constraints. The next meaningful checkpoints are parcel-specific proposals, environmental and land-use review, and any request for congressional authority.

RECENT SAFETY ACTION · MEDICATIONS · NOVEMBER 9

FDA finds a small but higher NAION rate in pooled GLP-1 trials and orders label changes

Across 126 randomized controlled trials, FDA calculated 6.4 NAION cases per 100,000 person-years among GLP-1 users and 3.8 among placebo or non-GLP-1 comparator groups. The absolute event rate was low, and the agency described the evidence as a potential increased risk rather than proof that every reported case was caused by the drug. The practical change is class-wide warnings and precautions, with prompt evaluation for sudden, painless one-eye vision loss.

Official FDA safety communication

RECENT FALLBACK · OFFICIAL SUPREME COURT AUDIO · NOVEMBER 9

Nielsen v. Watanabe oral argument

A former federal prisoner alleges that a prison nurse ignored months of serious pain from a fractured coccyx; the nurse asks the Court to bar a judicially created damages action against the federal official, and the United States asks the Court to overrule the 1980 medical-care precedent that permitted such claims. The Court granted certiorari on June 22 and heard argument Monday; it has not decided the merits. The case may determine whether federal prisoners can still seek individual-capacity damages for deliberate indifference to medical needs.
