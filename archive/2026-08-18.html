1. Ukraine and Russia traded another major wave of long-range attacks

Ukraine launched almost 800 drones toward Russia in one of its largest aerial operations of the war; Russia said it intercepted 791. 2. A ship was hit while leaving the Strait of Hormuz as the U.S.–Iran negotiating window expired

An unidentified projectile damaged a vessel's engine room off Oman and caused one crew casualty; Oman's coast guard assisted while authorities investigated. President Trump said no U.S.–Iran talks were planned after a 60-day negotiating period ended, while Iran continued restricting a waterway that normally carries roughly one-fifth of traded oil. 3 · Top story

3. The U.S. invoked a rarely used tariff law against Canada

President Trump invoked Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 to set 50% duties on about $20 billion of Canadian products—roughly 5% of Canada's exports to the United States—starting at 12:01 a.m. Wednesday unless negotiations produce a truce. The action raises near-term price and supply risks for affected goods and sharpens a dispute with the largest U.S. trading partner. Associated Press

4 · Top story

4. Disney sued the FCC, alleging retaliation against ABC stations

Disney and ABC filed a First Amendment lawsuit accusing the Federal Communications Commission of using an early review of eight local broadcast licenses to punish the network over Jimmy Kimmel's satire and diversity policies. The filing puts the administration's use of communications licensing against a major media company directly before a federal court. Associated Press

5 · Top story

5. A judge blocked the Trump administration's FBI headquarters switch

U.S. District Judge Theodore Chuang ruled that the administration unlawfully discarded the previously approved Greenbelt, Maryland, plan in favor of Washington's Ronald Reagan Building. The order blocks renovation, relocation and repurposing of congressionally directed funds while leaving the long-running headquarters project to return to the lawful process. Associated Press

2. Most Useful Development Today

Practical federal funding

HHS awarded $96.7 million for behavioral-health services

The August 17 awards include $63.4 million for outreach, case management, treatment and housing-related help for people with serious mental illness who are homeless; $16.8 million for youth suicide prevention; $7.1 million for early intervention for young people at high risk of psychosis; and smaller awards for substance-use training, disaster distress, eating-disorder training and peer networks.

Why it is useful: the official notice identifies the funded programs and, for PATH, links to state contacts that connect people to local providers. The money goes to systems and grantees rather than directly to individuals, but it can expand service availability. HHS award details and program links

The Ninth Circuit ruled Nevada's acting U.S. attorney was unlawfully installed

A unanimous panel held that Attorney General Pam Bondi could not keep Sigal Chattah leading the Nevada office by naming her first assistant after the vacancy and then invoking the Federal Vacancies Reform Act. The ruling disqualifies Chattah from supervising the challenged prosecutions; it leaves the indictments in place for now and allows the Justice Department to seek further review. 5. Policy & Benefits

Policy & benefits

ICE proposed subsidized liability insurance for deputized local officers

An agency proposal would reimburse up to $250 a year for professional-liability policies covering as much as $500,000 in legal fees, settlements or judgments for local police trained under federal immigration partnerships. ICE was seeking industry feedback and had not set a launch date; the proposal could make local participation easier while intensifying accountability concerns. Federal procurement document · Reuters — potentially limited access

Policy & benefits

DOJ finalized a process to restore some federal firearm rights

The final rule lets eligible people barred under federal law apply individually to the attorney general. Relief is not automatic; applicants must show they are unlikely to endanger public safety, violent felons and several other categories face presumptions against relief, and state restrictions still apply. The rule takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication. Justice Department rule summary and application link

Policy & benefits

Prescription-drug prices fell sharply in the federal index, but bills may not track it

The July consumer-price data showed prescription-drug prices down 0.8% for the month and 3.1% over 12 months, the steepest annual drop since 1963. The index measures what pharmacies receive from insurers and consumers—not an individual's out-of-pocket bill—and analysts cited generic competition and Medicare negotiation alongside newer administration initiatives. Associated Press · BLS data
