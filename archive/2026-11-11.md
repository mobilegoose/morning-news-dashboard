## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Ukraine, Russia and the United States end two days of talks with a prisoner-exchange agreement in principle

The U.S.-mediated Abu Dhabi talks concluded Wednesday with Ukraine and Russia agreeing in principle to a major prisoner exchange; the numbers are to be finalized Thursday and an exchange could occur within two to three weeks. No ceasefire or broader settlement emerged. Territorial control, security guarantees and Europe's role remain unresolved, and fighting continued while the talks were under way.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/7c8d1024302e0c6ad8be064eec852a64)

### CDC extends its Ebola-related Title 42 entry suspension for another 30 days

CDC issued a new order continuing restrictions on covered noncitizens who were in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda or South Sudan during the previous 21 days. The order took effect at 5 p.m. Eastern on November 10 and includes specified government, humanitarian and public-health exceptions. It cites the fast-moving Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak and provides a 15-day comment period; the restriction is temporary but immediately operative.

[Official CDC order page](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/legal-authorities/evdorder.html) · [Signed November 10 order](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/media/pdfs/2026/11/November-T42-Order_signed.pdf)

### House blocks the Hegseth impeachment resolution, 211–204

The House approved a motion referring Rep. Thomas Massie's eight-article impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth back to committee. The vote halts this attempt for now; it was not an acquittal or a merits finding, and it does not prevent a later House from considering impeachment. The resolution principally challenged military actions involving Iran and asserted war-powers and related misconduct claims.

[Official House roll call](https://clerk.house.gov/votes/2026317) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/87a78193502f716e6fb8eee5aef06ac0)

### White House gives federal wildfire suppression a one-year Clean Water Act exemption

A presidential determination exempts federal agencies and their contractors from many federal, state and local water-pollution requirements when conducting wildfire-suppression activities, including aerial retardant drops, from November 10, 2026 through November 10, 2027. The memorandum excludes Clean Water Act sections 306 and 307 from the exemption and does not suspend other environmental laws. It trades permitting and liability constraints for faster suppression while shifting water-quality risk.

[Official presidential determination](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/presidential-determination-concerning-wildfire-suppression-activities-in-the-united-states/) · [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-advances-wildfire-suppression/)

### Trinidad and Tobago's final appellate court strikes down the criminalization of consensual gay sex

Activist Jason Jones challenged provisions of the country's 1986 Sexual Offences Act that criminalized private consensual sex between adults of the same sex. The Judicial Committee of the Privy Council held that those provisions cannot constitutionally apply to private consensual adult conduct. The court will settle the precise remedial order after further submissions, but the constitutional ruling is final and removes the legal basis for that criminalization.

[Official judgment and case materials](https://www.jcpc.uk/cases/jcpc-2025-0095) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/4ad477cea8ca4dd7455373e572a4e392)


## Most Useful Development Today

### CMS publishes a wide-ranging proposed rule; comments are due January 11

The proposed CARE rule would revise Medicare and Medicaid participation requirements across hospitals, long-term-care facilities and other providers. Among many changes, CMS proposes rescinding Medicaid institutional payment-transparency reporting for nursing-facility direct-care staff and ICF/IID support staff, and it seeks comment on discharge notices to long-term-care ombudsmen, workforce shortages and other requirements. CMS estimates $320 million in annual savings and 3.26 million staff hours, but these are agency projections. Nothing is final: affected patients, families, advocates and providers may submit comments through January 11, 2027.

[Federal Register proposal and comment instructions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22812/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-cutting-administrative-requirements-for-excellence-in-patient-care) · [Official CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-medicaid-programs-cutting-administrative-requirements-excellence-patient-care-care-cms-3484)

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Jones v. Attorney General of Trinidad and Tobago: consensual adult same-sex intimacy cannot be criminalized

Jason Jones asked Trinidad and Tobago's final appellate court to invalidate provisions criminalizing 'buggery' and 'serious indecency' as applied to private consensual adult conduct. The Judicial Committee of the Privy Council allowed the constitutional appeal and held the criminal provisions cannot stand in that application. The court has not merely agreed to hear the case: it decided the constitutional issue, with the final form of the remedial order to follow after submissions.

[Official judgment and case page](https://www.jcpc.uk/cases/jcpc-2025-0095) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/4ad477cea8ca4dd7455373e572a4e392)

### Committee referral stops the Hegseth resolution without deciding its allegations

The House's 211–204 vote sent H.Res. 1655 back to committee after it was introduced as a privileged matter. Procedurally, referral removes the immediate floor vote on impeachment; it does not establish that the eight articles are true or false, and it does not bar another impeachment resolution. One Republican joined Democrats against referral, while one independent joined Republicans for it.

Arizona, Florida, Nebraska, Ohio and Wisconsin joined the FTC, Utah and California in an amended federal complaint. The plaintiffs allege that Hims & Hers shared sensitive health information with advertising platforms despite privacy assurances and misled users about prescription charges and cancellation. ### Eleventh Circuit affirms case-specific notice requirements before Florida terminates family-related Medicaid

In Chianne D. v. Secretary, Florida Agency for Health Care Administration, the court affirmed a permanent injunction requiring termination notices to identify the household size, countable income, eligibility category and applicable income limit. The ruling concerns the certified class receiving family-related Medicaid, not every aged, blind, disabled or SSI-linked Medicaid pathway. It matters because a generic denial reason does not give families enough information to understand or challenge a loss of coverage.

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### New Free Journal Articles

### [Two metabolites show promise as early esophageal-cancer biomarkers—but require independent validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13748909/)

Cancer Science published a peer-reviewed, single-center observational metabolomics study in Japan. Plasma samples came from 80 people with esophageal cancer and 29 healthy controls; urine samples came from 59 patients and 61 controls, with partly overlapping cohorts. For Stage I–II disease, combined plasma-and-urine 3-indoxyl sulfate produced a reported AUC of 0.863 and plasma O-acetylcarnitine an AUC of 0.902. Why it matters: a blood-or-urine signal could support less invasive early detection. Major limitations are the small single-center sample, healthy rather than clinically similar controls, demographic and exposure imbalances, predominantly squamous-cell disease and no independent validation. This is biomarker discovery—not an available screening test or proof of clinical benefit.

The Labor Department brought administrative proceedings seeking monetary remedies for alleged violations affecting H-2A and corresponding domestic agricultural workers. Sun Valley Orchards argues that Article III and the governing statute do not permit an agency adjudicator to impose those monetary remedies. The Supreme Court granted certiorari on those two questions and heard argument Tuesday; it has not decided them. The recording is useful for hearing the justices test agency power, jury and Article III concerns, and statutory authorization before a merits ruling later in the term.
