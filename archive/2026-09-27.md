## 2. Most Useful Development Today

### Medicaid work-requirement paperwork may depend sharply on your state

Federal rules for the 2027 Medicaid work requirement allow expansion adults to self-attest that they are medically frail during the first year, but Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, New Hampshire, North Carolina and Ohio have already barred that option and plan to require documentation immediately. The law generally calls for 80 hours per month of work, volunteering or half-time school, with exemptions for disability and several other circumstances. **Practical step:** if you may rely on a medical-frailty or disability exception in one of those states, begin gathering a clinician’s certification, functional records and current contact information before renewals start; do not assume an existing diagnosis will automatically transfer into the new system. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/medicaid-work-requirement-self-attestation-012ebf8a94ce672bd8ae5c863db566e5)

### New Free Journal Articles

#### LRP12 repeat expansions may explain a narrow subset of unresolved adult-onset spinal muscular atrophy

**Neurology Genetics · online September 23, 2026; newly indexed in the 72-hour window · peer-reviewed observational case series.** Researchers studied eight preselected Japanese families with genetically unresolved, adult-onset non-5q spinal muscular atrophy, plus 496 unrelated ALS cases and 1,000 controls. LRP12 CGG repeat expansions appeared in six of the eight SMA probands, compared with two ALS cases and two controls, supporting targeted repeat-expansion testing in a specific slowly progressive lower-motor-neuron phenotype. This is a diagnostic clue, not a treatment result or a population prevalence estimate. The main limitation is the tiny, highly selected referral series from one country; short-read sequencing can also miss these expansions. [Free full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13609829/)

#### Automated speech errors distinguished two primary-progressive-aphasia variants in a small validation study

**Brain Communications · online September 12, 2026; newly indexed in the 72-hour window · peer-reviewed diagnostic machine-learning study.** The study included 104 people—40 with nonfluent PPA, 40 with logopenic PPA and 24 healthy controls—reading one short passage. Eight automated dysfluency features separated PPA from controls, while held-out discrimination between the two PPA variants reached AUC 0.798 with a random-forest model and was lower with other validation approaches. This could eventually support more scalable speech assessment, but it is not a clinical diagnostic test: the sample was small, the task was tightly controlled and all validation was internal. [Free full text (Oxford Academic)](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/advance-article/doi/10.1093/braincomms/fcag353/8792780)

#### Long-term cancer responders still reported fatigue and unmet quality-of-life needs

**Immuno-Oncology and Technology · online September 17, 2026; newly indexed in the 72-hour window · peer-reviewed multicenter cross-sectional study.** At 16 French hospitals, 234 adults who had responded to immune-checkpoint inhibitors for at least two years—117 with advanced non-small-cell lung cancer and 117 with melanoma—completed quality-of-life, fatigue and social-difficulty measures. Most reported no or slight problems on standard health dimensions, yet moderate-to-severe fatigue was common and about 22% said they could not express quality-of-life concerns as they wished. The result highlights survivorship, function and care-coordination needs, but the cross-sectional, self-reported and selected survivor sample cannot show what treatment caused or represent people with poorer outcomes. [Free full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602145/)
