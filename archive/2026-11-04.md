### Judge pauses planned deportations of detained Afghans through Saturday

U.S. District Judge Valerie Caproni temporarily barred the government from sending detained Afghans to Afghanistan or Pakistan after advocates warned that returnees could face Taliban retaliation, persecution or death. ICE told the court it had no such flights scheduled through the end of the week. The short order is a reprieve—not a final ruling—and expires Saturday unless the court acts again.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/a419fbb329b96480d806d9ffbd52ccb4)

### Iran reports four tankers hit mines as regional leaders warn the conflict could widen again

Iranian state television said four oil tankers caught fire after striking mines on routes Tehran called unauthorized. The report came after a limited strike on Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz and while the United States sends more forces to the region. Back-channel talks continue, but the military incidents leave commercial shipping and energy markets exposed to renewed escalation.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/f3c48a92df2bd8a658d3e704a7291d11)

### Justices hear Colorado dispute over public preschool funding and nondiscrimination rules

Two Catholic preschools asked the Court to require Colorado to include them in a state-funded universal preschool program even though they would not comply with rules protecting LGBTQ children and families. Colorado says participating schools must serve eligible families without that discrimination; the schools say exclusion burdens religious exercise. The Court heard argument Tuesday but issued no ruling; a merits decision is expected by the end of June.

## Supreme Court & Legal

### The Afghan-deportation order preserves the status quo for only a few days

More than 80 detained Afghans reportedly received notices that they might be removed through Pakistan; the group includes women, religious minorities, a former police officer and people with relatives who served the United States. Judge Caproni’s order keeps the government from carrying out those removals through Saturday. It does not grant asylum, release anyone from detention or finally decide whether removal would be lawful.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/a419fbb329b96480d806d9ffbd52ccb4)

### What the Colorado preschool argument means procedurally

In St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, the Catholic schools are asking the Court to reverse lower-court rulings and hold that Colorado cannot condition generally available preschool funding on compliance with sexual-orientation and gender-identity nondiscrimination rules. Colorado argues the same participation conditions apply to religious and secular providers. The Court heard merits argument November 3; it did not grant emergency relief or decide the case. The next major step is a written merits decision, expected by late June 2027.

[Official transcript](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2026/25-581_h3cj.pdf) · [Case-specific explainer](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/11/03/st-mary-catholic-parish-v-roy-supreme-court-oral-arguments/)

### Justices examine when changed diagnostic standards can support a second federal habeas petition

In Guerrero v. Johnson, Texas prison director Eric Guerrero is asking the Court to stop death-row prisoner Dexter Johnson from using a second federal habeas petition to claim intellectual disability. Johnson argues that later medical standards made the Eighth Amendment protection against executing intellectually disabled people meaningfully available to him for the first time; Texas says the underlying constitutional rule existed before his first petition. The Court granted review in June and convened for merits argument today. That is not a ruling or a stay; a written decision will determine whether Johnson’s second petition may proceed.

[Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1003.html) · [Official November 4 day call](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/daycall/Day%20Call_11-04-26.pdf) · [Cornell Legal Information Institute explainer](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/cert/25-1003)

### FDA updates hepatitis B testing recommendations for blood and plasma donations

FDA finalized guidance for blood establishments on testing donations for hepatitis B virus. It covers whole blood, blood components and source plasma, and explains how licensed, approved or cleared screening tests should be used with donor deferral and notification requirements. The guidance is directed to collection establishments; it is not a new vaccine recommendation or evidence of a current transfusion outbreak.

### New Free Journal Articles

### PIVKA-II showed fair discrimination for liver cancer—but performed poorly in earlier-stage disease

PLOS One published a peer-reviewed case-control biomarker study of 160 adults with chronic liver disease in Pakistan: 74 had established hepatocellular carcinoma and 86 had nonmalignant disease. PIVKA-II produced an AUC of 0.753, with 86.4% sensitivity but 56.0% specificity at the selected cutoff; performance fell to an AUC of 0.571 in early/intermediate disease. This supports further validation as a complementary marker, not a stand-alone screening test. The single-center design, case-control sampling, unstable cutoff and lack of external validation limit generalizability. Published online November 3, 2026.

[Free full text — PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0361802) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42976211/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0361802)

### Systematic review finds leptomeningeal spread is rare, aggressive and supported by very limited evidence

Diseases of the Esophagus published a peer-reviewed systematic review of 31 reports covering 43 patients with leptomeningeal carcinomatosis from esophageal cancer. Median survival after diagnosis was 1.1 months; three reported patients survived 12–19 months after central-nervous-system-directed treatment. The review is useful for recognizing a severe complication, but it cannot show that any treatment caused longer survival. Evidence came only from case reports and small series, with publication bias, heterogeneous reporting and moderate-to-high risk of bias. Published online November 3, 2026.

[Free full text — Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/dote/article/39/6/doag126/9010807) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42974869/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/dote/doag126)
