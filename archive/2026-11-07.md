## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Trump names Stephen Miller to succeed Susie Wiles as chief of staff

President Donald Trump announced Friday that deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller will become the 33rd White House chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles. Miller has been a central architect of the administration's immigration and border policies, so the appointment places an especially influential policy adviser in the office that controls White House process, personnel and access to the president. The appointment itself—not predictions about every policy consequence—is today's concrete development.

[Official White House announcement](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/president-trump-names-stephen-miller-as-33rd-white-house-chief-of-staff/) · [Readable AP report](https://apnews.com/article/95690fe076f6bb8cfe2bf77435a1910d)

### Russian missile and drone barrage wounds at least nine before new talks

Ukrainian officials said Russia launched 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones against Kyiv and 12 other regions overnight, damaging homes and infrastructure, causing power and water outages, and wounding at least nine people, including a child. Russia, Ukraine and the United States are due to meet Tuesday in Abu Dhabi. The attack is confirmed as a major escalation before the talks, but battlefield interception and damage claims remain difficult to verify independently in real time.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/b042adf1f95848f5d233da866a72e312) · [Readable CBS/AP report](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/zelenskyy-russia-attacks-us-talks/)

### Airstrikes near Sanaa kill at least 25 as Yemen's truce collapse deepens

A spokesman for Yemen's Houthi-run Health Ministry said strikes on homes in al-Shariya killed at least 25 people and wounded 30, mostly women and children. He blamed Saudi Arabia; AP could not independently verify responsibility, and Saudi officials had not responded at publication. The reported toll and renewed attacks show the humanitarian stakes of a civil war that sharply re-escalated after the yearslong truce collapsed this summer.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/1b5aef5b86be2e96e1cd8a18165afaf6)

### U.S. measles total passes 5,000—the highest annual count since 1991

CDC's November 6 update reported 5,182 confirmed U.S. cases through November 5, more than twice the 2025 total. The agency reports that 96% of patients were unvaccinated or had unknown vaccination status, 562 were hospitalized and 43 outbreaks occurred. The figures are surveillance counts subject to revision, but they document a sustained nationwide resurgence of a vaccine-preventable disease.

[Official CDC data](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/) · [Readable Washington Post report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2026/11/07/cdc-has-recorded-more-than-5000-measles-cases-this-year/)

### Justice Department moves to dismiss its own disputed-ballot lawsuit

DOJ asked a federal court Friday to dismiss its suit seeking to preserve roughly 20,000 California ballots it alleged were associated with possible noncitizens. The judge had rejected DOJ's earlier request, and the ballots were counted anonymously, making the requested segregation impossible. The filing seeks voluntary dismissal; it is not a merits ruling that every challenged voter was eligible, and a separate federal voter-roll case continues.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/09e1048507008385dae20160786dc490)


## Most Useful Development Today

### SSA reports shorter phone, office and disability-decision waits

SSA's new fiscal-year service summary says the National 800 Number's average wait fell from 27.6 minutes two years ago to 5.6 minutes. Compared with fiscal 2024, the agency says initial disability decisions averaged 40 days faster and hearing decisions 72 days faster; office waits fell 31% to 20.6 minutes, or 5.8 minutes for people with appointments. Practical takeaway: an appointment may materially reduce an office wait, but these are national agency-reported averages—not deadlines or predictions for an individual claim.

## Supreme Court & Legal

### DOJ abandons its bid to have California set aside about 20,000 ballots

The federal government moved to dismiss the case after the trial judge denied emergency relief and questioned the reliability of DOJ's list, which California and outside analysts said contained mismatches and naturalized citizens. Because already-counted secret ballots cannot be traced back to individual voters, the requested remedy was no longer available. The dismissal request would end this suit without a final adjudication of every eligibility allegation.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/09e1048507008385dae20160786dc490)

### National-security chief and South Florida U.S. attorney resign

Assistant Attorney General John Eisenberg, who led DOJ's National Security Division, and U.S. Attorney Jason Reding Quiñones resigned Friday. Quiñones' office had overseen the administration's wide-ranging 'grand conspiracy' investigation; Eisenberg supervised national-security prosecutions. The departures follow Civil Rights Division chief Harmeet Dhillon's resignation and create three senior vacancies, but the resignation announcements do not establish why each official left.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/9a10b4ffcb620edb3c2298cec066062d)

### Tenth Circuit reverses nationwide order granting a fifth eligibility year

In Wisne v. NCAA, the federal appeals court reversed a lower-court ruling that had extended an additional year of eligibility to athletes who graduated high school in 2022. The panel held that the plaintiffs had not adequately defined the relevant market for their antitrust claim. The ruling restores the NCAA's federal-court position in this case but does not resolve about 200 related state-court cases involving as many as 850 athletes.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/e4798c29e17e7cdc88a9108323c14e9c)

### Court will decide whether bankruptcy automatic-stay damages claims must go to arbitration

In Goldman Sachs Bank USA v. Brown, debtors Rhea Brown and Gregory Maze allege that Goldman continued credit-card collection after they sought bankruptcy protection. Goldman asked the Court to review the Fourth Circuit's refusal to compel arbitration, arguing that the Federal Arbitration Act requires enforcement of their credit-card arbitration clauses; the debtors say bankruptcy courts must retain authority to enforce the automatic stay and award damages under 11 U.S.C. § 362(k). The Court granted certiorari Friday, meaning it will hear the case—it did not rule for either side. Briefing and argument will follow, and the eventual decision could determine whether these stay-violation claims proceed before bankruptcy judges or private arbitrators.

[Official November 6 order](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/110626zr_q8l1.pdf) · [Official case docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1408.html) · [Case-specific bankruptcy explainer](https://www.ncbrc.org/automatic-stay/2026/08/31/debtors-urge-supreme-court-to-deny-review-in-goldman-sachs-automatic-stay-arbitration-case/)

## Policy & Benefits

### SSA's improved averages are useful benchmarks, not enforceable processing times

SSA reports faster fiscal-year averages for initial disability and hearing decisions, plus much shorter 800-number and appointment waits. Claimants and representatives can use the figures to decide when an individual delay is unusually long, but the update provides no claim-specific service standard and does not separate processing times by state DDS, hearing office, claim type or decisional outcome.

[Official SSA update](https://www.ssa.gov/blog/en/posts/2026-11-06.html) · [SSA hearing-office status resources](https://www.ssa.gov/appeals/hearing_process.html)

### Updated KFF analysis finds the 2027 payment change shifts revenue away from safety-net hospitals

CMS's finalized outpatient rule will cut reimbursement for qualifying 340B drugs from average sales price plus 6% to ASP minus 28% on January 1, while increasing non-drug outpatient payments 7.5% for budget neutrality. KFF's update, using CMS estimates, projects a 5.2% net OPPS-revenue reduction for its safety-net-hospital category and a 6.8% increase for for-profit hospitals. CMS projects lower coinsurance for patients receiving 340B drugs, but higher cost sharing for some non-drug outpatient services; hospital-level effects will vary.

[KFF analysis updated November 6](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/medicares-proposed-cut-to-340b-drug-payments-would-hit-safety-net-hospitals-while-benefiting-for-profit-hospitals/) · [Official CMS final-rule summary](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/medicare-outpatient-final-rule-expands-surgical-options-lowers-out-pocket-costs-340b-drugs) · [Federal Register final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/06/2026-22739/medicare-program-hospital-outpatient-prospective-payment-and-ambulatory-surgical-center-payment)

CDC reports 562 hospitalizations and four measles-associated deaths nationally, while noting that states may have more current jurisdiction-level data. High case counts show population-level transmission; they do not establish the source or outcome of any individual infection.

[Official CDC data](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/) · [Readable Washington Post report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2026/11/07/cdc-has-recorded-more-than-5000-measles-cases-this-year/)

### FDA investigates tumor risk after two Zolgensma-treated children develop malignancies

FDA said two tumors contained vector DNA integrated at the GLI1 gene, supporting product-related insertional oncogenesis after Zolgensma treatment. Because Itvisma uses the same AAV9 vector, FDA says the potential risk applies to both products, although no Itvisma-associated cases have been reported. The agency says current benefits still outweigh known risks and is evaluating regulatory action; clinicians and families should monitor for tumors and report adverse events. Two cases establish a serious safety signal, not its frequency across all treated patients.

[Official FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/fda-investigating-serious-risk-malignancy-linked-zolgensma-gene-therapy)

### New Free Journal Articles

### [Participants describe benefits—and setbacks—from tailored exercise for post-COVID POTS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13734440/)

Journal: PLOS ONE. Published electronically November 4, 2026. Evidence: peer-reviewed qualitative content analysis nested in a feasibility intervention. Researchers interviewed 23 adults who completed a semi-supervised, individually tailored exercise program for COVID-19-induced postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome. Participants described perceived gains in physical, mental and social function and valued flexible pacing, energy management and support from condition-specific physiotherapists; symptom fluctuations and setbacks were common. The study matters for Long COVID rehabilitation design, but completer self-reports without a control group cannot establish efficacy, causation, comparative safety or suitability for all people with POTS, and selection bias is likely.

### Maxwell v. Dinis oral argument

Federal prisoner Maxwell says the Bureau of Prisons wrongly withheld First Step Act time credits and seeks transfer to prerelease custody through a 28 U.S.C. § 2241 habeas petition. The Supreme Court granted review only on whether that type of claim may be brought under § 2241; it heard argument November 2 and has not ruled. The government supports vacating the judgment below, while a court-appointed amicus defends it. The eventual ruling will govern the procedural channel for earned-credit claims—not decide on this record whether Maxwell is entitled to release. The recording is useful for hearing the justices test habeas jurisdiction and prison-administration remedies.
