## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Isaias leaves widespread outages as its flood threat moves inland

Isaias made landfall Friday near Destin, Florida, as a Category 2 hurricane with 105 mph winds, then weakened to a post-tropical system near Montgomery. More than 915,000 customers in Florida, Alabama and Georgia lost power, while downed trees and lines blocked roads. ### U.S. sanctions cut the ICC off from American finance and technology

The United States imposed institution-wide sanctions on the International Criminal Court, barring U.S. financial services, technology and dollar transactions after a six-month wind-down period. The Court called the action an attack on judicial independence. The breadth matters because it could impede salaries, banking, evidence systems and investigations far beyond the officials already sanctioned individually.

### Federal judge vacates DOJ’s nationwide voter-roll data policy

A federal district judge ruled that DOJ unlawfully collected states’ unredacted voter files, shared them with DHS and pressured states to remove voters. The court vacated the nationwide policy in Common Cause v. Department of Justice, meaning it has no legal effect unless a higher court stays or reverses the judgment. DOJ may appeal; the ruling does not itself restore any individual registration already changed under separate state procedures.

[District-court opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2026cv01352/291580/51/) · [Case tracker](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/doj-national-voter-roll-database-challenge/)

## Most Useful Development Today

### Federal agencies draw a proposed line between botanical kratom and high-potency opioid products

HHS recommended Schedule I treatment for products containing more than 1 mg of 7-hydroxymitragynine or mitragynine pseudoindoxyl per gram of solid or milliliter of liquid, or with a mitragynine-to-7-OH ratio below 100:1. DOJ and DEA are beginning a new scheduling process for concentrated 7-OH-related substances while distinguishing trace levels in unadulterated kratom leaf. Consumers should not treat the thresholds as an immediate nationwide ban: the notice-and-comment and scheduling steps still matter, and product labels may not reliably disclose concentration.

[HHS threshold recommendation](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) · [Justice Department and DEA update](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid)

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Judge sets aside DOJ’s voter-roll collection and sharing policy

Common Cause and two voters challenged DOJ’s effort to obtain complete state voter files—including sensitive personal data—and use them with DHS systems to seek removals. The district court granted partial summary judgment and vacated the policy, finding legal defects under federal privacy, voting and information-collection rules. Vacatur stops the policy nationwide at the trial-court level; it is not a Supreme Court ruling, and DOJ can seek a stay and appeal.

[District-court opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2026cv01352/291580/51/) · [Case tracker](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/doj-national-voter-roll-database-challenge/)

### Pentagon plans a livestreamed firing-squad execution as legal and political doubts grow

The Pentagon says it will livestream the December 3 execution of Nidal Hasan, whom a military jury convicted of killing 13 people and wounding 32 at Fort Hood in 2009. Vice President Vance questioned whether the plan will proceed, and lawyers are examining possible challenges to the method and public broadcast. No military execution has occurred since 1961, so both the procedure and its transmission would be extraordinary; the announcement is not proof that all review is complete.

[CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/fort-hood-shooter-execution-livestream-reaction-pentagon/) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/fort-hood-execution-hegseth-vance-e806a305d9c6460aa20b14e40047680e) · [Reuters legal analysis (potentially limited)](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/is-it-legal-us-livestream-fort-hood-shooters-execution-2026-10-09/)

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### HHS ties potency thresholds to opioid-like risk while preserving a distinction for natural leaf

The new recommendation focuses on concentrated 7-OH and related products rather than automatically treating all botanical kratom alike. HHS cites opioid-receptor activity, dependence and overdose concerns, but federal scheduling is a legal risk-control decision, not proof that every product above a threshold causes the same harm. Consumers should avoid mixing sedating substances and seek urgent help for slowed breathing or unresponsiveness.

[HHS threshold recommendation](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) · [Justice Department and DEA update](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid)

### New Free Journal Articles

#### [First-in-human TEAD inhibitor shows biological activity but dose-limiting exposure problems](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-026-01012-z)

Biomarker Research · online October 9, 2026 · open-label, multicenter, single-arm dose-escalation trial of BPI-460372 in 28 adults with advanced solid tumors after standard options. One confirmed partial response produced a 3.6% objective response rate; disease-control rate was 64.3%, median progression-free survival 5.6 months and median overall survival 13.2 months. No formal dose-limiting toxicity occurred, but treatment-related events affected 89.3%, grade 3 or worse events 39.3%, proteinuria 46.4% and exposure-related toxicity prevented reaching the desired biologically active dose. Why it matters: it is first-in-human evidence for a Hippo-pathway target, not an available treatment. Limitations include the very small, heterogeneous, uncontrolled cohort and one treatment-related death.

[Free full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40364-026-01012-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42855739/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40364-026-01012-z)

#### [All known IRF4 p.T95R immunodeficiency cases clarify disease severity and transplant risk](https://rupress.org/jhi/article/2/6/e20260069/283055/IRF4-p-T95R-combined-immunodeficiency-Clinical)

Journal of Human Immunity · online October 9, 2026 · detailed clinical characterization and follow-up of all 10 known patients with this rare genetic combined immunodeficiency. Pulmonary disease was universal, gastrointestinal involvement was often severe, and four patients received hematopoietic stem-cell transplants; two survived and two died, with both deaths occurring when active infection was present at transplant. Why it matters: the report improves recognition and suggests that infection control and timing may be crucial. Limitations are the tiny descriptive cohort, lack of a comparator and inability to establish that transplant timing caused the different outcomes.
