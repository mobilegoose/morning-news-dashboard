Saturday, July 25, 2026 Updated after the 10:30 a.m. ET Supreme Court check

A weekend briefing centered on the pause in overnight U.S.-Iran strikes, threats to regional energy infrastructure, two consequential federal lawsuits, a growing foodborne-illness outbreak, and verified medical and AI developments.

1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

Ranked by public importance
  1. An overnight pause interrupted nearly two weeks of U.S.-Iran strikes. No new U.S. strikes were reported overnight into Saturday after earlier attacks damaged Iranian transport, communications and water infrastructure. The pause lowers the immediate tempo but is not a ceasefire or confirmed settlement. AP, July 25
  2. Saudi Arabia intercepted missiles aimed at oil infrastructure. Saudi air defenses said they intercepted two ballistic missiles launched from Yemen toward the Yanbu refinery area, with another warning involving Jizan. The attacks keep energy facilities and shipping routes exposed even during the reported U.S.-Iran pause. Reuters, updated July 25 — link may be limited
  3. Small businesses sued over the new forced-labor tariffs. Burlap & Barrel and Collective Horology asked the U.S. Court of International Trade to block tariffs of 10% to 12.5% on goods from 60 trading partners, arguing the administration imposed them without the country- and practice-specific findings Section 301 requires. The case could test the legal basis of a policy affecting most U.S. imports. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited
  4. An appeals court kept the block on the $100,000 H-1B fee in place. The 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals rejected the administration’s request to pause a lower-court ruling that called the fee an unauthorized tax. The injunction remains effective while the government appeals, affecting employers and prospective skilled-worker visa applicants. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited
  5. The Cyclospora outbreak linked to shredded iceberg lettuce expanded. CDC reports 1,947 illnesses and 98 hospitalizations across nine states, with no deaths, linked to Taylor Farms lettuce served at Taco Bell. Because cases can take weeks to be reported, the totals may continue to rise after the implicated product leaves circulation. CDC investigation timeline, updated July 24 · AP explainer, July 24

2. Most Useful Development Today

What to do

Food-safety action

Check recalled shredded iceberg lettuce

CDC says the implicated Taylor Farms products were distributed in 27 states and supplied to Taco Bell locations. Consumers and businesses should use the product names, codes and distribution list in the recall notice to identify affected lettuce, then discard or return it. Cyclospora commonly causes prolonged watery diarrhea; people with symptoms should contact a clinician, especially if illness is persistent or severe.

Why this is usefulThe official notice contains the identifying details needed to act, while CDC’s timeline explains why recently reported illnesses may reflect exposure weeks earlier.

Education minister resigned after exam-leak protests

Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned after youth-led demonstrations over a leaked medical entrance examination affecting roughly two million students. Protest leaders ended their action following the resignation, although the integrity and administration of national exams remain under scrutiny. Reuters, July 25 — link may be limited

Judge ordered FDA to reconsider prescribing restrictions

A federal judge found that Biden-era special certification requirements for clinicians and pharmacies prescribing mifepristone were not adequately explained. The judge did not immediately erase the requirements; FDA must reconsider them, so the procedural next step is agency review rather than instant nationwide deregulation. 5. Policy & Benefits

Immigration, access and federal authority

Immigration · Employment

H-1B injunction survives the government’s stay request

The appeals court’s refusal to grant a stay means the lower court’s block on the $100,000 fee continues during the appeal. That is not a final ruling on the merits, but it prevents the administration from collecting the fee under the challenged policy for now. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

Trade policy · Small business

Tariff challenge targets the administration’s statutory findings

The new Court of International Trade suit argues that a broad forced-labor rationale cannot substitute for the specific investigation and findings required by Section 301. The filing does not suspend the tariffs; it begins a judicial test whose outcome could affect importers well beyond the two plaintiffs. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

Healthcare access · Reproductive health

Mifepristone ruling creates an agency process, not an immediate access change

Patients and prescribers should not treat the court decision as an immediate removal of certification rules. FDA must supply a legally adequate explanation or revise its policy after reconsideration, and further litigation is possible. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Verified actions and approvals

FDA · Vision

FDA approved Lytenava for wet age-related macular degeneration

The approval covers an ophthalmic formulation of bevacizumab for wet AMD and gives Outlook Therapeutics its first U.S.-approved product. The company expects availability near year-end; approval does not mean the medicine is on pharmacy or clinic shelves immediately. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

FDA · Pain relief

FDA approved an over-the-counter acetaminophen–naproxen combination

The combination is intended to provide up to 12 hours of pain relief. Because both ingredients already have important dosing, liver, stomach, kidney and interaction considerations, approval is not a reason to combine it with other acetaminophen- or NSAID-containing products without checking the label. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

FDA advisers · Compounding

Advisers backed broader compounding access for six peptides

After a two-day review, FDA advisers recommended allowing broader compounding access for six unapproved peptides while excluding sleep-inducing emideltide. The recommendations are nonbinding, and agency staff emphasized gaps in safety and effectiveness evidence; this is not an FDA approval of the compounds as treatments. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5

Anthropic launched its new flagship model for complex, long-running and coding tasks, priced at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens.
