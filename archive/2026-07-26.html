Sunday, July 26, 2026 Updated after the 10:30 a.m. ET Supreme Court check

Today’s edition centers on a second day of U.S.–Iran de-escalation, mass wildfire evacuations in France and Spain, the deadly Berlin Pride attack, the highest U.S. measles count in 35 years, and fresh violence in the West Bank.

1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

Ranked by public importance
  1. The U.S.–Iran pause held for a second day while technical talks advanced. The Pentagon halted bombing after 13 consecutive nights, Iran did not report new weekend attacks on neighboring countries, and Iran confirmed several rounds of Oman-mediated technical discussions about Strait of Hormuz transit. The lull is meaningful but remains a pause, not a signed ceasefire or final settlement. AP, July 26 · Reuters, July 26 — link may be limited
  2. Wildfires forced roughly 220,000 people from one French region and advanced toward Bordeaux. Another 55,000 people were evacuated overnight as flames moved within about 15 kilometers of Bordeaux; Spain also continued large-scale evacuations. The event has become a major cross-border emergency affecting homes, transportation, tourism and public health. AP, July 26
  3. Germany classified the Berlin Pride crowd-ramming as a terrorist attack. A minivan struck pedestrians near the Christopher Street Day celebration Saturday night, killing one person and injuring 29, including eight seriously. Police identified a suspect known to security services and warned the public not to approach him while the manhunt continues. Reuters, July 26 — link may be limited
  4. U.S. measles cases reached their highest annual total in 35 years. CDC counted 2,318 confirmed cases as of July 23, already exceeding all of 2025; 93% were associated with outbreaks. The agency continues to recommend two MMR doses as the best protection. CDC data, updated July 24 · AP, July 24
  5. Israeli settlers set fire to two West Bank mosques after a deadly clash. Palestinian officials reported attacks on villages and religious sites after Friday violence killed four Palestinians and two Israelis. The Israeli military said police were investigating the arson and reinforced units in the West Bank, increasing the risk of further escalation. Reuters, July 26 — link may be limited

2. Most Useful Development Today

ADA rights check

36th ADA anniversary

Use today to check which civil-rights protections apply

The Americans with Disabilities Act was signed 36 years ago today. It bars disability discrimination in employment, state and local government services, public transit, businesses open to the public and telecommunications. ADA coverage can include a current impairment, a record of an impairment, or being regarded as having one; the exact title and enforcement route depend on the setting.

Practical next stepStart with DOJ’s plain-language coverage guide. If the facts appear to involve an ADA violation, use the official complaint page to identify the responsible agency and submission route; preserve dates, written requests, responses and accessibility barriers.

3. U.S. & World

Indo-Pacific security

U.S. and Japan joined Philippine South China Sea drills

The five-day exercises used warships, coast guard vessels and aircraft to improve interoperability. They followed confrontations in which the Philippines said Chinese personnel struck a sailor and used water cannons; China said Philippine vessels were the aggressors. Reuters, July 26 — link may be limited

U.S. elections

Maine Democrats selected Troy Jackson for a pivotal Senate race

Party delegates chose the former state Senate president and fifth-generation logger to replace Graham Platner on the November ballot. Jackson will face Republican Senator Susan Collins in a contest that could help determine control of the Senate. AP, July 25

United Nations · Human rights

General Assembly extended the U.N. human-rights chief’s mandate

The assembly voted 144–10, with 13 abstentions, to give Volker Türk another four-year term despite opposition from the United States, Russia and Israel. Türk will become the first high commissioner for human rights to serve two full terms. Reuters, July 24 — link may be limited

Immigration detention · Speech

Appeals court refused to let the government re-detain a Georgetown scholar

A divided 4th Circuit held that immigration law did not prevent a federal judge from hearing Badar Khan Suri’s claims that detention punished protected pro-Palestinian speech. The ruling keeps Suri free while immigration proceedings continue; it does not terminate the government’s removal case, and other circuits have reached conflicting conclusions. Reuters, July 23 — recent fallback; link may be limited

Germany · Refugee law

Top court barred blanket cancellation of Afghan resettlement pledges

Germany’s Constitutional Court ruled that officials could not void about 640 prior admission promises without considering individual circumstances. The government still has immigration discretion, but it must make lawful case-specific decisions and continue supporting the family that brought the complaint while reconsideration proceeds. Reuters, July 24 — recent fallback; link may be limited

5. Policy & Benefits

Disability and civil rights

Disability rights

The ADA turns 36 today

The anniversary marks the continuing federal prohibition on disability discrimination across employment, government services, transportation, public accommodations and communications. DOJ’s guide explains that protection is broader than benefit eligibility and does not depend on receiving SSDI or SSI. Official DOJ ADA guide

Enforcement routes

ADA complaints go to different agencies depending on the barrier

Employment, transportation, housing, education and access to state or local programs can involve different filing offices and deadlines. DOJ’s official routing page is the safest starting point before assuming one complaint form covers every issue. Official ADA complaint guide

6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Verified surveillance updates

United States · Measles

CDC reported 2,318 cases across 45 jurisdictions

The new count is the country’s highest annual total since 1991, with five months still remaining. CDC says 93% of confirmed cases are outbreak-associated; its vaccination page recommends two MMR doses for best protection and includes separate travel guidance. CDC case data · CDC vaccination guidance

Democratic Republic of Congo · Ebola

Confirmed Ebola cases passed 3,000

Government data showed 3,075 confirmed cases and 1,354 deaths as of Saturday. The sharp cumulative toll confirms that the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak remains a major regional public-health emergency; the figures describe reported cases, not an estimate of all infections. Reuters, July 25 — link may be limited

Australia · H5N1

Queensland became Australia’s fourth state to confirm H5N1

Authorities said a migratory seabird tested positive for highly pathogenic avian influenza. The finding expands animal surveillance geographically; it is not a report of human infection. Reuters, July 25 — link may be limited
