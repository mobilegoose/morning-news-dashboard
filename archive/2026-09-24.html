2. Most Useful Development

Earlier this week · September 22

Federal Marketplace cancellations: check your coverage status if you received a notice

On September 22, CMS said it canceled about 315,000 marketplace enrollments covering more than 760,000 people, citing unauthorized or potentially improper enrollments. Those figures are the agency's reported administrative actions, not a finding that every affected individual committed fraud. People who receive coverage notices should verify their status through their Marketplace account and preserve correspondence.

CMS official announcement · AP reporting and context

Challenge to Medicaid work-rule medical exemptions receives new reporting

A September 24 report details a lawsuit filed the preceding week by five Medicaid recipients, medical organizations and a municipality. 6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Research announcement · September 23

NIH-funded Alzheimer's research maps individual brain-cell patterns

NIH described a large comparative single-cell mapping project spanning Alzheimer's and related brain disorders on September 23. The maps are research resources that could inform future diagnostics and treatments; the announcement is not an approved therapy or proof of a clinical benefit.

NIH research announcement

Animal research and observational analysis · September 23

Experimental compound reverses some MS-like deficits in mice, not yet proven for MS patients

NIH reported that kamuvudine-9 reduced neurologic damage and improved existing vision and motor impairment in a mouse model. A separate observational claims analysis of other antiviral drugs suggests an association, not proof that the experimental compound treats human multiple sclerosis. Human MS trials are still needed.
