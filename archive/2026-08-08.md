# Morning News Dashboard

**Saturday, August 8, 2026**  
Updated after the final same-morning Supreme Court check.

## 1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

### 1. Senate passed a sweeping Russia-sanctions bill, 86–11

The bipartisan package would sanction Russian leaders, financial institutions, energy projects and older reflagged oil tankers, while authorizing tariffs on leading buyers of Russian oil and gas. It now goes to the House, so none of its new penalties is law yet. **Why it matters:** this is Congress’s most substantial move of President Trump’s second term to pressure Russia and countries financing its war revenue. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/senate-russia-sanctions-graham-tariffs-8d37498c3f27d96cae7da147250ebe9f)

### 2. Senate approved temporary federal funding through December 11

The 90–6 vote came nearly two months before current funding expires. The Senate text would preserve housing and food-assistance adjustments, restrict transfers to border enforcement and pause a White House grant-control rule. The House passed a different version, so a shutdown is not yet averted. **Why it matters:** beneficiaries and federal workers have an unusually early signal of bipartisan support for continuity, but the chambers still must reconcile the bills before September 30. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/a50b86875105a299498b846184a3736d) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-senate-passes-short-term-funding-bill-avert-federal-shutdown-before-election-2026-08-08/)

### 3. Todd Blanche was confirmed as attorney general, 50–49

Two Republicans joined every Democrat in opposition. Blanche, President Trump’s former personal lawyer and acting attorney general since April, won the decisive vote after written commitments to abandon a proposed $1.8 billion compensation fund and narrow an IRS settlement. **Why it matters:** he now has confirmed authority over DOJ amid continuing concerns about departmental independence. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/senate-blanche-attorney-general-justice-department-fcb4157d45d7b13a05de4d2b0f4f92e2)

### 4. Appeals court halted aboveground White House ballroom construction

A divided D.C. Circuit panel upheld a preliminary injunction, ruling that the administration cannot continue the $400 million ballroom without congressional authorization. Underground national-security work may continue, and the order is stayed for 14 days while the administration seeks Supreme Court review. **Why it matters:** the ruling draws a concrete limit on unilateral executive construction, but it is not a final Supreme Court decision. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/trump-ballroom-court-ruling-1528247275ec6103da892d271d4ee883)

### 5. Thailand school-shooting death toll rose to at least nine

Police said a 12-year-old girl died Saturday after Friday’s attack near Bangkok. Authorities say a 14-year-old killed his grandparents, five school staff members and himself; 14 other people remained hospitalized, seven critically. **Why it matters:** the country is confronting one of its deadliest recent shootings and renewed scrutiny of firearm access. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/thailand-school-shooting-nonthaburi-bodies-forensic-63f7b1f3b97842251c98b5932f1d234a) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/thailand-school-shooting-toll-rises-nine-after-death-12-year-old-girl-police-say-2026-08-08/)

## 2. Most Useful Development Today

### The funding vote protects important guardrails—but it is not the final bill

The Senate’s continuing resolution keeps current funding broadly in place through December 11 and includes adjustments for housing and food assistance. It would also temporarily block a rule giving political appointees control over large grant allocations. Because the House version differs, agencies, grantees and beneficiaries should watch the conference result rather than treat today’s vote as enacted protection. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/a50b86875105a299498b846184a3736d) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-senate-passes-short-term-funding-bill-avert-federal-shutdown-before-election-2026-08-08/)

### New Free Journal Articles

#### [Randomized trial of intraoperative nitrous oxide for postoperative pain reduction in cervical spinal cord injury](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13446972/)

*Pain Reports* · electronically published August 5, 2026 · **Peer-reviewed randomized trial.** A single center randomized 80 adults having emergency surgery for traumatic cervical spinal-cord injury (AIS C or D); 59 were analyzed (mean age 70.3, 78% male). Nitrous oxide did not improve the primary outcome and acute day-one pain was higher than with air (mean NRS 5.10 vs 3.27; p=.021). A six-month reduction in resting pain was exploratory (0.53 vs 1.72; p=.030), so it is hypothesis-generating rather than an established treatment effect. **Why it matters:** chronic neuropathic pain is a major functional burden after SCI. **Material limitation:** single center, 21 of 80 randomized participants excluded from analysis, and the encouraging long-term result was secondary. [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13446972/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42565101/)

#### [Eligibility for Anti-Amyloid Therapies in Patients With Biomarker-Confirmed Atypical Alzheimer Disease Phenotypes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13445492/)

*Neurology* · electronically published August 5, 2026 · **Peer-reviewed retrospective eligibility analysis.** Mayo Clinic researchers applied major trial and appropriate-use criteria to 184 biomarker-confirmed patients with posterior cortical atrophy, logopenic aphasia, dysexecutive Alzheimer disease or corticobasal syndrome due to Alzheimer disease. Depending on the framework, 70%–85% would be ineligible, often because standard bedside cognitive-score cutoffs excluded patients despite early symptomatic disease. This is an association with selection rules, not evidence that anti-amyloid drugs work or fail in atypical disease. **Why it matters:** rigid criteria may shape access for people whose disability presents outside typical memory-first patterns. **Material limitation:** retrospective, single tertiary-center analysis of theoretical eligibility—not treatment outcomes. [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13445492/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42555876/)

#### [Classification of sporadic Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease based on resting-state scalp EEG-derived indices](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13446709/)

*PLOS ONE* · electronically published August 6, 2026 · **Peer-reviewed exploratory diagnostic-classification study.** Researchers compared six people with sporadic CJD, 23 with Alzheimer disease and 10 healthy older adults. An EEG-feature classifier reached 84.6% in leakage-controlled nested leave-one-out validation; the higher 97.4% headline accuracy came from the selected model. **Why it matters:** sporadic CJD is rapidly disabling and difficult to diagnose early, and it is on SSA’s Compassionate Allowances list. **Material limitation:** only six CJD cases, no independent external cohort and no demonstrated clinical utility; this is early-stage signal work, not an available diagnostic replacement. [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13446709/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42560982/)
