### Riyadh airport reopens as Saudi defenses intercept new Houthi attacks

King Khalid International Airport reopened terminals 1 and 2 for domestic and international flights Sunday, more than two weeks after strikes killed 17 people and wounded more than 330. Saudi Arabia said its defenses also intercepted a ballistic missile and drone aimed at Riyadh and 10 ballistic missiles aimed at the south. ### Starlink speeds return after Musk acknowledges throttling Ukraine's service

Ukraine said Starlink and the military-focused Starshield network returned to prior speeds Saturday after restrictions disrupted drone missions and air defenses for more than a week. Elon Musk said SpaceX imposed limits because civilian Starlink may not be used for offensive strikes; Ukrainian officials dispute that explanation, saying existing technical limits already prevented that use. Restoration is operationally significant, while responsibility for the interruption and control of Starshield remain contested.

### Justice Department sends all California counties a 48-hour ballot demand

Letters dated Friday told county election officials to help verify people on federal lists and segregate mail ballots whose voters' citizenship could not be confirmed, warning of federal scrutiny and possible legal exposure. The lists are part of roughly 20,000 registrations the administration alleges may involve noncitizens. California officials dispute the data and the deadline, and the demand itself does not prove any listed voter is ineligible.

## Most Useful Development Today

### Use total annual cost—not the premium alone—when comparing job-based health plans

KFF's new survey of 1,937 employers found that workers now contribute an average $7,471 toward family premiums, up 9% in one year; the average deductible among workers with a general single-coverage deductible is $1,955. During employer open enrollment, compare payroll premiums, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, drug formulary, network, employer HSA or HRA funding and spouse-plan rules together. The survey reports national averages, so your plan documents and Summary of Benefits and Coverage control your actual costs.

Audi says it will build a Georgia plant with capacity for up to 200,000 vehicles a year and approximately 4,500 jobs. Days of heavy rain pushed rivers to record or near-record levels across New Mexico, Colorado, Arizona and the Navajo Nation. Around 2,000 people were affected near Farmington and several hundred needed rescue; evacuations remained because utilities were damaged and additional storms could trigger new surges. ### Japan's revised imperial law takes effect with male-only succession intact

The law now permits adoption of unmarried male descendants from distant paternal-line branches and lets princesses retain royal status after marrying commoners, while excluding their spouses and children from royal status. It leaves Princess Aiko outside the succession despite broad public support for female succession. The changes address a shrinking royal workforce but preserve the system's central gender restriction.

### Workers' average family-premium contribution rises 9%

KFF's 28th annual employer survey found average family premiums of $28,303 and an average worker contribution of $7,471—$621 more than in 2025. Workers at firms with 10–99 employees paid substantially more on average than those at larger firms. The 1,937-employer survey describes national patterns, not any one plan, but it quantifies the access pressure behind this year's enrollment choices.

### FDA creates a cross-center neuroscience program with an Alzheimer's prevention focus

The Neuroscience Advancement and Innovation Nexus will coordinate drug, biologic and device regulators on brain and nervous-system product development. Its first initiative, the Alzheimer's Prevention Accelerator, is intended to work on biomarkers, digital tools, trial design and stage-appropriate endpoints for delaying or preventing disease. FDA announced a regulatory-science program, not a new therapy, approval or clinical result, and did not give a delivery timetable.

[Official FDA announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-new-program-advance-neuroscience-medical-product-development)

### Energy Department adds helium to the critical-materials list

The designation recognizes helium's role in semiconductor manufacturing, medical imaging, aerospace and advanced energy systems. It enables greater federal financing eligibility and closer supply-chain monitoring; it does not create new domestic supply by itself. The move follows disruption and concentration risks in the global helium market.

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### France reports the first documented human Shamonda-virus infection

The virus, previously known as a livestock infection spread by biting midges, was detected in a severely immunocompromised patient who later died. Authorities have not established whether the virus caused the death, and ECDC and WHO assess wider spread as unlikely on current evidence. One exceptional case does not establish sustained human transmission or a general-population emergency, but it changes surveillance assumptions for this animal virus.

### New Free Journal Articles

### Four-target antibody strategy strengthens NK-cell activity in esophageal-cancer models

Journal for ImmunoTherapy of Cancer · peer-reviewed preclinical study. Researchers engineered antibodies that bind EGFR and MET on esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma and two natural-killer-cell receptors, then tested them with cancer cell lines, NK cells from multiple donors and mouse xenograft and metastasis models. The combination increased NK-cell killing by up to 30% in vitro and reduced tumor volume by 82.3% in an early-stage mouse model and 65.7% in an advanced model versus controls. Why it matters: it offers a multi-target strategy for heterogeneous and drug-resistant disease. Major limits: there was no human treatment trial, mouse tumors do not reproduce patient outcomes, donor and animal sample counts were not clear in the abstract, and the antibodies also induced NK-cell exhaustion. This is early translational evidence, not an available therapy.

[Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13691859/) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42914560/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-015492)
