## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Russian missiles and drones kill at least 20 people across Ukraine

Russia launched roughly 70 missiles and waves of drones overnight, striking Kyiv and several other regions. A missile destroyed part of a five-story apartment building in Pryluky, where officials reported at least 14 deaths, including four children; strikes elsewhere raised the nationwide toll to at least 20 and forced emergency power cuts in five regions. Ukraine reported intercepting more than 80% of 46 jet-powered drones, but said ballistic missiles remain the hardest threat to stop.

### Houthi missile and drone attacks widen as Saudi-led forces intensify the Yemen offensive

Yemen's Houthis claimed attacks on Riyadh's King Khalid airport, Abha airport and military sites in southern Saudi Arabia; the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile north of Riyadh. The Houthis also struck toward Aden as Saudi and allied Yemeni forces expanded ground and air operations. Flight disruptions and fighting near the Bab el-Mandeb shipping corridor raise the risk of a broader regional and trade shock.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/4c2cf27f819b6e36940f88a7af49ea78) · [Reuters (potentially limited)](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/houthis-launch-missiles-yemens-aden-airport-fighting-intensifies-2026-10-07/)

### Justices begin hearing whether the Air Force must review environmental harm before renewing a Guam explosives permit

In Department of the Air Force v. Prutehi Guahan, a Guam conservation group challenges the Air Force's continued open detonation of obsolete munitions near ancestral lands, fishing areas, endangered-turtle habitat and a major drinking-water aquifer. The Air Force asked the Court to reverse a Ninth Circuit ruling that its permit-renewal application was reviewable final agency action and required National Environmental Policy Act analysis. The Court began merits argument today; it has not ruled. The decision will determine when federal permit applications can be challenged and whether a hazardous-waste permitting law displaces NEPA review.

[Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-579.html) · [Cornell case explainer](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/cert/25-579)

### The U.S. trade deficit jumps 13.7% as imports reach a record

The August goods-and-services deficit widened by $12.7 billion to $105.6 billion. Imports rose 4.3% to a record $420.8 billion while exports increased 1.4% to $315.2 billion; capital-goods imports and imports from Mexico and Vietnam also set records. The data are a concrete sign that tariffs have not eliminated the monthly trade gap and may weigh on third-quarter growth, although the year-to-date deficit remains 19.9% below 2025.

[Census Bureau release](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/current/) · [BEA report](https://www.bea.gov/index.php/news/2026/us-international-trade-goods-and-services-august-2026)

### HHS creates an in-house legal team for unaccompanied migrant children

The Department of Health and Human Services is establishing a legal-representation division inside the same department that holds unaccompanied children in federal custody. HHS says the lawyers will provide direct advice and help reunify families; independent legal-aid groups warn that government-employed counsel may not provide the independence children need while defending against removal. The move follows the end of a network that had managed about 24,000 cases and a new $244 million contract with Our Rescue.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/66f27919898d2c2bfa18279c1c85d8c3)

## Most Useful Development Today

### Thousands of disabled beneficiaries may have missed higher retirement benefits

A Social Security inspector-general follow-up estimates that SSA failed to tell 8,726 disability beneficiaries—whose payments were reduced because of workers' compensation or public-disability benefits—that filing for early retirement might pay more. Another 4,785 people may be owed about $41 million after filing but receiving the wrong amount. SSA accepted all four recommendations. Practical takeaway: if SSDI is offset by workers' compensation or a public disability benefit and you are old enough for retirement benefits, ask SSA to compare the amounts and document the answer; people who already elected retirement should ask whether the payment was computed correctly.

[SSA OIG findings](https://oig.ssa.gov/news-releases/2026-10-06-ssa-missed-opportunities-to-inform-disabled-beneficiaries-of-their-potential-eligibility-for-higher-retirement-benefits/) · [Full audit and recommendations](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/follow-disabled-beneficiaries-who-are-eligible-higher-retirement-insurance-benefits)

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Today's merits argument tests reviewability and environmental analysis for Guam detonations

Prutehi Guahan says the Air Force should have completed NEPA review before applying to renew a permit for open detonation of hazardous munitions at Tarague Beach; the Air Force says its application was not a final agency action and that the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act supplies the relevant environmental process. The Ninth Circuit let the nonprofit's suit proceed. The Supreme Court granted certiorari in March and began hearing merits argument today; it did not grant or deny emergency relief today. The ruling could affect environmental review and the timing of Administrative Procedure Act suits across federal agencies.

[Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-579.html) · [Official argument page](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/audio/2026/25-579) · [Detailed neutral explainer](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/cert/25-579)

### Yesterday's argument asks how much 401(k) evidence workers must plead before discovery

Intel plan participants allege that fiduciaries exposed retirement accounts to excessive risk and underperformance through hedge funds, private equity and other alternatives. Intel asked courts to require a closely comparable 'meaningful benchmark' before the suit can proceed; the workers argue that this imposes an extra pleading barrier when an unusual investment has no twin. The Ninth Circuit dismissed the claim, the Supreme Court granted certiorari and heard merits argument October 6; no decision has issued. The ruling will affect whether ERISA prudence cases reach discovery and trial.

[Official audio and transcript](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/audio/2026/25-498) · [Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-498.html) · [AARP case explainer](https://www.aarp.org/aarp-foundation/our-work/legal-advocacy/2026-supreme-court-preview/erisa-pleading-standard/)

## Policy & Benefits

### SSA agrees to fix notification and payment controls for higher retirement benefits

The inspector general estimates missed notices affected 8,726 disabled beneficiaries and inadequate documentation affected another 3,659. It also estimates SSA may be able to pay about $41 million to 4,785 beneficiaries who filed for retirement but were not paid the correct higher amount. The audit does not guarantee an individual payment; SSA agreed to identify similar cases, correct records and strengthen follow-up.

[SSA OIG](https://oig.ssa.gov/news-releases/2026-10-06-ssa-missed-opportunities-to-inform-disabled-beneficiaries-of-their-potential-eligibility-for-higher-retirement-benefits/)

### Audit estimates incorrect denials and $1.2 million in underpayments after later approvals

SSA OIG reviewed 100 child-benefit claims initially denied on disability grounds and later approved; only 49 were handled correctly. Projecting from the sample, auditors estimated about 600 incorrect denials, 450 cases missing required documentation and 1,100 approved claimants with incorrect entitlement dates or payment calculations. SSA agreed to correct identified records and address why employees failed to adopt prior disability findings.

[SSA OIG findings](https://oig.ssa.gov/news-releases/2026-10-06-report-finds-ssa-can-improve-accuracy-of-child%E2%80%99s-insurance-benefit-disability-determinations/) · [Audit record](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/childs-insurance-benefit-claims-denied-disability)

### FTC sends GOAT refund payments to 6,205 customers

The agency is distributing more than $100,000 by check and PayPal to customers affected by allegedly deceptive expedited-shipping and refund practices at sneaker marketplace GOAT. Checks must be cashed within 90 days and PayPal payments accepted within 30 days. The FTC says recipients do not have to pay or provide account information to receive the money.

[FTC refund announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/10/ftc-issues-redress-payments-consumers-impacted-goats-deceptive-shipping-refund-policies) · [Refund FAQ and contact](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/goat-refunds)

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### New study documents major school disruption among adolescents with ME/CFS

A peer-reviewed cross-sectional analysis of 40 adolescents in CDC's multisite ME/CFS study found substantial symptom and functional burden: 64% received homebound or homeschool instruction, 89% missed school because of illness, and participants missed a mean 10.8 school days in the preceding month. Persistent post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, fatigue and cognitive symptoms were common. This supports early clinical and educational accommodations, but the small specialty-clinic sample cannot establish prevalence or treatment effects.

[PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42838410/)

### ACP recommends hormone therapy first for eligible patients seeking relief from hot flashes and night sweats

The American College of Physicians' new guideline recommends estrogen plus a progestogen for patients with a uterus and estrogen alone for those without one, when treatment is sought and no contraindication applies. It suggests venlafaxine or desvenlafaxine second line, then selected SSRIs, gabapentin or neurokinin-receptor antagonists. Treatment should be individualized for risks, preferences, cost and access; the recommendation is not a reason to start hormones without clinician review.

[ACP guideline summary and document](https://www.acponline.org/pharmacologic-treatments-for-females-with-menopausal-vasomotor-symptoms-a-clinical-guideline-from) · [ACP release](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/acp-recommends-hormonal-therapy-as-a-first-line-treatment-for-menopausal-vasomotor-symptoms) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42832802/)

### Annals retracts and republishes a randomized trial of fluvoxamine and metformin for fatigue

Annals of Internal Medicine issued a formal retraction-and-republication notice for its March randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 399 adults with Long COVID fatigue and posted a corrected article under a new DOI. The original article is now marked retracted. Until the correction is fully compared with the prior report, readers should cite and rely on the republished version rather than the earlier paper; this editorial action does not by itself establish whether either drug works.

[Retraction and republication notice](https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-26-03050) · [Republished trial](https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-26-02144) · [Original article marked retracted](https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-25-03959)

It supports an 8,000-token context and modular encoders, with Google reporting about 567 MB of active RAM for the quantized full model on a Pixel 11 Pro. The release is useful for offline search and RAG, but company benchmark claims still need independent replication.

[Google DeepMind release](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2/)
