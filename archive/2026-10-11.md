### Riyadh airport attack toll rises to 12 dead and more than 300 injured

Saudi authorities said the missile strike on King Khalid International Airport killed 12 people, including a U.S. citizen, and injured more than 300. Airport operations were suspended through Sunday evening, and the Saudi-led coalition blamed Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthis. The deadliest Gulf-country attack since the Iran war began raises the risk of a wider response while disrupting a major regional air hub.

### Category 4 Simon pounds Mexico’s west-central coast

Hurricane Simon was moving just offshore Sunday with sustained winds near 150 mph, exposing coastal communities and Puerto Vallarta to catastrophic winds, life-threatening storm surge, flash flooding and mudslides. Jalisco closed schools in 22 coastal municipalities and began evacuations. The National Hurricane Center said the eye’s coast-parallel track made the exact landfall point uncertain, not the severity of the danger.

### At least 109 migrants die in a shipwreck off Djibouti

The International Organization for Migration said a boat carrying more than 300 people from Djibouti toward Yemen capsized near Guhere overnight Thursday into Friday. At least 109 people, including 15 children, died; more than 80 survived and at least 116 remained missing as search operations continued. The disaster underscores the lethal risks on the Horn of Africa–Gulf migration corridor.

U.S. Central Command said a fighter jet struck the stern of the Panama-flagged M/V Ocean Molica, also called Arika Sun, after repeated warnings in the Gulf of Oman. CENTCOM said the precision strike disabled propulsion without harming the crew. The action is a significant enforcement escalation around a chokepoint that carried roughly one-fifth of global oil consumption before the war.

### Justice Department opens an antitrust inquiry into the White House TV pool

DOJ said it is investigating whether ABC, CBS, CNN, Fox and NBC violated antitrust law when the television pool collectively paused White House coverage after the administration excluded CNN, MS NOW and Politico. Coverage has resumed and the inquiry is exploratory; no violation has been found. ## Most Useful Development Today

### New Medicare Advantage and Part D star ratings are now in Plan Finder

CMS published 2027 quality ratings for Medicare Advantage and stand-alone Part D plans in Medicare Plan Finder ahead of Open Enrollment, which runs October 15 through December 7. The practical move is to compare a plan’s star rating alongside total drug costs, pharmacy network, prior-authorization rules and provider access; a high rating is useful but does not guarantee the best fit for an individual’s medications or clinicians.

[CMS 2027 Star Ratings fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2027-medicare-advantage-part-d-star-ratings) · [CMS performance data](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-drug-plans/part-c-d-performance-data)

DOJ is now asking whether the five-network pool acted as an unlawful group boycott. An investigation is not a charge or finding, and the access lawsuit and antitrust inquiry address different questions: the government’s treatment of reporters versus coordination among competing networks.

A federal judge granted the Republican National Committee and North Carolina GOP permission to intervene alongside election officials in a suit brought by advocacy groups and voters challenging tightened mail-ballot procedures. Intervention makes the party groups defendants able to present their own arguments; it does not decide whether the rules are lawful. A hearing on the plaintiffs’ request to block the rules is scheduled for October 14.

## Policy & Benefits

### CMS releases the plan-quality data people can use starting October 15

The 2027 star ratings measure health-plan and drug-plan quality and are now displayed in Medicare Plan Finder. CMS says the ratings will also affect 2028 Medicare Advantage quality-bonus payments. People comparing plans should still enter their exact drugs and preferred pharmacies and verify clinicians directly, because ratings summarize plan-wide performance rather than personal cost or network fit.

[CMS 2027 Star Ratings fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2027-medicare-advantage-part-d-star-ratings) · [CMS performance data](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-drug-plans/part-c-d-performance-data)

### Federal funding will add clinics, opioid treatment and mobile crisis teams

CMS announced more than $17 million for rural North Carolina behavioral-health services: $4 million for three clinics, $3 million for medication treatment access in five counties, $4 million for mobile crisis teams in six more counties, and $6.7 million for early psychosis, regional crisis partnerships and perinatal care. This is system funding rather than a new cash benefit, and local availability will depend on implementation by participating providers.

[CMS funding announcement](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/trump-administration-announces-more-17-million-new-mental-health-clinics-mobile-crisis-teams)

### Court pauses a policy that prevented detained applicants from completing biometrics

A federal district judge stayed DHS’s policy of not collecting fingerprints and photos needed for detained applicants’ pending immigration cases, including asylum, U visas, T visas and special-immigrant juvenile applications. The stay preserves access to those application steps while the case proceeds; the court did not issue a final merits judgment and required a compliance report by October 21.

[District-court opinion and order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2026cv01510/291982/46/) · [Case explainer](https://nipnlg.org/news/press-releases/federal-court-blocks-trump-vance-policy-denying-detained-immigrants-access)

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### New Free Journal Articles

#### [EARLY-ALS study finds a retrospective pattern of subtle pre-diagnosis complaints](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352396426003890)

eBioMedicine · online October 9, 2026 · multicenter case-control study of 475 people with ALS and 285 controls across 20 centers in Germany and Switzerland. ALS participants retrospectively reported a higher burden of prodromal complaints (odds ratio 7.50, 95% CI 4.27–13.17), especially subtle motor, sensory and pain symptoms, plus different pre-onset healthcare use. Why it matters: it supports studying an earlier recognition window, not a screening test or proven prevention strategy. The major limitation is retrospective self-report and recall bias; associations require prospective validation.

[Free full text](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352396426003890) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854356/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106505)

#### [Review maps how cognition changes in progressive supranuclear palsy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13650757/)

Movement Disorders Clinical Practice · online October 9, 2026 · narrative review of longitudinal cognitive evidence in progressive supranuclear palsy, a rare, rapidly progressive neurodegenerative disorder. The authors describe executive dysfunction and slowed processing as central features and evaluate which bedside and trial measures may detect change over time. Why it matters: cognition strongly affects function, caregiving and trial design even when motor symptoms dominate. Limitations include heterogeneous cohorts and tests, no pooled sample or meta-analysis, and no evidence that a specific treatment changes cognitive decline.

[Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13650757/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42852583/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mdc3.70847)
