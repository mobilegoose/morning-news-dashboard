### Israel's election produces no clear governing majority

Partial official counts and revised exit polls left neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor former military chief Gadi Eisenkot's opposition alliance at the 61 Knesset seats needed to govern. Netanyahu remains caretaker while votes are finalized and President Isaac Herzog later selects a coalition-builder. ### Cyberattack disrupts the FEC's campaign-finance filing system

The Federal Election Commission kept its electronic filing system offline after an email warned of a bad actor trying to breach agency systems; by Tuesday evening the agency said it had identified the issue and was implementing a fix. Voting systems are not involved, but candidates and committees cannot submit time-sensitive 24- and 48-hour spending reports days before the midterms, reducing real-time public disclosure until service returns.

### Court declines emergency relief in the immigration-judge closure dispute

In City of Baltimore v. Texas, Baltimore, New Haven, Columbus and Centro Legal asked the Court to stay a Texas judgment that vacated a 2024 rule allowing immigration judges to pause removal cases, including while people pursue visas or humanitarian relief. The Court denied the stay in a one-sentence order without reasoning or noted dissent. This denial of interim relief is not a merits ruling or certiorari decision; the nationwide vacatur remains in force while the applicants' appeal continues.

[Official docket — No. 26A500](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26a500.html) · [Associated Press](https://www.durangoherald.com/articles/associated-press/supreme-court-wont-intervene-to-allow-judges-to-pause-some-deportation-proceedings/)

### Cyclone Arnab crosses Andhra Pradesh after mass evacuations

India's weather service said the very severe cyclonic storm completed landfall near Narsapur Wednesday morning with sustained winds of 125–135 km/h, gusting to 150 km/h, before weakening inland. Authorities evacuated more than 180,000 people and transport and power services were disrupted; no deaths were immediately reported. Flooding and damage assessments continue as the system moves north-northwest.

### IAEA says it sees no repair work—but still lacks access to Iran's nuclear sites

IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi told the U.N. Security Council that satellite monitoring has not identified repair or reconstruction at facilities damaged in 2025 and 2026 attacks. But inspectors remain outside Iran and cannot verify the current location or composition of the previously estimated 440.9 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60%. The absence of observed repairs is therefore not verification that material has not moved or enrichment has stopped.

## Most Useful Development Today

### New gene-editing pathway opens a concrete comment window

FDA issued draft guidance describing how multiple patient-specific genome-editing variants could fit within one biologics license application and, after approval, how in-range variants might be added using laboratory safety and activity data rather than a new trial. Comments are due November 27 in docket FDA-2026-D-11783. Separately, CMS plans an application to expand its voluntary Medicaid cell-and-gene-therapy access model beyond sickle cell disease. Neither step approves a therapy or guarantees coverage, but patients and rare-disease groups now have a specific regulatory record to influence.

### Judge rules Bill Essayli is unlawfully serving as Los Angeles's top federal prosecutor

### Court temporarily blocks a shorter deadline for refugees facing status termination

In Partnership for the Advancement of New Americans v. Edlow, refugee groups and individuals challenged a USCIS rule cutting the response period on termination notices from 30 days to 10. Judge Julia Kobick entered a 10-day administrative stay through November 6 and barred action on short-deadline notices issued since October 14. The order only preserves the status quo while the court considers fuller relief; it does not decide the merits or the lawsuit's separate challenges to re-vetting and green-card holds.

### Federal analysis reports continued declines in opioid exposure and deaths

HHS released an ASPE analysis saying the overall 2025 age-adjusted death rate fell to 689.2 per 100,000 and opioid-related deaths reached their lowest level since 2017. Pharmacy data from March 2015 to March 2026 show opioid prescriptions at 45.3% of baseline, total dosage at 35.2%, and buprenorphine or naltrexone patients at 218%. ### New Free Journal Articles

### Annual wellness visits are associated with more new dementia-medication prescriptions

JAMA Network Open published a peer-reviewed retrospective cohort study using 100% traditional Medicare claims for 799,268 beneficiaries age 68 or older with Alzheimer disease or related dementias and continuous Parts A, B and D coverage. New prescriptions rose from 9.8% among people with no annual wellness visit to 14.1% among those with at least three; after weighting, three or more visits were associated with a 16% higher prescription rate (HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.13–1.19). This links a covered preventive visit to symptom-treatment access, but it cannot establish causation: self-selection, claims-based severity, exclusion of Medicare Advantage and Part D restrictions may bias the association, and fills do not prove use or appropriateness. Published online October 27, 2026.

[Free full text — JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2855861) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40804)


## AI Today

### HHS removes federal certification requirements for AI model cards

The HTI-5 final rule, published today, removes 14 of 60 health-IT certification criteria and revises seven. Most notably, certified predictive decision-support tools will no longer have to expose standardized model-card details about training, intended use, limitations, subgroup performance or risk management. HHS says the information was not sufficiently used and estimates lower compliance costs; many commenters warned that removing it shifts transparency and bias-evaluation burdens to clinicians and health systems. Most provisions take effect 60 days after publication, with some later dates.

[Official Federal Register rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/28/2026-21943/health-data-technology-and-interoperability-onc-deregulatory-actions-to-unleash-prosperity) · [ONC criteria table](https://healthit.gov/resources/hti-5-final-rule-removals-or-revisions-of-certification-criteria/)
