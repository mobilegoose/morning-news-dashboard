1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

1 · Middle East

Trump holds off on new U.S. strikes while mediators pursue an Iran deal

President Trump said the United States would pause a planned new attack after regional allies outlined parameters that include reopening the Strait of Hormuz and addressing Iran’s nuclear program. No final agreement has been signed, Iran’s acceptance remains unclear, and fighting in Gaza continued.

Why it mattersA durable agreement could reduce immediate military and energy-market risk, but the announcement is a negotiating pause—not a ceasefire.

2 · Infrastructure security

Michigan confirms water-system cyberattacks as the multistate investigation grows

Nine Michigan water systems were affected after attacks disrupted more than 30 Minnesota systems. Officials said the Michigan systems remained safe and operational; the FBI is investigating, and authorities have not identified an attacker.

AP: Michigan update

3 · AI regulation

EU AI transparency rules and enforcement powers take effect

Article 50 disclosure duties now apply to certain chatbots, deepfakes and AI-generated public-interest content. The European AI Office and national authorities also begin supervising and enforcing the law; separate high-risk-system deadlines were extended.

European Commission: enforcement starts

4 · Public safety

Three people are killed and seven injured in an Idaho shooting

Police in Twin Falls found the suspected gunman dead nearby and said there was no continuing threat. Investigators had not announced a motive as of the morning update.

AP: Twin Falls shooting

5 · Labor and travel

WestJet cabin crews strike, canceling more than 300 flights

About 4,400 flight attendants began a strike over compensation for ground duties during Canada’s holiday weekend. WestJet Encore and code-share flights are unaffected; the airline says travelers on canceled flights may seek refunds or rebooking.

6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Evidence and practical health

Smoke exposure

Wildfire smoke risk rises with active western fires

Smoke can worsen asthma, breathing problems and cardiovascular symptoms. CDC recommends checking local air quality, keeping smoke outside, limiting exposure and using a well-fitting N95 or P100 when outdoor exposure cannot be avoided.

7. AI Today

Official sources first

European Union

Consumer-facing AI disclosures become enforceable

The new duties cover informing people when they interact with certain AI systems and marking specified synthetic content, including deepfakes and some public-interest text. The Commission’s guidance describes scope and exceptions; enforcement responsibility now sits with the AI Office and member-state authorities.

What changes in practiceDevelopers and deployers serving EU users need documented labeling and disclosure workflows. This does not mean every AI output must carry the same label.
