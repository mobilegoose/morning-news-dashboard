1 · SOCIAL SECURITY & SSI

Social Security announces a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027

SSA said Social Security benefits for nearly 72 million people will rise 3.6% beginning in January, while increased SSI payments for about 7.3 million recipients begin December 31. The agency estimates the average retired-worker benefit will increase by about $75 a month. Medicare premiums can reduce the net increase for beneficiaries whose premiums are deducted from their checks.

September inflation rises 0.6%; real hourly earnings fall 0.5%

The Consumer Price Index increased 0.6% in September and 3.7% over 12 months, with a 9.1% gasoline jump accounting for more than half of the monthly rise. Core inflation was 2.6% over the year. Separate BLS data show inflation-adjusted average hourly earnings fell 0.5% for the month and 0.7% over the year, underscoring the squeeze behind the new benefit adjustment.

4 · PUBLIC HEALTH

Congo Ebola outbreak exceeds 9,000 confirmed cases, with cross-border spread documented

WHO's latest situation report counted 9,066 confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases and 4,381 deaths through October 11 across 65 health zones in seven Congolese provinces, with imported cases in Kenya and Uganda. WHO said there was no sustained downward trajectory. Because no approved vaccine or specific treatment exists for this strain, isolation, contact tracing and supportive care remain central.

Most Useful Development Today

New benefit amounts and practical next steps

SSA · ACTIONABLE

The 2027 COLA changes benefit amounts and several work-related thresholds

The federal SSI maximum rises to $1,029 a month for an individual and $1,544 for a couple; resource limits remain $2,000 and $3,000. Substantial gainful activity rises to $1,770 a month for nonblind beneficiaries and $2,950 for blind beneficiaries, while the trial-work amount becomes $1,270. The average disabled-worker benefit is projected to rise from $1,642 to $1,701. COLA notices should appear online in late November for people who choose paperless delivery by November 18; otherwise SSA will mail notices in December. Check the personalized notice before budgeting because Medicare premiums and other deductions may change the deposited amount.

Policy & Benefits

Disability, healthcare and consumer impact

SSI · ELIGIBILITY TAKE-UP

Florida study finds automatic eligibility raised SSI participation among very-low-birth-weight infants—but many still did not enroll

A Pediatrics study used the 1,200-gram automatic-eligibility cutoff and records for 21,606 Florida infants born from 1990 through 2015. Infants just below the cutoff were 19.1 percentage points more likely to receive SSI than those just above it, but participation remained far from universal. The quasi-experimental result shows that eligibility rules affect take-up; one state's older birth cohorts and the narrow cutoff limit generalization.

OPIOIDS · FEDERAL HEALTH PROGRAMS

CVS agrees to pay $315 million to resolve unlawful-prescription and false-claims allegations

The Justice Department said CVS will pay $315 million to resolve civil allegations that pharmacies filled invalid controlled-substance prescriptions and billed many to Medicare and other federal programs from 2013 through 2025. CVS also entered a DEA agreement requiring a reporting hotline, proactive dispensing-pattern monitoring and review of suspect prescribers. A civil settlement resolves allegations without a trial finding of liability.

Justice Department settlement announcement

SOCIAL SECURITY · WORK RULES

2027 earnings limits and contribution base rise alongside the COLA

SSA set the 2027 taxable-earnings maximum at $192,300. The retirement earnings-test limit becomes $25,560 for people below full retirement age and $67,920 for people reaching full retirement age during 2027. These limits affect withholding and payroll taxes, not the 3.6% COLA itself; the agency's fact sheet provides monthly equivalents and the separate disability-work thresholds.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

New evidence with limitations

LONG COVID · RETROSPECTIVE COHORT

Autoimmune rheumatic disease is associated with higher coded Long COVID risk in a large U.S. health-record study

Arthritis & Rheumatology · online October 14, 2026 · matched N3C cohort of 187,106 people with a first recorded infection from September 2021 through December 2023. A Long COVID diagnosis or clinic visit appeared in 2.8% of patients with autoimmune rheumatic disease versus 1.5% of controls; the adjusted hazard ratio was 1.56. Rituximab, glucocorticoid use, pulmonary disease, obesity and acute severity were also associated with risk. This is association, not proof of causation; EHR coding likely misses cases and residual confounding remains.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEER-REVIEWED PILOT INTERVENTION

Medical-food supplementation and seizures in CDKL5 deficiency disorder

Epilepsia Open · online October 13, 2026 · longitudinal pilot intervention in 13 people with drug-resistant epilepsy; 10 completed both medical-food periods. A few participants had large seizure reductions, but responses were heterogeneous and the small cohort showed no reliable group-level efficacy signal. Why it matters: CDKL5 deficiency is a rare, severe neurologic disorder and the study tests a microbiome-directed adjunct. The uncontrolled design, tiny sample and within-person variability make this hypothesis-generating—not evidence to start treatment.

Artificial intelligence in suicide prevention: clinical promise and unresolved safety risks
