## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Scott Colom wins Mississippi's Senate race in a historic upset

Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith after officials finished processing enough absentee and provisional ballots for the race to be called Wednesday. Hyde-Smith conceded. Colom will be Mississippi's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction and the state's first newly elected Democratic senator since 1947, materially strengthening the Democratic majority that takes office in January.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/56c18f292a734443992f9466c3b0f346)

### Israeli airstrike hits Nasser Hospital, killing six and halting key services

Gaza health officials said the strike on the surgical wing killed six people, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old patient, and wounded at least 28. Israel said it targeted a Hamas commander and other militants but offered no evidence; the named commander was not among the dead. The attack damaged operating rooms and stopped surgery, radiology and laboratory services at one of Gaza's main remaining hospitals.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/50b547b5a82388b6b415aee4d776fbfb)

### Interior adopts a five-year strategy to shrink the federal estate through land sales

The Interior Department's new 2026–2030 strategic plan says the government will offer federal land to states, local governments and Tribes and focus federal resources on undefined 'nationally significant' lands. It identifies no parcels, acreage, prices or schedule. The plan is a policy roadmap, not an executed sale: protected lands and other federal holdings face statutory limits, and major transfers may require congressional cooperation.

[Official Interior strategic plan](https://www.doi.gov/libraries/pdf.js/web/viewer.html?file=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.doi.gov%2Fsites%2Fdefault%2Ffiles%2Fdocuments%2F2026-11%2Fdoi-strategic-plan-2026-11-10.pdf) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/9c6078b69f8525ab1b220e5aa321d62f)

White House counsel Will Scharf told incoming House Oversight chair Robert Garcia that threats, abusive process or subpoenas aimed at violating constitutional rights could warrant investigation. Garcia said Congress would proceed with oversight of the administration. The letter is a warning, not a criminal charge or court ruling, but it opens a consequential separation-of-powers conflict before Democrats take control of Congress in January.

## Most Useful Development Today

### FDA requires every GLP-1 medicine to warn about a rare eye condition

FDA ordered labeling changes for the full GLP-1 class—including semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide products—because of a potential increased risk of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION), which can cause sudden, painless and sometimes permanent vision loss in one eye. FDA's pooled analysis found 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years with GLP-1 drugs versus 3.8 with placebo or non-GLP-1 comparators. Patients should not stop treatment on their own, but sudden vision loss or a marked change in vision requires prompt medical attention.

[Official FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential)

### Nasser Hospital loses operating capacity after a deadly strike

The strike killed a working surgeon and patients, damaged the surgical wing and forced suspension of surgery, radiology and laboratory services. International humanitarian law protects hospitals and medical personnel unless they are being used for acts harmful to the enemy, and any attack still must satisfy distinction and proportionality requirements. Israel alleged militant use but had not supplied supporting evidence in the cited report.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/50b547b5a82388b6b415aee4d776fbfb)

### Indonesia's vice president resigns after courts question his eligibility

Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned after the Constitutional Court and Supreme Court found that his overseas education documents did not satisfy election requirements and 120 lawmakers began an impeachment process. President Prabowo Subianto accepted the resignation. Under Indonesia's Constitution, Parliament must choose a replacement from two presidential nominees within 60 days; there will not be a new national election.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/abfe5e288fad3b8b26719eae634f2ee0)

### Brazil's electoral court fines Facebook for late and incomplete election compliance

Brazil's top electoral court imposed a 7.5 million-real fine—about $1.3 million—after finding that Facebook failed to comply fully and on time with election-related orders. The court said it had not found evidence that the company intended to influence the election. The ruling is a platform-compliance penalty, not a finding that Facebook changed the result.

The incoming House Oversight chair says his committee will investigate possible corruption and abuse; the White House argues that process used merely to intimidate officials, or subpoenas that violate constitutional rights, could become criminal. ### Federal judge lets Trump's BBC defamation claim proceed but dismisses his trade-practices count

President Trump alleges that a BBC documentary falsely spliced separate portions of his January 6 speech to make him appear to urge violence. Judge Jeffrey Kuntz held that the complaint plausibly pleaded defamation and actual malice enough to reach discovery, while dismissing the Florida unfair-business-practices claim. This is not a finding that the BBC is liable; Trump must still prove falsity, fault, damages and the other elements.

### Judge rejects Peltola campaign's request for a centralized list of rejected absentee ballots

The campaign sought names and rejection reasons to help ensure qualified voters could cure or count their ballots. Superior Court Judge Thomas Matthews ruled that the request was not reasonable at this stage because election staff could not produce and redact the database records without disrupting the count and exposing confidential information. The ruling denies this records request; it does not decide the Senate election, whose ranked-choice tabulation is targeted for November 18.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/f42a49a45a2870786f36086c7ad02824)

### Supreme Court denies James Pavatt's execution stay and certiorari petition

Pavatt, sentenced to death for Rob Andrew's 2001 murder, argued that newly disclosed information showed prosecutors falsely told jurors he alone fired the fatal shots and that a juror's affidavit demonstrated the assertion affected the death verdict. The Court denied both emergency relief and review without explanation: the stay denial means the Court did not pause the November 12 execution, and the certiorari denial ends this requested Supreme Court review without deciding or endorsing the merits. No justice noted a dissent in the order.

## Policy & Benefits

### Interior's sale policy is now official strategy, but no land is yet identified for sale

The five-year plan directs Interior to reduce the federal estate and return part of sale proceeds to buyers for conservation elsewhere. Federal law bars sale of protected land and limits when other parcels can be sold; the plan itself does not override those constraints. The next meaningful checkpoints are parcel-specific proposals, environmental and land-use review, and any request for congressional authority.

[Official Interior strategic plan](https://www.doi.gov/libraries/pdf.js/web/viewer.html?file=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.doi.gov%2Fsites%2Fdefault%2Ffiles%2Fdocuments%2F2026-11%2Fdoi-strategic-plan-2026-11-10.pdf) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/9c6078b69f8525ab1b220e5aa321d62f)

### CMS finalizes the permanent negotiation framework for prices effective in 2029

CMS codified how it will select up to 20 Part D and Part B drugs, negotiate or renegotiate maximum fair prices, receive patient and manufacturer evidence and monitor generic or biosimilar competition beginning with the 2029 cycle. The rule takes effect December 5, 2026, and CMS says it will name the next drugs by February 1, 2027. It establishes process; it does not announce the 2029 drug list or prices.

[Official CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program-medicare-prescription-drug-benefit-program-final-rule-cms) · [Federal Register rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2026-22792)


## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### FDA finds a small but higher NAION rate in pooled GLP-1 trials and orders label changes

Across 126 randomized controlled trials, FDA calculated 6.4 NAION cases per 100,000 person-years among GLP-1 users and 3.8 among placebo or non-GLP-1 comparator groups. The absolute event rate was low, and the agency described the evidence as a potential increased risk rather than proof that every reported case was caused by the drug. The practical change is class-wide warnings and precautions, with prompt evaluation for sudden, painless one-eye vision loss.

[Official FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential)

### Nielsen v. Watanabe oral argument

A former federal prisoner alleges that a prison nurse ignored months of serious pain from a fractured coccyx; the nurse asks the Court to bar a judicially created damages action against the federal official, and the United States asks the Court to overrule the 1980 medical-care precedent that permitted such claims. The Court granted certiorari on June 22 and heard argument Monday; it has not decided the merits. The case may determine whether federal prisoners can still seek individual-capacity damages for deliberate indifference to medical needs.

Teaching hospitals challenge an HHS rule that counts labor-and-delivery beds when calculating their Medicare graduate-medical-education payments; adding beds lowers the payment ratio.
