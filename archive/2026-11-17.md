**Tuesday, November 17, 2026 · Updated after 10:45 a.m. Eastern**

## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Iran launches a third recent attack on U.S. targets in Bahrain

Iran fired drones at a U.S. radar facility in Bahrain today, ending a roughly two-month lull with a third attack on American military targets there in recent days. Bahrain said its defenses intercepted the drones; the U.S. military reported no injuries or equipment damage. The attack matters because Bahrain hosts the U.S. Navy's 5th Fleet and the renewed strikes could further complicate stalled diplomacy and already-constrained Gulf shipping.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/582e0b5d51be06f0ed630fac24f39b72)

### Ukraine says its drones and missiles severed the road link across the Kerch Bridge

Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia with occupied Crimea after a weeks-long operation against surrounding defenses. Ukraine's General Staff said a road arch and span collapsed, while Russia acknowledged damage and halted road traffic; rail traffic later resumed. The Associated Press could not independently verify Ukraine's imagery. The bridge is a major military-supply and civilian route, so losing the road span is strategically and symbolically significant even if repairs follow.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/df4b867cf8f70e88843d8fefea5d643c)

### Federal judge vacates DHS's fixed-term rule for international students and exchange visitors

U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV granted the challengers summary judgment and vacated DHS's rule replacing the long-standing duration-of-status system for F-1 students, J-1 exchange visitors and foreign-media representatives with fixed admission periods. The court held the rule arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act. This is a final merits judgment—not the earlier temporary injunction—so the prior framework remains controlling unless the ruling is stayed or reversed on appeal.

[November 16 court order](https://www.nafsa.org/sites/default/files/media/document/2026-11-16-District-Judge-Summary-Judgement.pdf) · [Case-status explainer and documents](https://www.nafsa.org/legal-defense-DS)

### CMS proposes sweeping fraud-control changes across federal health programs

CMS issued its CRUSH proposed rule, estimating nearly $90 billion in ten-year savings. The proposal would expand provider-ownership disclosures, enrollment denial and revocation authority, Medicare Advantage and Part D fraud reporting, Medicaid overpayment recovery, and Marketplace broker restrictions. These provisions are not yet final; public comments are due January 19, 2027.

[Official CMS release](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-crush-proposed-rule-aims-reap-nearly-90-billion-taxpayers-savings-targeting-fraud-across) · [Official fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/comprehensive-regulations-uncover-suspicious-healthcare-crush-proposed-rule-cms-6098-p) · [Proposed-rule PDF](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23461.pdf)

### FDA approves giredestrant plus everolimus for a defined metastatic breast-cancer group

FDA approved giredestrant with everolimus for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer after specified endocrine therapy, and authorized a companion liquid-biopsy test. In the 207-patient ESR1-mutated subgroup of the randomized evERA trial, median progression-free survival was 10 months versus 5.5 months with physician-chosen endocrine therapy plus everolimus; overall-survival data remain immature.

[Official FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-giredestrant-combination-everolimus-er-positive-her2-negative-esr1-mutated-locally)


## Most Useful Development Today

### Medicare Open Enrollment ends December 7—review the total cost, not only the premium

SSA reminded beneficiaries that Medicare's annual Open Enrollment window closes December 7 and changes generally take effect January 1, 2027. Compare plan notices against current prescriptions, pharmacies, clinicians, prior-authorization rules and annual out-of-pocket exposure. A zero-premium Medicare Advantage plan can still change networks or cost sharing, and Original Medicare users should separately assess Part D and Medigap options.

[Official SSA reminder](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/advocates/2026-11-16.html) · [Official Medicare plan comparison](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/)


## U.S. & World

### Bahrain intercepts Iranian drones aimed at an American radar facility

The U.S. says today's strike caused no casualties or equipment loss, but it follows two attacks on American radar facilities in Bahrain since Friday. Iran describes the strikes as retaliation for U.S. attacks and surveillance; Qatar and other Gulf states condemned them. The immediate military damage was limited, while the escalation risk is broader because the Persian Gulf and Strait of Hormuz remain under pressure.

Ukraine says it first degraded air defenses, radars and electronic-warfare equipment, then used drones and missiles against the bridge's road and rail sections. Russia says the bridge will be rebuilt and reports that rail traffic resumed. Separately, Russian strikes closed important bridges in Kyiv. The parties' battlefield claims remain partly unverifiable, but both sides acknowledge damage to the Kerch crossing.

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Presidents' Alliance v. DHS ends the fixed-period visa rule at the district-court level

A coalition of higher-education and labor groups challenged DHS's rule that would have replaced duration-of-status admission with fixed periods and new extension requirements. Judge Saylor granted the plaintiffs summary judgment and vacated the entire rule under 5 U.S.C. § 706, finding DHS inadequately considered existing enforcement tools and imposed restrictions outside its reasoned justification. The government may appeal and seek a stay, but an appeal alone would not revive the rule.

[November 16 court order](https://www.nafsa.org/sites/default/files/media/document/2026-11-16-District-Judge-Summary-Judgement.pdf) · [Litigation documents and procedural history](https://www.presidentsalliance.org/duration-of-status-litigation/)

### Judge orders the White House to produce targeted records in the ABA's law-firm intimidation case

In American Bar Association v. Executive Office of the President, Judge Amir Ali partly granted the ABA's motion to compel communications and draft materials concerning nine law firms and firms the administration allegedly targeted. The ABA claims a coordinated campaign punished firms for clients, speech and diversity work. The ruling opens targeted discovery; it does not decide whether the administration violated the Constitution.

[Readable Bloomberg Law report](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/judge-approves-key-aba-records-request-in-suit-over-firm-attacks) · [Reuters — access may be limited](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/white-house-ordered-hand-over-records-lawsuit-over-trumps-law-firm-crackdown-2026-11-17/)

### Virginia plaintiffs challenge ICE's alleged pattern of warrantless civil arrests

Centreville Immigration Forum and six residents filed a proposed class action in the Eastern District of Virginia alleging that ICE arrests people without a warrant and without probable cause both of unlawful presence and likely escape before a warrant can be obtained. They seek declaratory and injunctive relief. The complaint records the plaintiffs' allegations; DHS and ICE have not yet answered, and the court has not ruled on the merits.

## Policy & Benefits

### CRUSH would expand screening, reporting, recovery and broker-enforcement tools

Among the proposal's practical provisions: CMS could deny or revoke Medicare enrollment when owners cannot be verified or create undue fraud risk; Medicare Advantage and Part D plans would report suspected or substantiated fraud; states would strengthen Medicaid provider data and recovery processes; and CMS could suspend Marketplace brokers over credible allegations of consumer harm. CMS's $90 billion figure is an estimate for a proposal, not realized savings.

[Official CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/comprehensive-regulations-uncover-suspicious-healthcare-crush-proposed-rule-cms-6098-p) · [Proposed-rule PDF](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23461.pdf)

### FHA reports 935,000 borrowers served and a 10.29% insurance-fund capital ratio

HUD's FY 2026 report says FHA supported more than 935,000 borrowers, including over 530,000 first-time buyers. The Mutual Mortgage Insurance Fund's overall capital ratio was 10.29%, down 1.18 percentage points from FY 2025 but above the 2% statutory minimum. The reverse-mortgage portfolio's ratio fell more sharply to 5.43%, a risk indicator worth watching alongside the headline homeownership figures.

[Official HUD release and report links](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-092)

### Minnesota secures a 10-year oversight agreement for the Sutter–Allina combination

Minnesota's attorney general completed review of the transaction after obtaining a $2.15 billion five-year investment commitment, five-year protections for core rural services and specified reproductive, gender, charity-care, Medicare and Medicaid participation, and ten years of reporting and oversight. The agreement allows the integration to proceed; it does not guarantee that every service or location will remain unchanged beyond the stated commitments.

[Minnesota attorney general announcement](https://ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/11/16_Sutter-Allina.asp) · [Transaction FAQ](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Health-Care/Transactions/Sutter-Allina_FAQ.asp)

### CMS estimates a 7.32% Original Medicare improper-payment rate for FY 2026

CMS reported $33.96 billion in estimated Original Medicare improper payments, up from 6.55% and $28.83 billion in FY 2025. An improper payment is not necessarily fraud: it can reflect missing documentation, coding or eligibility mistakes, overpayments or underpayments. The measure identifies program-control problems but should not be described as a fraud total.

[Official CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/fiscal-year-2026-improper-payments)


## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### Giredestrant combination nearly doubles median progression-free survival in the approval population

The approval covers a biomarker-defined group after recurrence or progression on endocrine-based therapy. In the randomized open-label evERA trial, the ESR1-mutated subgroup had a progression-free-survival hazard ratio of 0.38; objective response was 27% versus 14%. Common adverse reactions included stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, pneumonitis and nausea. Longer follow-up is needed because overall survival was not mature.

[Official FDA approval and trial details](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-giredestrant-combination-everolimus-er-positive-her2-negative-esr1-mutated-locally)

### New Free Journal Articles

### [Preliminary U.S. surveillance report finds serious maternal and infant outcomes with measles](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/sites/default/files/page-files/PHA-Measles%20in%20pregancy111626.pdf)

NEJM Evidence published a preliminary descriptive public-health surveillance analysis of 112 confirmed measles cases in pregnant women across 16 U.S. jurisdictions from January 2025 through September 28, 2026. Among 21 pregnancies with outcomes within 14 days of rash onset, 19 ended in live births, eight were preterm and eight infants required intensive care; two pregnancies ended in loss, and 13 congenital-measles cases were reported. Ninety-eight percent of the women were unvaccinated or had unknown vaccination status. Why it matters: the report documents preventable maternal, obstetric and neonatal risk during the current resurgence, but it does not establish comparative risk or causation. Major limitations include preliminary and incomplete outcome reporting, missing longer follow-up, a small outcome subset and no uninfected comparison group. Published online November 16, 2026.
