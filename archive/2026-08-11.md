## 1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

### 1. The White House narrowed federal childhood-vaccine recommendations

President Trump signed an executive order Monday recommending routine immunization against 11 diseases rather than 18, moving several vaccines to shared clinical decision-making and calling for the combined MMR shot to be separated across different visits. WHO said Tuesday that vaccine timing is based on extensive review of disease risk and immune-system development; major medical groups warned that spacing shots can leave children unprotected longer. **Why it matters:** federal agencies and states are being urged to change guidance that affects pediatric care and school-vaccine policy. [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-delivers-gold-standard-childhood-vaccine-recommendations-for-americans/) · [WHO response via Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/who-defends-immunisation-schedules-after-trump-orders-fewer-childhood-shots-2026-08-11/) · [Readable AP report](https://apnews.com/article/72f1ac63ddceb542775aba556b45c701)

### 2. Congo's Ebola death toll passed 2,000

Government data showed 4,381 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths in the Bundibugyo-virus outbreak. The first 1,000 deaths took about nine weeks; the second 1,000 took roughly three weeks, making this the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record. **Why it matters:** conflict, bad roads and unpaid health workers are slowing a response to a virus for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/congo-ebola-outbreak-2316ac0838e4383ea443584adc267136) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/ebola-deaths-surpass-2000-democratic-republic-congo-2026-08-11/)

### 3. A Houthi missile attack killed four people in the Bab el-Mandeb

Yemeni authorities said Houthi forces fired three ballistic missiles at a commercial vessel, killing three Pakistanis and an Indonesian. They were the first known shipping deaths in the latest round of Yemen fighting. **Why it matters:** with the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, the Bab el-Mandeb is an essential alternative route for oil and other trade; attacks there could deepen the global shipping and energy shock. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-strait-hormuz-august-11-2026-91e4efdfe1ac035b2065127550377289)

### 4. Russian attacks killed at least 10 civilians in Ukraine

Missiles, drones and glide bombs hit Ukrainian cities Tuesday. Officials said seven people were killed and 24 wounded in Zaporizhzhia, while three more—including a 15-year-old—were killed in Dnipropetrovsk. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said North Korean ballistic missiles were used and Pyongyang is preparing to send more troops to Russia. **Why it matters:** the attacks show both immediate civilian harm and the widening military partnership sustaining Russia's war. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-north-korea-troops-missiles-7aff38d17dd195e63b7c55fc5a008c22)

### 5. Colombia raised the earthquake death toll to at least 111

Rescue crews continued searching after the magnitude-7.4 earthquake in western Colombia; the president said he would declare a national emergency. **Why it matters:** this is a material escalation from Monday's early damage reports, and remote communities may still be difficult to reach. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/colombia-ecuador-earthquake-26fd40f93272d834fced47a4a673edc9) · [Reuters rescue video](https://www.reuters.com/video/watch/idRW454211082026RP1/)

## 6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

### Genetic sequencing points to a new animal-to-human origin for the Ebola outbreak

A study of the Bundibugyo-virus genome found a previously unrecorded variant, supporting a new spillover from animals rather than continued circulation from an earlier human outbreak. This is surveillance evidence, not a treatment result, but it changes how investigators understand the outbreak's origin. [Reuters study report — access may be limited](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/new-transmission-animals-likely-caused-congo-uganda-ebola-outbreak-study-says-2026-08-11/)

### New Free Journal Articles

#### Translational reading frame predicts the pathogenicity of C-terminal frameshift deletions in MeCP2

**Journal and date:** *eLife*, online August 10, 2026. **Evidence label:** Reviewed preprint; human variant databases, engineered cultured cells and knock-in mouse models. The researchers found that a short protein-ending motif created by one reading frame sharply reduced MeCP2 levels and separated pathogenic Rett-syndrome deletions from apparently benign deletions. In mice, changing the stop codon restored protein expression and Rett-like features; base editing also worked in cultured cells. **Why it matters:** C-terminal MECP2 deletions account for about 10% of Rett-causing mutations, so the work may improve variant interpretation and supplies an early proof of concept for a shared editing strategy. **Limitation:** this is mainly mechanistic animal and cell evidence, not a human treatment trial; safety, delivery and durability remain unknown. [Free full text and peer review](https://elifesciences.org/articles/109170) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42573087/)
