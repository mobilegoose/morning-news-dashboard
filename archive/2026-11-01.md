### Russian strikes cut power to hundreds of thousands in Ukraine

Russian attacks hit power infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions overnight, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Sunday, while Ukraine launched large drone attacks into Russia and officials there reported major damage at an industrial site in Nizhny Novgorod. ### Judge dismisses Trump's $10 billion Wall Street Journal lawsuit with prejudice

U.S. District Judge Darrin Gayles dismissed President Donald Trump’s amended defamation complaint over the Journal’s reporting about a purported birthday letter to Jeffrey Epstein. Dismissal with prejudice ends this district-court case rather than inviting another amended complaint; an appeal remains possible. The ruling found the complaint had not plausibly alleged that the Journal knew the reported letter was false or seriously doubted it.

### Alaska asks a judge to reject DOJ’s conditional certification block

Alaska’s Republican-led government urged a federal judge Saturday to dismiss the Justice Department’s request to stop certification if Democrat Mary Peltola wins the Senate race. DOJ says the presence of a second candidate with a name similar to Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan could confuse voters; Alaska says the requested remedy is legally unsupported and practically impossible. This is a dismissal filing, not a ruling, and no certification block has been entered.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/5b975dc87222858f6239e9170d675698) · [Alaska Public Media explainer](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-27/state-of-alaska-says-doj-lawsuit-over-senate-race-has-no-legal-basis)

### Analysis finds major mismatches in DOJ’s California voter list

Two UCLA data scientists told a federal court that 8,880 of the 20,930 names DOJ flagged as possible noncitizens did not match California’s current voter roll. Their filing also identified at least 22 U.S. citizens and said about 7,500 entries appeared to be naturalized citizens. DOJ disputes the challengers’ conclusions. The analysis raises immediate error-risk concerns but does not itself determine any person’s eligibility or resolve the lawsuit.

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Trump’s Wall Street Journal suit is dismissed with prejudice

The president sued over a report that he contributed a suggestive letter to Jeffrey Epstein’s 50th-birthday album. Judge Gayles concluded that the amended complaint still did not adequately plead actual malice—the demanding fault standard a public figure must meet—and dismissed it with prejudice. That ends the case at the trial-court level; it is not a finding that every factual assertion in the article was independently proven, and Trump may appeal.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/f27c9fa832044f2f9edea9b1fbbba061)

### Alaska says DOJ’s requested post-election remedy has no legal basis

DOJ is asking the court to withhold certification only if the incumbent Republican loses closely, pending inquiry into whether a similarly named candidate confused voters. Alaska and intervenors argue federal law supplies no such conditional remedy and that ballots cannot be retroactively separated by voter intent. The judge has not ruled; the operative development is the state’s Saturday dismissal request.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/5b975dc87222858f6239e9170d675698) · [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/news/politics/elections/2026-10-27/state-of-alaska-says-doj-lawsuit-over-senate-race-has-no-legal-basis)

## Policy & Benefits

### HHS excludes some gender dysphoria from its Section 504 disability definition

HHS finalized a rule stating that gender dysphoria not resulting from a physical impairment is excluded from disability coverage under the agency’s Section 504 regulations. It applies to programs and activities receiving HHS funds. The change narrows one disability-discrimination route in those settings; it does not erase every possible protection under other federal, state or local law, and future litigation may test HHS’s interpretation.

[HHS final-rule announcement](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-finalizes-section-504-rule-gender-dysphoria-disability.html) · [Federal Register rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-22380.pdf)

### FDA approves revised warnings for three more estradiol patches

FDA approved updated labeling for three additional menopausal-hormone-therapy products, removing boxed-warning statements about cardiovascular disease, breast cancer and probable dementia from these estradiol transdermal patches. HHS also reported supply-expansion work and said it saw no imminent nationwide shortage. A label revision is not a finding that hormone therapy is risk-free or appropriate for everyone; product-specific prescribing information and individual risks still matter.

[HHS/FDA announcement](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/fda-advances-menopause-care-hormone-therapy-labeling-access.html) · [FDA updated-prescribing-information list](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/menopausal-hormone-therapies-updated-prescribing-information)


## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### FDA gives accelerated approval to the first plasmid-DNA immunotherapy

FDA approved Voceryq for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, an HPV-driven disease that can require repeated airway surgery. The evidence came from an open-label multicenter trial in 32 adults; among 26 with at least three baseline surgeries, seven required no surgical intervention in the first treatment year. Approval rests on reduced surgery frequency as an intermediate endpoint, so the manufacturer must verify clinical benefit in post-approval trials.

[FDA approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-plasmid-dna-based-immunotherapy-adults-recurrent-respiratory-papillomatosis) · [FDA product page](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/cellular-gene-therapy-products/voceryq)

### New Free Journal Articles

### NIH funding for transgender and nonbinary youth research remained a tiny share of the portfolio

JAMA Network Open published a peer-reviewed cross-sectional analysis of NIH awards from 2012–2024. Researchers identified 97 unique awards plus 42 supplements totaling $158.3 million—0.06% of NIH funding; 22 awards concerned gender-affirming medical care and none focused on surgery. The study maps investment rather than measuring health outcomes or establishing the optimal funding level. Keyword and RePORTER methods can miss or misclassify grants, and population-proportional comparisons are benchmarks, not causal evidence. Published online October 30, 2026.

[Free full text — JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2856026) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41377)

### Risk-reducing mastectomy is associated with lower mortality in BRCA1/2 carriers—but the evidence is observational

A peer-reviewed network meta-analysis in JAMA Network Open synthesized five nonrandomized studies, with individual sample sizes from 247 to 2,857. Risk-reducing mastectomy was associated with lower all-cause mortality than MRI-based screening after accounting for salpingo-oophorectomy; the breast-cancer-specific association was strongest for BRCA1. This supports shared decision-making, not a universal surgical recommendation. Residual confounding, immortal-time bias, indirect comparisons, no closed network loop and inability to assess publication bias limit causal conclusions. Published online October 30, 2026.

[Free full text — JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2856030) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.41231)
