Top 5 Things to Know Today

Broad public importance

1 · SUPREME COURT & IMMIGRATION

Justices let rapid third-country deportations resume and take the case for December

In Department of Homeland Security v. D.V.D., the administration asked to lift a lower-court judgment requiring notice and a meaningful opportunity to raise torture or persecution fears before removal to a country other than a person’s homeland. The Court granted a stay, so those protections are paused while the case proceeds, and treated the application as a petition for certiorari, granting review on the policy’s legality, jurisdiction and scope of relief. That is not a merits ruling. Arguments are set for December; Justices Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson would have denied the stay.

2 · CONGRESSIONAL SPENDING POWER

GAO says the White House cannot run out the clock on more than $800 million

GAO concluded that a September 25 rescission proposal covering 11 appropriations accounts does not authorize the president to keep the money frozen past today’s fiscal-year deadline. Under the Impoundment Control Act, funds must remain available unless Congress enacts a rescission; letting them expire would bypass Congress’s power of the purse. The accounts include refugee and unaccompanied-minor services, migrant education, housing counseling and civil-rights programs.

GAO decisionAssociated Press

3 · UKRAINE

Russian barrage kills seven and strikes Ukraine’s power grid

A large overnight missile-and-drone attack killed seven civilians, including a child, and wounded 24, Ukrainian officials said. Energy sites and a key internet exchange point were hit, causing outages. The scale of the combined assault—the heaviest against the grid since the last heating season—raises concern that Russia is beginning another winter campaign aimed at heat, electricity and water systems.

Associated Press

4 · MEDICAID ACCESS

Coverage losses begin tomorrow for many lawfully present immigrants

A 2025 federal law narrows the immigration categories eligible for federally matched Medicaid beginning October 1. State data collected by KFF Health News identify more than 281,000 people at risk in nine states and Washington, D.C., including refugees, asylees and trafficking survivors; green-card holders, children and several specified groups remain exempt. Emergency Medicaid remains available, and some states are using their own funds for affected residents.

5 · U.S. ECONOMY

Second-quarter growth is revised up to 2.2%

The Commerce Department’s third estimate raised annualized GDP growth from 1.5% to 2.2%, down from 2.5% in the first quarter. Consumer spending grew 3.8% and non-housing business investment 9%, while a 12.6% rise in imports subtracted nearly 1.7 percentage points. The revision shows more momentum than previously reported, but also greater reliance on AI-related investment and spending by wealthier households.

MOST USEFUL · MEDICAID

If a termination notice looks wrong, respond immediately and use appeal rights

CMS directed states to update eligibility systems, verify status and notify affected enrollees. People who receive an October termination should read the notice for the exact reason and deadline, promptly provide any missing proof of status, and request a fair hearing if the determination is wrong; some notices allow benefits to continue when an appeal is filed quickly. Children, pregnant people covered under the lawful-residence option, green-card holders and several other categories may remain eligible. State Medicaid offices make individual decisions.

Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning court check included

SUPREME COURT · DHS v. D.V.D.

What the Court did—and did not decide

The class action asks whether DHS may rapidly remove people with final deportation orders to unfamiliar third countries without enough notice to seek protection under due-process, immigration and anti-torture law. DHS sought emergency relief from a First Circuit-backed judgment. The Court granted a stay, temporarily suspending that judgment, and granted certiorari, agreeing to decide the merits. It did not hold the policy lawful. December argument will also address jurisdiction and classwide relief; the stay lasts until final judgment.

MEDICAID & IMMIGRATION

Federal eligibility narrowing reaches the first coverage cutoff

Starting October 1, federally matched full Medicaid generally remains available to citizens, U.S. nationals, lawful permanent residents and a shorter statutory list of noncitizens; emergency treatment remains federally matchable. State estimates suggest immediate losses may exceed early national assumptions. People should not infer eligibility from a headline—state notices, exceptions and state-funded coverage differ.

FOOD SAFETY

Nationwide raw-milk cheese recall expands with E. coli warning

FDA published the recall of all Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk medium cheddar, sharp cheddar, jalapeño jack and Monterey jack because of possible Shiga toxin-producing E. coli O26:H11. NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLES · CANCER & REHABILITATION

Home-based prehabilitation before esophagectomy was feasible—but efficacy remains unproved

Langenbeck’s Archives of Surgery · online September 29, 2026 · peer-reviewed prospective single-center feasibility study with matched historical controls. Twenty-six adults age 65 or older entered a four-week endurance, respiratory, resistance and balance program with geriatric assessment and medication review; 22 completed it and were compared with 42 historical controls. Overall complications, leakage, mortality and length of stay did not differ significantly. ICU readmission was lower before adjustment (4.5% vs. 26.2%) but not after adjustment (adjusted OR 0.30, p=.172). Why it matters: the program may be workable during cancer treatment and targets function, frailty and medication risk. Limitations: small, single-center and nonrandomized; controls differed at baseline, adherence records were incomplete, and the study was not powered to prove benefit.
