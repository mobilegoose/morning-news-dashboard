**Monday, November 16, 2026 · Updated after 10:45 a.m. Eastern**

A concise, source-linked briefing focused on concrete developments, public impact and practical relevance.

## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Court leaves $59 million in University of California research grants protected

The Supreme Court denied the administration’s petition in Trump v. Thakur, leaving in place a preliminary injunction that protects about $59 million in National Science Foundation and National Endowment for the Humanities grants. UC researchers say the cancellations targeted projects because they addressed diversity or gender; the government argued it may consider the viewpoints advanced by competitive grants. A certiorari denial is not a merits ruling or a nationwide endorsement of the Ninth Circuit’s reasoning, but the lower-court protection remains operative while the case proceeds.

[Official Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-210.html) · [Readable Reuters companion](https://wsau.com/2026/11/16/supreme-court-rejects-trump-appeal-defending-diversity-related-grant-cuts/) · [DOJ petition](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/trump-v-thakur)

### EU adds 113 people and entities to its Russia sanctions list

The European Union imposed asset freezes and funding prohibitions on 113 additional people and entities tied to Russia’s war, including nearly 50 banks and financial institutions plus energy, oil-trading and disinformation actors. The Council said the listings are intended to restrict Russia’s ability to finance the invasion as attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure continue before winter.

[Official Council of the EU release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/11/16/ukraine-eu-lists-113-individuals-and-entities-to-further-restrict-russia-s-ability-to-finance-its-war-of-aggression/) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/cde686fba0e452b10ca613b3517e884e)

### United States closes a remote-compute route to restricted advanced chips

The Commerce Department issued an interim final rule extending export controls to overseas data centers that remotely provide large amounts of advanced computing to companies based in China, Russia, Iran, North Korea and other restricted jurisdictions. It also adds licensing rules for certain advanced-chip transfers to data centers in Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil. The rule includes customer due-diligence duties and an exception for commercial recommendation systems.

[Official BIS announcement](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/bis-issues-rule-addressing-remote-access-american-super-intelligence-technology) · [Official interim final rule](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/rin-0694-ak50.pdf)

### OpenAI releases healthcare computer-use agents and more than 20 plugins

OpenAI made computer-use agents available for healthcare workflows in ChatGPT for Healthcare, ChatGPT for Clinicians and the API, alongside improved clinical search and more than 20 healthcare plugins. The agents can navigate existing software for tasks such as prior authorization and referrals. OpenAI reports high safety and helpfulness ratings on internal evaluations, but those vendor-reported results are not evidence of improved patient outcomes; deployments still require human review, access controls, audit logs and an applicable BAA for protected health information.

[Official OpenAI product announcement](https://openai.com/index/more-ways-to-move-care-forward-with-chatgpt-for-healthcare/) · [Technical and safety details](https://openai.com/index/advancing-computer-use-for-healthcare-teams/)

### House Democrats request a GAO audit of the FAA’s new SMART system

After Friday’s nationwide ground stop, House Democrats asked the Government Accountability Office to review the FAA’s $875 million SMART modernization system, citing limited controller input, training concerns and the risks of AI errors. The FAA and developer Peraton say SMART did not cause the outage and that human controllers remain responsible for decisions. The audit request is a concrete oversight action, not a finding that SMART caused the failure.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/08af345749c81fd8364299cd75275fa6)


## Most Useful Development Today

### What today’s grant-funding denial does—and does not—settle

Trump v. Thakur remains in the lower courts. The Supreme Court declined review at the preliminary-injunction stage, so the protected grants remain in place under the Ninth Circuit’s order; the justices did not decide whether the First Amendment ultimately bars viewpoint-based termination of competitive research grants. For researchers and advocates, the practical step is to track the district-court merits proceedings rather than cite the denial as Supreme Court precedent.

[Official Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-210.html) · [Official November 16 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/111626zor_cb7d.pdf)

## Supreme Court & Legal

### Lower-court protection for UC research grants remains in force

University of California researchers challenged federal termination of projects addressing subjects such as racial equity in science education and opportunities for Indigenous students. The administration asked the Supreme Court to decide whether government may consider a project’s viewpoint when awarding or ending competitive grants. The Court denied certiorari without explanation. That leaves the preliminary injunction and Ninth Circuit ruling in place but creates no Supreme Court precedent and does not finally resolve the merits.

[Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-210.html) · [DOJ petition](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/trump-v-thakur) · [Readable Reuters companion](https://wsau.com/2026/11/16/supreme-court-rejects-trump-appeal-defending-diversity-related-grant-cuts/)

### Court declines challenge to California’s implicit-bias CME requirement

Physicians and an instructor argued that California unconstitutionally compels private continuing-medical-education speakers to address how implicit bias affects healthcare outcomes; state officials defended the content as part of a regulated licensing program. The Court denied certiorari, leaving the Ninth Circuit’s government-speech ruling intact without adopting it nationwide. Justice Gorsuch would have granted review; Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Alito, dissented. The litigation ends at the Supreme Court unless another procedural route remains below.

[Official order and dissent](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/111626zor_cb7d.pdf) · [Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1240.html)

### Court sends two cases back for reconsideration under new precedents

In Casarez v. Irigoyen Farms, a family sued Walmart and a produce supplier after a fatal trucking crash, disputing whether federal trucking law preempts state negligence and wrongful-death claims. The Court granted review, vacated the California judgment and remanded for reconsideration under Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II; that GVR is not a merits win for either side. In Huffman v. United States, a federal defendant argued that his written sentence was more severe than the sentence pronounced in court despite an appeal waiver. The Court likewise granted, vacated and remanded for reconsideration under Hunter v. United States, so the Fifth Circuit must apply that newer sentencing precedent.

[Official November 16 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/111626zor_cb7d.pdf) · [Casarez official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1421.html) · [Casarez lower-court opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2025/f086901.html) · [Huffman petition appendix](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-5528/423349/20260908135818697_Appendix_FINAL.pdf)

### Court seeks U.S. views on terrorism victims’ attempt to reach central-bank reserves

Families holding judgments against the Taliban for the September 11 and U.S.-embassy attacks seek access to roughly $3.5 billion in Afghan central-bank assets held in New York; the Second Circuit held that the reserves cannot be used to satisfy those judgments. In Havlish v. Taliban, Ashton v. Taliban and James Owens v. Taliban, the Supreme Court invited the Solicitor General to file briefs expressing the United States’ views. A CVSG is not a certiorari grant or a ruling on ownership; it pauses the review decision while the government prepares its position. Justice Kagan recused from the first two petitions because of prior government service.

[Official November 16 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/111626zor_cb7d.pdf) · [Havlish official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-292.html) · [Case-specific frozen-assets explainer](https://ccrjustice.org/home/what-we-do/our-cases/havlish-v-taliban-re-911-attacks-owens-v-taliban-frozen-afghan-assets)

## Policy & Benefits

### CMS formally extends nationwide home-health and hospice enrollment moratoria

CMS extended for six months its freeze on initial Medicare enrollment of new home health agencies and hospices, effective November 13, while modifying the policy so currently enrolled providers may add practice locations or branches. CMS says the moratoria target fraud risk and have not materially reduced beneficiary access. The notices do not suspend existing beneficiaries’ home-health or hospice benefits and leave states to decide whether comparable Medicaid or CHIP moratoria are appropriate.

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### CDC and FDA close the raw-milk-cheese E. coli outbreak investigation

FDA’s November 13 update says the multistate E. coli O26:H11 outbreak linked to Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk cheese is over. Officials counted 18 illnesses in 10 states and nine hospitalizations, with no deaths; all 15 interviewed patients reported eating the cheese, and samples from homes matched the outbreak strain by whole-genome sequencing. Ending the investigation does not make recalled or contaminated product safe, and consumers should still follow the product-specific notice.

[Official FDA investigation update](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o26h11-raw-milk-cheese-september-2026)

The release lets governed healthcare workspaces use agents that navigate EHRs, payer portals and other software, with configurable approvals and audit logs. OpenAI says individual verified U.S. clinicians can access ChatGPT for Clinicians for free. ### BIS applies advanced-chip controls to remote data-center access

The interim final rule targets indirect access to restricted U.S. computing through third-country data centers, requires ownership and customer due diligence, and adds licensing requirements for specified destinations. It is a binding regulatory action rather than a proposed policy; affected existing data-center operators receive a transition period described in the rule.

[Official BIS announcement](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/bis-issues-rule-addressing-remote-access-american-super-intelligence-technology) · [Official rule text](https://www.bis.gov/media/documents/rin-0694-ak50.pdf)

### GitHub retires three Copilot models today

GitHub is deprecating Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Terra across Copilot chat, inline edits, ask and agent modes, and code completions. GitHub recommends Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol or GPT-6 Luna depending on the model being replaced. Teams with model policies or pinned workflows should update them; eligible annual individual subscribers retain a limited Opus 4.8 exception.

[Official GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-15-upcoming-deprecation-of-selected-github-copilot-models-in-mid-november/)

### GAO asked to examine FAA’s new SMART modernization system

The congressional request focuses on testing, controller involvement, training and potential AI hallucinations after a national outage. FAA and Peraton deny that SMART caused Friday’s ground stop, and SMART aggregates data for human controllers rather than independently directing aircraft. An audit could produce findings later; today’s development is the formal request.
