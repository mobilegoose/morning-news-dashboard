1 · Trade and prices

Twenty-five states sue over the administration’s replacement global tariffs

The coalition filed in the U.S. Court of International Trade, challenging 10% and 12.5% duties on imports from roughly 60 trading partners. The states say the forced-labor rationale is a pretext for rebuilding the tariff wall the Supreme Court invalidated in February; the administration says Section 301 of the Trade Act authorizes the new action.

2. Most Useful Development Today

Medicare rule change

Hospitals and medical devices

CMS finalizes fiscal 2027 hospital payment policy—and a tougher test for breakthrough-device add-ons

CMS issued its FY 2027 inpatient-hospital final rule. Beginning in FY 2028, the rule generally ends the alternative pathway that allowed FDA-designated Breakthrough Devices to seek extra Medicare hospital payments without separately demonstrating substantial clinical improvement.

Practical meaningThis does not remove FDA clearance or Medicare coverage. It changes eligibility for temporary add-on payments, so hospitals, device makers and patient advocates should distinguish payment incentives from coverage decisions.

4. Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning check completed

Emergency application · 26A141

Palestine Liberation Organization, et al. v. Sokolow, et al.

Americans killed or injured in six attacks in and around Jerusalem from 2002 to 2004 sued the PLO and Palestinian Authority under the Anti-Terrorism Act. A jury awarded $218.5 million, automatically trebled to $655.5 million; after years of jurisdiction litigation and a 2025 Supreme Court ruling that revived the claims, the Second Circuit reinstated the judgment in March.

The Palestinian entities asked Justice Sotomayor to stay enforcement while they file a petition for certiorari, arguing immediate collection could impair West Bank government services and that the old judgment could not lawfully be revived. Sotomayor denied the emergency stay on August 3 without explanation and did not refer it to the full Court. This is not a merits ruling or a certiorari decision: enforcement may continue, and a later cert petition can still be filed.

What happens nextThe plaintiffs may pursue collection while the applicants seek ordinary Supreme Court review. An unexplained stay denial does not establish a new legal rule.
