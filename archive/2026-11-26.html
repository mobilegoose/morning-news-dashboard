1 · MIDDLE EAST · REGIONAL SECURITY

Iran-linked escalation reaches Kuwait and Gulf shipping lanes

Kuwait said it intercepted Iranian drones and that falling debris killed two Italian air-defense technicians and injured two other people. Separately, a British maritime monitor reported two tankers struck by unknown projectiles near Oman and west of Dubai, while overnight Israeli strikes killed 10 Palestinians in Gaza and Houthi media reported strikes on Sanaa. 2 · UKRAINE · ENERGY AND CIVILIANS

Overnight strikes hit 20 Ukrainian locations as both sides trade large drone attacks

Ukraine said Russia launched ballistic missiles and 113 drones overnight, with hits at 20 locations that damaged an apartment building, a hospital, businesses and energy infrastructure. Russia said it intercepted 343 Ukrainian drones, while Ukraine reported striking a Russian military airfield and oil-pumping facility. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy also said intelligence indicated preparations for a larger Russian energy-grid attack; that warning is not itself a completed strike.

Associated Press

3 · CONSTITUTION · IMMIGRATION · PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

Federal judge blocks the administration’s second birthright-citizenship order

In Nathan v. Trump, parents and U.S.-born children challenged an August order that would deny citizenship to several categories of children—including some born to foreign-government employees without diplomatic immunity and some linked to alleged birth-tourism arrangements. Judge Christopher Cooper certified a child-only class and preliminarily enjoined DHS, State and SSA from enforcing the order against class members, finding they are likely to succeed under the Fourteenth Amendment and 8 U.S.C. § 1401. This is interim relief, not a final merits judgment; the government may appeal.

4 · FIRST AMENDMENT · MEDIA REGULATION · PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

Judge halts the FCC’s early review of eight ABC station licenses

ABC, Disney and eight owned stations alleged that the FCC accelerated license-renewal review to retaliate for editorial and programming decisions. Judge Loren AliKhan denied the government’s dismissal motion and issued a preliminary injunction, finding ABC likely to succeed on its First Amendment retaliation claim and describing the chilling effect as irreparable harm. The order pauses the early review while litigation continues; it does not finally resolve the stations’ licenses or the merits.

Full district-court opinionAssociated Press

5 · MEDICARE · IMMIGRATION STATUS · ACTION DEADLINE

SSA notices put some noncitizens’ Medicare coverage on a February 1 termination track

SSA is mailing notices under the new Medicare eligibility limits in Public Law 119-21. Coverage for people found ineligible generally ends February 1, 2027; people incorrectly classified can upload citizenship or immigration documents and may file a written appeal within 60 days of the decision letter. Reporting indicates roughly 300,000 lawfully present beneficiaries—including older and disabled people and some with end-stage renal disease—may be affected. The official notice controls each recipient’s deadline.

SURVEILLANCE LAW · DRONES · OLC OPINION

DOJ issues legal guidance on law-enforcement drone-detection systems

The Justice Department’s Office of Legal Counsel concluded that acoustic, optical, infrared and radar detection generally do not implicate the Pen Register Act or Wiretap Act, and that receiving FAA-required remote-identification broadcasts generally is lawful. Radiofrequency interception of other drone communications remains technology- and communication-specific. The opinion guides government agencies; it does not authorize every counter-drone interception or displace constitutional limits.

Official OLC opinion pageDOJ summary

IMMIGRATION DETENTION · MATERNAL HEALTH · COMPLAINT FILED

Organizations sue to restore ICE protections for pregnant and nursing immigrants

Six organizations filed Kennedy Human Rights Center v. DHS in D.C. federal court, alleging ICE abandoned a 2021 directive that presumptively limits detention and GPS shackling of pregnant, postpartum and nursing people. The complaint cites ICE records showing sharply increased detention and alleges inadequate care; plaintiffs seek APA relief requiring compliance with the directive. These are allegations at the start of the case, not judicial findings.

RECENT FALLBACK · HOUSING · IMMIGRATION · NOVEMBER 23

States and housing authorities challenge HUD’s mixed-status-family rule

Two coordinated federal suits challenge HUD’s final rule ending prorated assistance for mixed-status households and requiring every member to verify eligible status. Plaintiffs argue Congress expressly permits prorated aid and that HUD exceeded its authority; HUD says the rule implements Section 214. The rule is scheduled to take effect December 14, but neither complaint itself suspends it—court-ordered relief would be required.

Immediate device warnings and new evidence

FDA SAFETY ALERT · INSULIN PUMPS

FDA classifies Tandem Mobi insulin-pump action as its most serious recall type

FDA said certain Tandem Mobi pumps can generate false-positive occlusion alarms and suspend insulin delivery until the alert is acknowledged. Tandem reported 1,248 serious injuries and no deaths as of October 28. Users should check the pump serial-number list, request replacement when directed, respond to every alarm, keep app notifications enabled and maintain a backup insulin-delivery method under their care plan.

Official FDA recall and instructions

FDA EARLY ALERT · NEONATAL AIRWAY DEVICES

FDA warns hospitals to remove affected Portex neonatal endotracheal tubes

ICU Medical identified affected lots made with resin that may be softer than expected, creating risks of kinking, resistance to ventilation and delayed intubation in neonates and young infants. One death had been reported as of November 16. FDA says facilities should immediately discontinue use and destroy or quarantine affected lots while its review continues.

PEER-REVIEWED PROOF-OF-CONCEPT CASE SERIES · ONLINE NOVEMBER 25

Non-invasive muscle recordings enabled three people with cervical spinal-cord injury to control virtual devices

Nature Human Behaviour. Three men with C1–C4 spinal-cord injuries and no functional hand use learned to control individual motor units detected by high-density surface EMG; control transferred to two-dimensional games and, in one participant, virtual-wheelchair navigation. The result matters because it suggests a non-surgical assistive interface that can use faint residual signals despite spasticity. It is very early evidence: only three participants were studied, two performed the three-signal task, and no physical wheelchair or restored hand movement was tested.

PEER-REVIEWED PROSPECTIVE OBSERVATIONAL STUDY · INDEXED NOVEMBER 26

Six-month study finds cognitive deficits can persist after apparently milder hemorrhagic stroke

Frontiers in Neurology. Researchers followed 74 adults with hemorrhagic stroke and preserved consciousness at baseline and at 1, 3 and 6 months. Average cognitive scores improved, but 25.7% had an unfavorable cognitive or functional outcome at six months; older age, less education and prior stroke were associated with worse outcomes. The study supports early cognitive screening even when consciousness is preserved, but the single modest cohort and exploratory models cannot establish causation or broad prediction rules.

PEER-REVIEWED RETROSPECTIVE MULTICENTER STUDY · INDEXED NOVEMBER 26

Real-world pediatric cohort reports strong larotrectinib response concentrated in infantile fibrosarcoma

Frontiers in Oncology. Fifteen Turkish centers reported 21 children with extracranial NTRK-fusion tumors treated with larotrectinib. Fourteen of 15 with infantile fibrosarcoma responded, while five of six with other heavily pretreated tumors progressed and died; no treatment-limiting adverse events were reported. This supports molecular testing and disease-specific interpretation, but the retrospective, small, heterogeneous early-access cohort has no comparator and cannot establish effectiveness across all NTRK-positive tumors.

ALSO NEW · PROSPECTIVE LONGITUDINAL STUDY · INDEXED NOVEMBER 26

Also new: movement-fear trajectories after esophageal-cancer surgery

Frontiers in Psychology followed 320 patients after minimally invasive esophagectomy. About 19% had persistently high, slowly declining fear of movement; pain, fatigue, anxiety, depression, catastrophizing, lower support and lower resilience were associated with that trajectory. The findings are useful for rehabilitation screening, but come from one Chinese tertiary center and observational associations do not prove that changing those factors changes recovery.

Two nonduplicative legal recordings

RECENT FALLBACK · AUDIO ARGUENDO · NOVEMBER 24

“Lyman v. Henderson, No. 26-4014” — Tenth Circuit argument recording

Utah citizen Phil Lyman sued the lieutenant governor under the National Voter Registration Act seeking private or withheld voter-roll records. The district court dismissed for lack of Article III standing, and Lyman asked the Tenth Circuit to revive the case by recognizing a concrete informational injury under the statute; the court heard argument November 17 and has not decided the appeal. The recording is useful for voting-record access, private enforcement and federal standing doctrine.

RECENT FALLBACK · AUDIO ARGUENDO · NOVEMBER 24

“Ramirez Ovando v. Mullin, No. 26-1027” — Tenth Circuit argument recording

Four immigrants and a community group obtained a Colorado preliminary injunction requiring ICE officers to make the flight-risk finding federal law demands before warrantless arrests; later enforcement orders required training and records. The government appealed, and the Tenth Circuit heard argument November 16; no appellate decision has issued. The November 24 recording is useful for immigration enforcement, classwide preliminary relief and judicial supervision of agency compliance.
