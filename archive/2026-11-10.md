## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Supreme Court lets a Texas border-barrier project resume

In Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church, environmental and historic-preservation groups challenged DHS's waiver of federal laws for a Big Bend Sector barrier project. The district court stopped construction, but the Supreme Court granted the government's emergency stay late Monday, concluding that the government is very likely to show the lower court lacked jurisdiction because the challengers framed a statutory dispute as a constitutional one. Construction may resume while the government's certiorari petition proceeds; the Court did not finally decide the project's legality. Justice Jackson would have denied the stay.

[Official Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a560_onkq.pdf) · [Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26a560.html)

### U.S. again skips its U.N. human-rights review

The United States did not appear for Tuesday's Universal Periodic Review in Geneva. The Human Rights Council postponed the review until January rather than conduct it without the U.S. delegation. The process reviews every U.N. member's human-rights record and produces nonbinding recommendations; missing it for a second year leaves U.S. practices on migration, racial discrimination, capital punishment and reproductive rights without the scheduled peer review.

## Most Useful Development Today

### CMS awards the second $10 billion round of Rural Health Transformation funding

CMS announced Year 2 awards to every state under the five-year, $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program. Tennessee receives $210.3 million; nearby Virginia receives $195.7 million and North Carolina $216.8 million. States may use the money for chronic-disease prevention, workforce, behavioral health, technology-enabled care, payment reform and delivery-system changes. ## Supreme Court & Legal

### Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church: construction may continue while review proceeds

DHS waived environmental and preservation laws for a border barrier in Texas's Big Bend Sector. A river guide and two nonprofits—including one protecting a historic Ruidosa church—argued that the waiver exceeded statutory authority and raised a constitutional major-questions claim; a district judge issued a preliminary injunction. The Supreme Court granted the government's emergency stay, which suspends that injunction and permits work to resume while the government seeks certiorari. The stay ends if certiorari is denied or, if review is granted, when the Court enters judgment. This is provisional relief based principally on jurisdiction, not a merits ruling that every waiver or construction choice is lawful.

[Official per curiam opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/26pdf/26a560_onkq.pdf) · [Official case docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26a560.html)

## Policy & Benefits

### Stream Auto settlement would require advertised prices to reflect the real vehicle price

The FTC says Stream Auto Outlet advertised artificially low prices, then required thousands of dollars in down payments and added undisclosed fees or products. A proposed federal settlement would require the dealer's advertised price to be the actual price, excluding only government charges, and would restrict misrepresentations about financing and add-ons. The complaint states allegations, and the order becomes binding only if the federal judge approves it.

[Official FTC announcement](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/ftc-secures-settlement-stream-auto-outlet-advancing-price-transparency-auto-buying-consumers)

### Court temporarily halts an alleged phantom-debt operation

At the FTC's request, a federal court issued a temporary restraining order, froze assets and appointed a receiver over an alleged collection network. The FTC says consumers paid more than $20 million for debts they often did not owe after collectors used fake-law-firm identities and threatened suits, garnishment or liens. The order preserves the status quo; it is not a final liability finding, and the defendants may contest the allegations.

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### Extracellular-vesicle RNA test distinguishes precursor lesions in an early study

A peer-reviewed Nature Medicine study of 506 people evaluated an extracellular-vesicle RNA signature called Tu-stroma in blood. It distinguished isolated serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma—a precursor lesion—from controls and benign conditions with reported AUCs of 0.99, substantially higher than CA125 in those comparisons. The work is promising diagnostic research, not an available population-screening test: prospective consecutive real-world validation is still needed, and the study does not show that using the marker reduces ovarian-cancer mortality.

[Nature Medicine article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04732-9)

### New Free Journal Articles

### [Depression and loneliness differentially predict visual hallucinations in veterans with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13740365/)

Alzheimer's & Dementia: Diagnosis, Assessment & Disease Monitoring published a peer-reviewed cross-sectional study of 583 veterans evaluated at the VA Boston Memory Clinic from 2021 through September 2024. Logistic-regression models linked depression and loneliness—but not measured social isolation—to visual hallucinations in Alzheimer's disease and/or Lewy body dementia groups; the design cannot establish that either factor causes hallucinations. Why it matters: it identifies potentially modifiable clinical and social signals worth assessing alongside neurologic causes. Major limitations include reverse causation, complete-case selection, a brief neuropsychological battery and a sample that was almost entirely male and predominantly White, limiting generalizability.

### Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission, Inc. oral argument

A private natural-gas pipeline used the Natural Gas Act's eminent-domain authority against North Dakota landowners. The landowners ask whether federal just-compensation rules or state law require the pipeline to reimburse attorney's fees. The Court heard argument Monday; it has not ruled. The recording is useful for hearing how the justices approach federal-state choice of law and the practical cost of defending property rights. A merits decision will follow later in the term.
