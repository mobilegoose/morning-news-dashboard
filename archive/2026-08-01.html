Health policy · Observational cohort · n=281,620

Remote Patient Monitoring Adoption for Hypertension Management Among Medicare Beneficiaries

JAMA Network Open; online July 31, 2026. In matched Medicare data from 2016–2022, beneficiaries who switched from fee-for-service to Medicare Advantage had lower remote-monitoring adoption, more clinician loss and more hypertension-related acute care than matched beneficiaries remaining in fee-for-service. This is an association, not proof that plan switching caused the outcomes. Why it matters: it points to continuity and payment-policy gaps during Medicare Advantage transitions. Limitation: residual confounding and use of plan type as a proxy for value-based contracting may distort comparisons.

Free full text

Public health · Quasi-experimental nonrandomized trial · 1,667 blocks

Neighborhood Environmental Interventions and Opioid Overdose Rates

JAMA Network Open; online July 31, 2026. Across one Philadelphia neighborhood, combined interventions were associated with a 6.6% reduction in fatal overdoses; abandoned-house remediation was associated with lower fatal and nonfatal overdoses. Trash pickup showed no association, and vacant-lot cleanup was associated with more reported nonfatal overdoses. Why it matters: built-environment policy may complement treatment and harm reduction. Limitation: one urban neighborhood, nonrandomized assignment and possible unmeasured confounding limit causal claims and generalizability.

Free full text

Pediatrics safety · Retrospective cohort · n=107,505

Probiotic-Associated Invasive Infection Among Preterm Infants

JAMA Network Open; online July 31, 2026. Eleven infants developed invasive Lactobacillus or Bifidobacterium infection; supplementation was associated with an absolute increase of 0.04%, about one additional infection per 2,500 supplemented infants. The event was rare and must be weighed against known benefits. Why it matters: it quantifies a serious safety signal in a medically fragile population. Limitation: retrospective data and no bacterial genomic analysis mean the study cannot prove the probiotic product caused each infection.

Free full text
