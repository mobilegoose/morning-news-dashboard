1 · U.S. public health

The White House narrowed federal childhood-vaccine recommendations

President Trump signed an executive order Monday recommending routine immunization against 11 diseases rather than 18, moving several vaccines to shared clinical decision-making and calling for the combined MMR shot to be separated across different visits. WHO defended the scientific process behind vaccine timing Tuesday; medical groups warned that spacing shots leaves children unprotected longer.

Why it matters Federal agencies and states are being urged to change guidance affecting pediatric care and school-vaccine policy.

2 · Global health

Congo's Ebola death toll passed 2,000

Government data showed 4,381 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths. The first 1,000 deaths took about nine weeks; the second 1,000 took roughly three weeks, making this the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record.

Why it matters Conflict, bad roads and unpaid health workers are slowing the response; there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment for this virus species.

3 · Middle East

A Houthi missile attack killed four people in the Bab el-Mandeb

Yemeni authorities said Houthi forces fired three ballistic missiles at a commercial vessel, killing three Pakistanis and an Indonesian—the first known shipping deaths in the latest round of fighting.

Why it matters With Hormuz effectively closed, this is an essential alternative trade and oil route.

4 · Ukraine

Russian attacks killed at least 10 civilians

Missiles, drones and glide bombs hit Ukrainian cities Tuesday. Seven people were killed and 24 wounded in Zaporizhzhia; three more, including a 15-year-old, were killed in Dnipropetrovsk. President Zelenskyy said North Korean missiles were used and more North Korean troops are being prepared.

Why it matters The attacks show immediate civilian harm and a widening military partnership sustaining Russia's war.

Associated Press

Credit repair

A court halted a nearly $200 million credit-repair operation

At the FTC's request, a federal court temporarily stopped 17 related companies and their principals from operating an alleged scheme that charged consumers for credit-repair services and promised results the agency says it could not deliver.

Outbreak science and journal watch

Ebola origins

Sequencing points to a new animal-to-human spillover

A study found a previously unrecorded Bundibugyo-virus variant, supporting a new spillover rather than continued circulation from an earlier human outbreak. This is surveillance evidence, not a treatment result.

Reuters study report · access may be limited

New Free Journal Articles · Reviewed preprint

Translational reading frame predicts the pathogenicity of C-terminal frameshift deletions in MeCP2

eLife · online August 10, 2026. Using human variant databases, engineered cultured cells and knock-in mice, researchers found a short protein-ending motif that sharply reduced MeCP2 levels and separated pathogenic Rett-syndrome deletions from apparently benign deletions. Editing restored protein expression and Rett-like features in mice, and base editing worked in cultured cells.

Why it matters C-terminal MECP2 deletions account for about 10% of Rett-causing mutations, so the work may improve variant interpretation and provides an early shared editing strategy. Limitation: mechanistic animal and cell evidence—not a human treatment trial; safety, delivery and durability are unknown.
