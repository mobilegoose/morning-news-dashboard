## Top 5 Things to Know Today

### Russian missiles and drones kill at least nine and cut power across 12 Ukrainian regions

Russia launched another large overnight attack on civilian and energy infrastructure, while President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met U.S. and European officials in Norway seeking more air-defense support. Ukraine says Patriot interceptors are running short and intelligence and satellite-service disruptions are constraining its defense; those claims remain part of a fast-moving wartime account.

### Britain expands military support for Saudi Arabia as the Yemen camp death toll rises to 73

The United Kingdom said it will expand military support to help Saudi Arabia defend against Houthi attacks. In southern Yemen, authorities raised the death toll from a Houthi missile strike on a government training camp to 73. The linked changes mark both a new outside military commitment and a sharp casualty update in the regional conflict.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/mideast-yemen-saudi-israel-gaza-october-20-2026-6f69abd6470c9b43b75de7b223011486)

### Alberta voters overwhelmingly reject a path toward separation from Canada

With more than 1.8 million ballots counted, unofficial results showed about 76% voting to remain in Canada and 24% supporting a process that could have led to a later independence vote. The result strengthens Prime Minister Mark Carney's national-unity position, though separatist organizers say they will continue campaigning.

[Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/ed0d58ef051859ce12052e75d2d6e8a0)

### Justices agree to hear a challenge to Montgomery County's race-neutral magnet-school policy

In Association for Education Fairness v. Montgomery County Board of Education, mostly Asian American parents challenge a lottery and screening system for four Maryland middle-school magnets, arguing it was designed to reduce Asian American enrollment even though it does not expressly use race. The Court granted certiorari only on whether a plaintiff alleging intentional discrimination must also show aggregate disparate harm relative to the applicant pool. This is review of the legal standard—not a ruling that the policy is unconstitutional—and argument is expected later this term.

[Official docket 26-12](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-12.html) · [Readable Associated Press explainer](https://apnews.com/article/29dda0e3ad032cbc5e5777d5ce495474)

### Single-family construction falls 4.6%; permits reach a three-and-a-half-year low

September single-family housing starts fell to an annualized 859,000 units and permits dropped 4.0% to 845,000, according to federal data reported by Reuters. With mortgage rates averaging about 7.4%, high prices and financing costs are suppressing demand even as the country faces a long-running housing shortage. The figures are backward-looking monthly data, but they sharpen the current affordability and supply picture.

[Reuters — potentially limited](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-single-family-housing-starts-building-permits-tumble-september-2026-10-20/)

A rare drone attack blamed by a monitoring group on the Rapid Support Forces killed children ages two, four and six in Khartoum. Attribution is contested in an active civil war, but the civilian toll and widening use of long-range drones are clear material developments.

### A $450 million shortfall forces the U.N. food agency to cut assistance for 700,000 Palestinians

The World Food Program reduced assistance this month for 500,000 people in Gaza and 200,000 in the West Bank, its acting director said. The agency is prioritizing people who are sick, pregnant, nursing or malnourished while seeking enough funding to restore food parcels and hot meals for six months. ## Supreme Court & Legal

### The Court will decide what plaintiffs must show when a facially neutral admissions policy is challenged as intentionally discriminatory

Parents challenging Montgomery County's middle-school magnet system say school-poverty comparisons and a lottery were adopted to reduce Asian American representation; the district says the process is colorblind and expands equitable access. The Court granted review on one threshold evidentiary question after lower courts dismissed the case, so the policy remains in place and no merits judgment has been entered. The eventual ruling could change how discrimination claims against race-neutral school policies survive dismissal nationwide.

[Official docket 26-12](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-12.html) · [Official October 19 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/101926zor_q8l1.pdf)

### Justices ask the Solicitor General to weigh in on Iowa's blocked immigration-arrest law

Iowa's attorney general wants the Court to review a ruling blocking a state law that would let Iowa officers arrest and prosecute certain people believed to be in the country unlawfully; immigrant-rights groups argue immigration enforcement is principally federal. The Court invited the Solicitor General to file the federal government's views, a procedural step often called a CVSG. It is not a certiorari grant or a ruling on the law, and there is no fixed deadline for the brief.

[Official docket 26-49](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-49.html) · [Official order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/101926zor_q8l1.pdf) · [Reuters — potentially limited](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-supreme-court-seeks-governments-input-iowa-immigration-arrest-law-2026-10-19/)

### The Court declines to revive plea agreements in the 9/11 military-commission cases

Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and two co-defendants asked the justices to review whether the defense secretary validly withdrew 2024 agreements under which they would plead guilty and avoid the death penalty. The unexplained denial of certiorari leaves the D.C. Circuit's ruling and the withdrawal of the deals in place; it does not endorse the lower court's reasoning. The capital prosecution now proceeds toward a military trial scheduled for June 2028.

[Official October 19 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/101926zor_q8l1.pdf) · [Readable Associated Press report](https://apnews.com/article/824102b1eee6ced225e56d54ff230acb)

## Policy & Benefits

### White House orders a new NIH Women's Health Division and revokes the prior initiative

The order directs HHS to establish a Women's Health Division within an NIH institute or center within 90 days and creates a White House task force focused on translating research into prevention, diagnosis and care across the lifespan. It also revokes the 2023 initiative and 2024 executive order on women's health research. The order sets organizational direction; funding levels and measurable research outputs will depend on implementation and appropriations.

### Labor Department funds 20 rapid-reskilling projects across 17 states, including North Carolina

The department awarded Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act grants intended to move people affected by layoffs into paid work, apprenticeships or structured training within six months. North Carolina's Capital Area Workforce Development Board received about $5.8 million. Awards are concrete funding commitments, but their effectiveness will depend on enrollment, completion and job-placement results.

[Official Labor Department release and award table](https://www.dol.gov/index.php/newsroom/releases/eta/eta20261019)

### EEOC sues BNSF after an employee with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome sought a smaller-handled tool

The agency alleges BNSF refused a laborer's request to use a smaller-handled spike maul during training, offered no alternative accommodation and fired him, violating the Americans with Disabilities Act. EEOC filed in federal court and seeks damages and injunctive relief. These are allegations at the start of litigation, not findings of liability, but the case is a practical reminder that employers must engage in the accommodation process.

## Science, Medicine & Public Health

### FDA expands Jemperli use for a biomarker-defined group with locally advanced rectal cancer

FDA granted accelerated approval for dostarlimab alone in previously untreated stage II or III rectal cancer that is mismatch-repair deficient or microsatellite-instability high—about 3% of locally advanced cases. In the 154-patient phase 2 study supporting the action, nearly three-quarters had avoided surgery, radiation and chemotherapy after a median follow-up above two years. Longer follow-up is required to confirm durable benefit; this is not an option for most rectal cancers, and immune-related adverse reactions can be severe or fatal.

[Official FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-immunotherapy-may-allow-certain-patients-rectal-cancer-avoid-chemotherapy-radiation-and) · [Clinical trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05723562)

### FDA finalizes how drug-labeling rules apply to apps and software supplied by drug sponsors

The final guidance covers software outputs distributed by or for a drug sponsor that supplement or explain a prescription drug or drug-led combination product, whether or not the software itself is regulated as a device. It replaces the September 2023 draft and clarifies when software content becomes part of regulated drug labeling. Guidance reflects FDA's current enforcement thinking and is not itself a new statute or approval of any app.

[Official FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/regulatory-considerations-prescription-drug-use-related-software) · [Final guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/172165/download)

## AI Today

### Google completes the open Coral NPU architecture and releases a v1.0 compiler toolchain

Google Research published the 32-bit open-source Coral NPU RISC-V architecture with a matrix unit rated up to 1 TOPS at a targeted 10 mW average power, plus the v1.0 IREE/MLIR compiler for moving JAX, PyTorch and TensorFlow models to Coral hardware. Synaptics announced three commercial products using the design. These are engineering specifications and launch claims; real-world performance will vary by model, workload and device.

[Official Google Developers announcement](https://developers.googleblog.com/unifying-edge-ai-how-coral-npus-open-standard-brings-portable-energy-viable-intelligence-from-model-to-silicon/)

### Google releases a cross-platform GPU engine for on-device AI inference

ML Drift is an Apache-2.0 GPU compute layer spanning OpenGL ES, OpenCL, Metal and WebGPU and is positioned as the successor to the older TensorFlow Lite GPU delegate. Google says it is already used in Chrome, Photos, Meet and YouTube Shorts, and cites lower latency in several internal and partner deployments. The framework is available now, while desktop support remains preview-stage and benchmark gains are workload-specific.

[Official Google Developers release](https://developers.googleblog.com/ml-drift-next-gen-gpu-aiml-inference-at-the-edge/) · [Source repository](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/ml-drift)
