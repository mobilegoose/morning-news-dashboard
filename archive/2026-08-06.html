1 · U.S.–Iran war

Iran and Oman reached an understanding on a Hormuz shipping route, but not a final security deal

Iran’s Foreign Ministry said the two sides agreed on the route’s coordinates and were preparing a joint announcement. Reporting indicates consequential operating and security terms remain unsettled, so this is progress rather than a reopened, guaranteed-safe strait.

Why it mattersThe Strait is a critical energy and trade corridor; the distinction between coordinates and an enforceable passage agreement matters to ships, governments and prices.

3 · Public health

FDA approved the first orexin-targeting treatment for narcolepsy type 1

FDA approved Orzeyful (oveporexton) tablets for adults with narcolepsy type 1. The agency says it is the first treatment designed to restore orexin signaling and address the condition’s full symptom range.

Why it mattersThis is a new treatment mechanism for a disabling neurological sleep disorder, not merely another stimulant for daytime sleepiness.

2. Most Useful Development Today

Practical impact

Narcolepsy treatment

What the Orzeyful approval does—and does not mean

FDA’s approval is for adults with narcolepsy type 1. It was based on two 12-week placebo-controlled trials measuring wakefulness, daytime sleepiness, cataplexy and symptom severity. It is a prescription treatment, not a cure, and the FDA notes risks including insomnia, headache and urinary effects; patients should discuss eligibility and labeling with a clinician.

Useful takeawayPeople affected by narcolepsy type 1 now have an orexin-receptor option to ask their sleep specialist about; availability, insurance coverage and individual suitability are separate questions from approval.

6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Evidence and approvals

FDA approval · Aug. 5

Orzeyful targets the orexin deficit underlying narcolepsy type 1

In FDA’s summary of two randomized, placebo-controlled 12-week trials, the drug improved objective wakefulness and patient-reported sleepiness, cataplexy and overall severity. Trial duration limits what is known about long-term benefit and uncommon harms; approval does not mean it works for every patient.
