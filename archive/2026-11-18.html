1 · ALASKA · U.S. SENATE RESULT

Mary Peltola wins Alaska's final Senate contest, giving Democrats a 56–44 majority

The Associated Press called Alaska's ranked-choice Senate race for Democrat Mary Peltola, who defeated two-term Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan. The result completes a nine-seat Democratic gain and makes Peltola the first Alaska Native elected to the Senate. DOJ's separate investigation and appeal concerning a similarly named candidate remain pending, but no court order blocked the vote count or AP's result call.

2 · PUBLIC HEALTH · MEASLES

The United States loses measles-elimination status after sustained transmission

An independent regional commission concluded that the same measles lineage circulated in the United States for at least 12 months, so PAHO accepted the finding that endemic transmission had been re-established. Mexico and Bolivia also lost elimination status. The designation does not mean measles is untreatable or present everywhere; it signals that vaccination and outbreak control failed to interrupt a continuous chain of transmission.

3 · CIVIL RIGHTS · MILITARY SERVICE

Appeals court blocks the Pentagon from expelling the transgender service members who sued

A divided D.C. Circuit panel denied the administration's request to postpone implementation of its June ruling while the government seeks Supreme Court review. The order protects the more than two dozen current service members who are plaintiffs from discharge; it does not open enlistment to new transgender recruits or suspend the policy nationwide. The government may still file a Supreme Court petition and request emergency relief.

Associated Press

4 · CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE · NSA OUTAGE

A classified NSA communications network suffers an outage during maintenance

The National Security Agency said scheduled maintenance caused temporary degradation of internal systems, including disruption to NSANet, while mission-critical capabilities remained operational. Officials said they had no evidence connecting this episode to the recent Microsoft, FAA, banking and airline disruptions or to malicious activity. The concrete concern is resilience: multiple legacy systems have failed during overlapping upgrades and security patching.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

Measles, a rare-disease approval and two new free journal articles

PUBLIC HEALTH · MEASLES STATUS

Sustained measles transmission ends the United States' elimination designation

PAHO's commission uses a 12-month continuity standard, not a simple case-count threshold. Across the Americas, 58,213 confirmed cases and 82 deaths had been reported through November 13, compared with 12,806 cases in the same period of 2025. CDC says restoring U.S. status will require interrupting endemic transmission through vaccination, surveillance and coordinated outbreak response.

PAHO official announcementCDC joint statement

FDA APPROVAL · NIEMANN-PICK DISEASE TYPE C

FDA approves the first therapy shown to extend survival in infantile-onset NPC

FDA approved intrathecal adrabetadex (Lembaz) for adults and children weighing at least 7 kilograms whose neurological symptoms began before age 6. In 36 treated patients compared with matched external controls, median survival was 8.5 versus 4.9 years and the estimated risk of death was 55% lower. The nonrandomized external-control design limits causal certainty, and the boxed warning covers hearing impairment, including permanent hearing loss.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE · NOVEMBER 16

Physics-informed graph models improve forecasts of tau-PET progression

Machine Learning: Health published a peer-reviewed human imaging/modeling study using 779 longitudinal tau-PET pairs from 462 amyloid-positive ADNI participants, plus a structural-connectome reference from 84 participants. Adding two biological propagation equations to a graph neural network improved forecasting across the authors' evaluation metrics and produced more localized, biologically plausible attributions. Why it matters: better individualized tau forecasts could aid research and trial design, but this is not a diagnostic test or treatment. Major limitations include a selected ADNI cohort, reliance on model assumptions and internal validation, and no demonstration yet of improved patient outcomes. Published online November 16, 2026.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · MEDICARE & MEDICAID · NOVEMBER 17

A temporary primary-care fee increase did not improve measured SNF outcomes for dual eligibles with dementia

Health Services Research published a peer-reviewed difference-in-differences study of a national 20% Medicare fee-for-service sample linked to nursing-home assessments from 2012–2019. Using the Affordable Care Act's 2013–2014 Medicaid primary-care fee bump as a natural experiment, researchers found no significant change in visit volume, seven quality measures, skilled-nursing-facility length of stay or Medicare spending for dual-eligible adults age 65 and older with Alzheimer's disease or related dementias. The result is an association from a policy natural experiment—not proof that payment never matters. Limitations include state-level implementation differences, possible unmeasured concurrent changes, fee-for-service-only data and outcomes that may miss care quality. Published online November 17, 2026; the December issue date is not the release date.
