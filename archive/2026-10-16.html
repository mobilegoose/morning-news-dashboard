1 · OREGON SCHOOL SHOOTING

Three children and a teacher are killed at a Bend elementary school

Police said a 16-year-old fired through locked doors at Silver Rail Elementary on Thursday, killing children ages 5, 8 and 11 and a 54-year-old teacher. A 7-year-old child and another teacher were hospitalized in critical condition. A person at the scene followed the attacker into the school and fatally shot him before police arrived; investigators had not identified a motive by this morning. Bend-La Pine schools canceled Friday classes and activities.

2 · HURRICANE TRUDY

Category 3 Hurricane Trudy threatens Mexico's Pacific coast this weekend

The National Hurricane Center put Trudy's sustained winds at 120 mph at 6 a.m. Central, about 240 miles southwest of Acapulco, and forecast the major hurricane to approach southwestern and west-central Mexico Saturday into Sunday. Hurricane-force winds, coastal flooding, heavy rain and landslides are the main hazards; the forecast can still change, so people in the watch and warning areas should use official local instructions rather than a static track image.

3 · VOTING RIGHTS · FEDERAL LAWSUIT

Justice Department sues California to hold back ballots from more than 20,000 challenged voters

DOJ asked a federal court to stop California from counting mail ballots associated with people DHS says are likely noncitizens until citizenship is verified. The complaint is the government's allegation, not a court finding; California says the underlying data may be outdated and could include naturalized citizens. The suit creates an immediate conflict between election integrity enforcement and the risk of disenfranchising eligible voters close to the federal election.

4 · UKRAINE

Russian attacks force emergency power cuts as Ukraine seeks major European financing

Russian strikes hit nine Ukrainian regions overnight, wounded at least 15 people and triggered emergency outages affecting hundreds of thousands, according to Ukrainian officials. A new U.N. update counted 328 civilians killed and 2,105 wounded in September—85% more casualties than a year earlier. At an EU summit, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy asked Europe for roughly €70 billion toward Ukraine's projected €155 billion war cost next year.

5 · TELECOM ACCESS · FEDERAL APPEALS COURT

Appeals court invalidates the administrator of the $9 billion Universal Service Fund

A unanimous Fifth Circuit panel ruled that the FCC exceeded its authority when it created the private Universal Service Administrative Company to run the federal subsidy system for rural and low-income phone and internet service, Tribal areas, schools, libraries and rural health facilities. The ruling targets the administrator's legal structure, not a finding that each subsidy program is unlawful, and it will not take effect for at least two months while the FCC considers its options. Millions of households and institutions rely on the system, making the remedy and any appeal consequential.

MEDICARE OPEN ENROLLMENT · ACTIONABLE

Compare 2027 Medicare coverage now—and check total costs, drugs and networks before switching

Medicare Open Enrollment opened October 15 and runs through December 7. Beneficiaries may join, drop or switch Medicare Advantage or Part D plans, or move between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare, with changes effective January 1. Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning court check included

FIFTH CIRCUIT · AGENCY AUTHORITY

Consumers' Research v. FCC: court rejects USAC's legal foundation, but relief is delayed

Consumers' Research challenged the FCC-created company that calculates and administers Universal Service Fund contributions. The Fifth Circuit held that Congress had not specifically authorized the FCC to create USAC under the Government Corporation Control Act and rejected the FCC's argument that later statutes ratified the arrangement. This is a merits appellate ruling, but the mandate is delayed for at least two months; the FCC may seek rehearing or Supreme Court review, and the opinion does not itself specify how ongoing subsidies will be administered if it takes effect.

Official opinionFifth Circuit opinions page

D.C. DISTRICT COURT · STANDING

Judge dismisses John Brennan's pre-enforcement records-preservation lawsuit

Former CIA director John Brennan sought an order requiring federal officials to preserve records he says could support a future selective- or vindictive-prosecution defense if he is charged. Judge Jia Cobb dismissed the case for lack of standing because a future indictment and deletion of relevant records were too speculative; she did not decide whether any future prosecution would be lawful or vindictive. Brennan's preliminary-injunction request was denied as moot.

Court opinion

CDC · EBOLA TRAVEL ORDER

CDC extends entry restrictions tied to the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak through November 10

A Federal Register notice published Thursday continues a 30-day order covering certain non-U.S. citizens who were present in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda or South Sudan during the previous 21 days; U.S. citizens and nationals are exempt, while lawful permanent residents may be covered subject to listed exceptions. CDC cited 8,665 confirmed DRC cases and 4,178 deaths as of October 5. Comments are due October 30. The order is a border measure, not a finding that every covered traveler was exposed or infectious.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

New evidence and approvals with explicit limits

RECENT FALLBACK · FDA CANCER APPROVAL · OCTOBER 14

FDA expands Anktiva plus BCG to a high-risk papillary-only bladder-cancer group

FDA expanded Anktiva with BCG to adults with BCG-unresponsive, high-grade papillary-only non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer—the first approval specifically for that population. The agency extrapolated from a single-arm 77-patient trial in carcinoma in situ, where 62% had a complete response and some responses lasted at least one or two years, plus clinical and laboratory similarities between tumor types. That means the approval is not backed by a randomized trial in the newly covered papillary-only group. The label warns that delaying cystectomy can allow lethal metastatic spread; treatment decisions require an oncology or urology specialist.

FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEER-REVIEWED OBSERVATIONAL HUMAN STUDY

Post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC) are associated with distinct clinical phenotypes two years after SARS-CoV-2 infection: A data-driven approach

PLOS One · online October 15, 2026 · cross-sectional phenotype study. Researchers assessed 153 adults about two years after infection (mean age 53; 66% women); 88 met the study's symptom-based PASC definition and 65 did not. A principal component combining post-exertional malaise, ME/CFS features, sleep and coping measures was strongly associated with PASC, and clustering suggested more physical-impairment and cognitive-performance profiles among participants with severe fatigue. This supports multidimensional assessment and is relevant to function and disability, but it does not validate a diagnostic test or treatment. Major limitations include the small selected sample, cross-sectional design, symptom overlap between the PASC definition and analyzed component, and a very imprecise association estimate (odds ratio 97.1; 95% CI 16.5–570.3).
