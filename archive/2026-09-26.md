4. **Court lets DHS use the revamped SAVE voter database while litigation continues.** In *Department of Homeland Security v. League of Women Voters*, voting and privacy groups challenged DHS’s expansion of SAVE to use Social Security data and bulk searches, arguing it violated federal privacy and administrative laws and could wrongly flag naturalized citizens. The federal government asked the Court to pause lower-court orders that had vacated the program. The Court granted that emergency stay, so the modified database may operate during further appeals; this is not a final merits ruling. The majority said the government is likely authorized to share the data, while Justices Jackson, Sotomayor and Kagan warned about errors and disenfranchisement. [Official opinion (PDF)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/26a308_pok0.pdf) · [Readable case report](https://apnews.com/article/save-database-noncitizen-voters-trump-supreme-court-412e12f5a492dd20e407812f30f7c139)

5. **Supreme Court keeps Missouri’s 2022 congressional map for the November election.** In *People Not Politicians v. Onder*, referendum supporters asked the Court to pause federal orders requiring use of a new, more Republican-friendly 2025 map even though a valid state referendum petition suspended that map pending a public vote. The Court granted the stay without noted dissents, meaning the 2022 map remains in place for the 2026 general election. It is not a final merits decision, but it resolves which map governs this election; voters will still consider the 2025 map in November. [Official opinion (PDF)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/26a388_q86b.pdf) · [Readable case report](https://apnews.com/article/supreme-court-missouri-redistricting-congressional-map-3f43ba5f47681ea759115fbc250712d4)

## 2. Most Useful Development Today

### Check refrigerators and freezers for recalled raw-milk cheese linked to E. coli

FDA says Sierra Nevada Graziers raw-milk medium and sharp cheddar, jalapeño jack and Monterey jack—sold nationwide and online in 8-ounce, 16-ounce and 5-pound packages—are linked to an E. coli O26:H11 outbreak. Thirteen people have been sickened, eight hospitalized and three developed hemolytic uremic syndrome; Tennessee is among the affected states. Do not eat, sell or serve the cheese: return or discard it, then clean and sanitize surfaces and containers it touched. Seek medical care for severe symptoms. [FDA outbreak notice and product details](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o26h11-raw-milk-cheese-september-2026)

### New Free Journal Articles

#### Surface EMG models estimate upper-limb impairment after subacute stroke

**Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation · online September 24, 2026 · peer-reviewed prospective measurement study.** Researchers studied 23 people with subacute stroke using bilateral surface EMG during four wrist and hand tasks, with leave-one-subject-out validation. Wrist extension plus pincer grasp classified impairment with mean accuracy 0.92 and AUC 0.99; score prediction errors were 3.12 points for the wrist/hand subscore and 6.68 for the full upper-extremity scale. The result could support portable functional assessment, but the sample was very small, validation was internal, and prediction does not show improved recovery. [Free full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-026-02156-7)

#### Less burdensome preparation showed similar outcomes in metastatic thyroid-cancer treatment

**Journal of Nuclear Medicine · online September 24, 2026 · peer-reviewed multicenter retrospective study.** Across 352 patients and 564 radioactive-iodine treatments, survival and treatment responses did not significantly differ between recombinant human thyrotropin and thyroid-hormone withdrawal, while laboratory liver abnormalities and a composite laboratory safety outcome were more frequent after withdrawal. This matters because avoiding induced hypothyroidism may reduce treatment burden, but the study was nonrandomized, the rhTSH-only group had just 40 patients, and the results show association rather than proof of equivalence. [Free full text](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/early/2026/09/23/jnumed.126.273112)

#### Younger cholangiocarcinoma patients experienced longer diagnostic delays

**ESMO Gastrointestinal Oncology · online September 17, 2026; newly indexed in the 72-hour window · peer-reviewed nationwide retrospective claims study.** Among 116,106 patients, including 5,146 diagnosed before age 50, younger adults had longer symptom-to-diagnosis times; in the 43,137-person symptom-documented subset, their odds of a delay of at least 30 days were 24% higher. Racial-minority and publicly insured groups also showed disparities. Claims data cannot establish that age caused delay, may miss symptoms and clinical nuance, and do not measure all outcomes. [Free full text (PubMed Central)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602251/)
