1 · U.S. MILITARY & IRAN

A third aircraft carrier and about 9,000 more U.S. troops are heading to the Middle East

The Pentagon is sending the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier group and a Marine amphibious group as President Trump weighs additional strikes on Iran. The reinforcement could put more than 20,000 U.S. personnel in the region by late October. It materially expands U.S. combat capacity; it is not itself an announcement that new strikes have begun.

2 · U.S. ECONOMY

September hiring slowed to 29,000 jobs and unemployment rose to 4.2%

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said payroll growth changed little in September; July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000 jobs. Health care added 17,000 positions, but hiring across the economy was weak. The report strengthens the case for the Federal Reserve to pause before another rate increase, though the Fed will also weigh inflation.

3 · FEDERAL COURT & VOTING

Judge dismisses a noncitizen-voting prosecution after finding the federal law unconstitutional

U.S. District Judge David Leibowitz dismissed the charge against Florida resident Chelsea Cox, ruling that the Constitution leaves voter qualifications to the states rather than Congress. Florida separately bans noncitizen voting. The ruling applies to Cox and does not bind other judges, but it is the first reported federal ruling invalidating the 1996 statute and could affect similar pending prosecutions.

4 · AFGHANISTAN & PAKISTAN

Pakistani airstrikes killed at least 10 Afghan civilians, the U.N. and Kabul say

The strikes hit Helmand and Kunar provinces; most of those killed were children, and nine people were injured, according to the U.N. mission. Pakistan said it targeted militant sites and reported 22 militants killed, a claim not independently verified. The incident deepens a cross-border conflict that has already caused more than 1,100 Afghan civilian deaths and injuries this year.

Associated Press

5 · SUPREME COURT & RELIGIOUS LAND USE

Supreme Court will decide whether zoning imposed a substantial burden on a Kentucky church

Missionaries of Saint John the Baptist wants to build an outdoor Marian shrine on residentially zoned land beside its church; neighboring homeowners opposed the project, including over traffic. The Court granted certiorari only on whether the restriction substantially burdens religious exercise under the federal Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act. A cert grant means the justices will decide that legal question after briefing and argument; it does not approve the shrine or reverse the Kentucky court yet.

ACA · PAYMENTS IN PROGRESS

What to know about the $500 HealthCare.gov refunds

The payments are limited to roughly 1 million people in 30 states who used the federal marketplace and did not receive premium tax credits; state-run marketplace customers are not in this program. Some households may receive more than one payment when multiple people qualify. There is no announced application process. Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning court check included

FEDERAL COURT · UNITED STATES v. COX

Noncitizen-voting charge dismissed on constitutional grounds

Chelsea Cox, a Jamaican national living in Florida, was accused of voting in the 2020 election. She asked the court to dismiss the federal charge, arguing that states—not Congress—set voter qualifications. Judge David Leibowitz agreed and dismissed this count; that is a trial-court merits ruling in her case, not a nationwide injunction or Supreme Court precedent. The Justice Department may appeal, and similar defendants can cite—but are not automatically governed by—the decision.

Readable case reportReuters (potentially limited)

FEDERAL COURT · OVERSEAS VOTING

DNC sues over a last-minute change to the federal overseas voter form

The complaint says the Pentagon used an emergency process to remove the checkbox used by U.S. citizens who have never lived in the country; 38 states and D.C. let at least some such citizens vote through family ties. The DNC asks a federal judge to restore the prior form. Filing a suit changes nothing by itself: the government has not yet been enjoined, and the court must decide whether the agencies skipped required procedures.

Associated PressCase page and filing

SUPREME COURT · MISSIONARIES OF SAINT JOHN THE BAPTIST v. FREDERIC

Court grants review in a church-zoning dispute

The Catholic nonprofit sought permission to build a grotto and shrine on residentially zoned land beside its Kentucky church; neighbors Joel and Elizabeth Frederic challenged the local approval, and the Kentucky Supreme Court ultimately rejected the church's federal religious-land-use claim. The church asked the U.S. Supreme Court to intervene. The justices granted certiorari limited to whether the land-use restriction creates a “substantial burden” under RLUIPA. That puts the legal issue on the merits docket for briefing and argument; it is not a merits ruling, and the justices declined for now to take the separate equal-terms question. The result could clarify how courts assess burdens on religious construction projects.

PASC · HUMAN OBSERVATIONAL STUDY

Whole-genome methylation sequencing uncovers PASC signature elicited by T4 lymphocytes, shared with pulmonary fibrosis risk

JCI Insight · online September 29, 2026 · peer-reviewed open-access observational study. Across two prospective cohorts totaling 203 people with PASC, plus an independent single-cell cohort, researchers found 16 blood DNA-methylation regions that distinguished PASC from multiple control groups; single-cell analysis pointed mainly to T4 lymphocytes, and part of the pattern overlapped with 68 pre-pandemic people at risk for pulmonary fibrosis. Why it matters: it offers a plausible immune-cell biomarker pathway for validation. Limitation: associations in blood do not establish cause, diagnosis or treatment benefit.

POTS · FUNCTION & DISABILITY

Multimorbidity, functional impairment and health-related quality of life in postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome

BMJ Open · online September 29, 2026 · peer-reviewed open-access cross-sectional study. An Australian registry included 500 people with physician-confirmed POTS; 86.8% were female, mean age was 31.3, median diagnostic delay was three years, and 22% were unemployed or unable to attend education. Greater autonomic symptom burden was associated with fatigue, gastrointestinal symptoms, health-care use and worse quality of life. Limitation: a self-selected, mostly White and female registry with self-reported measures cannot establish causation or population prevalence.

PEDIATRIC SPINAL CORD STROKE · CASE SERIES

Pediatric spinal cord stroke: clinical presentation, MRI features, and suspected mechanisms

Neurology: Clinical Practice · online September 30, 2026 · peer-reviewed retrospective case series. A specialized referral center reviewed 56 patients younger than 18 treated from 2010–2025. Presentations were hyperacute; lesions were often longitudinally extensive and cervical, and investigators proposed a mechanism in 63%, including vascular compression in 30%, while 38% remained idiopathic. Why it matters: it sharpens recognition of a rare, disabling emergency. Limitation: the single-center retrospective sample was small, mechanisms were often suspected rather than proven, and the study did not compare treatments.

Disabled resident physicians with depressive symptoms reported higher recognition and treatment seeking

Medical Education Online · online September 29, 2026 · peer-reviewed longitudinal-cohort analysis. Among 940 interns who screened positive for depression and had disability data, 127 reported a disability; adjusted analyses linked disability with higher symptom recognition and treatment seeking. Limitation: observational, self-reported subgroup results cannot show that disability caused the difference.
