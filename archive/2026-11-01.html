Analysis finds major mismatches in DOJ’s California voter list

Two UCLA data scientists told a federal court that 8,880 of the 20,930 names DOJ flagged as possible noncitizens did not match California’s current voter roll. DEFAMATION · FINAL DISTRICT-COURT DISMISSAL

Trump’s Wall Street Journal suit is dismissed with prejudice

The president sued over a report that he contributed a suggestive letter to Jeffrey Epstein’s 50th-birthday album. Judge Gayles concluded that the amended complaint still did not adequately plead actual malice—the demanding fault standard a public figure must meet—and dismissed it with prejudice. That ends the case at the trial-court level; it is not a finding that every factual assertion in the article was independently proven, and Trump may appeal.

Disability rights and menopause-care labeling

RECENT FALLBACK · DISABILITY RIGHTS · OCTOBER 30

HHS excludes some gender dysphoria from its Section 504 disability definition

HHS finalized a rule stating that gender dysphoria not resulting from a physical impairment is excluded from disability coverage under the agency’s Section 504 regulations. It applies to programs and activities receiving HHS funds. The change narrows one disability-discrimination route in those settings; it does not erase every possible protection under other federal, state or local law, and future litigation may test HHS’s interpretation.

RECENT FALLBACK · MENOPAUSE CARE · OCTOBER 30

FDA approves revised warnings for three more estradiol patches

FDA approved updated labeling for three additional menopausal-hormone-therapy products, removing boxed-warning statements about cardiovascular disease, breast cancer and probable dementia from these estradiol transdermal patches. HHS also reported supply-expansion work and said it saw no imminent nationwide shortage. A label revision is not a finding that hormone therapy is risk-free or appropriate for everyone; product-specific prescribing information and individual risks still matter.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

A rare-disease approval and two new free journal articles

RECENT FALLBACK · RARE DISEASE APPROVAL · OCTOBER 30

FDA gives accelerated approval to the first plasmid-DNA immunotherapy

FDA approved Voceryq for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, an HPV-driven disease that can require repeated airway surgery. The evidence came from an open-label multicenter trial in 32 adults; among 26 with at least three baseline surgeries, seven required no surgical intervention in the first treatment year. Approval rests on reduced surgery frequency as an intermediate endpoint, so the manufacturer must verify clinical benefit in post-approval trials.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · HEALTH RESEARCH FUNDING · OCTOBER 30

NIH funding for transgender and nonbinary youth research remained a tiny share of the portfolio

JAMA Network Open published a peer-reviewed cross-sectional analysis of NIH awards from 2012–2024. Researchers identified 97 unique awards plus 42 supplements totaling $158.3 million—0.06% of NIH funding; 22 awards concerned gender-affirming medical care and none focused on surgery. The study maps investment rather than measuring health outcomes or establishing the optimal funding level. Keyword and RePORTER methods can miss or misclassify grants, and population-proportional comparisons are benchmarks, not causal evidence. Published online October 30, 2026.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · BRCA1/2 · OCTOBER 30

Risk-reducing mastectomy is associated with lower mortality in BRCA1/2 carriers—but the evidence is observational

A peer-reviewed network meta-analysis in JAMA Network Open synthesized five nonrandomized studies, with individual sample sizes from 247 to 2,857. Risk-reducing mastectomy was associated with lower all-cause mortality than MRI-based screening after accounting for salpingo-oophorectomy; the breast-cancer-specific association was strongest for BRCA1. This supports shared decision-making, not a universal surgical recommendation. Residual confounding, immortal-time bias, indirect comparisons, no closed network loop and inability to assess publication bias limit causal conclusions. Published online October 30, 2026.
