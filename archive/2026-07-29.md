## 2. Most Useful Development Today

### Medicare Part D: do not treat $41.33 as your plan’s final price

The $41.33 national base beneficiary premium is used in plan calculations; it is not what every enrollee will pay. Standalone plans publish their premiums and benefits in September. When the Annual Notice of Change arrives, compare the premium, deductible, formulary, pharmacy network and total annual drug cost during October 15–December 7 open enrollment. [CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-part-d-2027-national-average-monthly-bid-amount-information) · [Medicare comparison guidance](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/health-plans/your-coverage-options/compare-original-medicare-medicare-advantage)

## 4. Supreme Court & Legal

### *Trump v. Barbara*: rehearing deadline passed

The dispute concerned an executive order that would deny citizenship to some children born in the United States when their parents were unlawfully or temporarily present. Affected parents and states challenged it under the Fourteenth Amendment and federal law. The Court granted review before judgment and ruled 6–3 on June 30 that the order was unlawful—a merits judgment, not a stay or denial of review. The administration did not request rehearing by the deadline, leaving the judgment final and the order blocked. The missed deadline is procedural; there was no new opinion today. [Official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-365.html) · [Official opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-365_4hdj.pdf) · [AP, July 29](https://apnews.com/article/b4db703166c906b8d9224f72268f61b5)

### Late July 28 execution orders

- **James Duckett:** The former police officer, convicted of the 1987 rape and murder of 11-year-old Teresa McAbee, sought stays and review of two petitions concerning innocence claims and further analysis of inconclusive DNA testing. The Court denied both stays and both certiorari petitions without noted dissent. Denial of a stay refused temporary emergency relief; denial of certiorari meant the Court would not review the claims. The orders permitted the execution but did not establish a merits precedent. [Official order, 26A119](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/072826zr_8n5a.pdf) · [Official order, 26A129](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/072826zr2_jifl.pdf)
- **Dominick Occhicone:** Occhicone, convicted of the 1986 murders of his former girlfriend’s parents, sought a stay and review based on his age and health and the conditions surrounding Florida’s rare same-day double execution. The Court denied the stay and certiorari without noted dissent, allowing the execution to proceed without resolving a broader constitutional issue. Florida executed both men later Tuesday. [Official order, 26A121](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/072826zr1_b97c.pdf) · [AP, July 28](https://apnews.com/article/4c313a84631ad20a62998f7dd061dbcf)

No separate July 29 merits opinion or order appeared on the official indexes before publication. [Slip opinions](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/slipopinion/25) · [Orders](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/ordersofthecourt)
