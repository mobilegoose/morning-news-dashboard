1 · SAUDI ARABIA · REGIONAL ESCALATION

Missiles targeting Riyadh damage an Aramco building after interception

Saudi Arabia said its defenses intercepted two ballistic and two cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh and other areas Sunday. Falling debris damaged several floors of a Saudi Aramco building in Riyadh's financial district; authorities reported no casualties. Yemen's Houthis claimed the launches as retaliation for Saudi-led strikes. The interception and physical damage are confirmed by Saudi authorities, while claims about military targets and responsibility come from the belligerents.

2 · UKRAINE · ATTACK ON RESCUERS

Russian drone hits emergency crews at the site of a deadly earlier strike

Ukraine's emergency service said a Russian drone struck rescuers Sunday at the former school and shelter in Komyshuvakha that guided bombs hit earlier in the week. No one was injured in the new strike, but specialized rescue equipment was damaged while 45 people remained listed as missing from the earlier attack. Russia said it had struck military-linked electrical infrastructure; the parties' targeting claims cannot be independently resolved in real time.

Associated Press

3 · ISRAEL · GOVERNMENT FORMATION

President Herzog gives Gadi Eisenkot the first mandate to form a government

Israeli President Isaac Herzog formally asked opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to try to assemble a governing coalition after the election produced no 61-seat majority. Eisenkot's bloc holds 48 seats, Benjamin Netanyahu's caretaker coalition 53 and Arab-led parties 19. Eisenkot has 28 days, with a possible two-week extension. The mandate starts negotiations; it does not install a new government, and Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister.

Associated Press

4 · COLOMBIA · MILITARY BASE ATTACK

Drone strike kills three soldiers and wounds 16 at a Colombian base

A drone attack on the Santa Rosa del Sur military base killed three soldiers and injured 16, Colombian authorities said. Officials had not identified the attacker by publication. The surrounding region is contested by the Gulf Clan, the National Liberation Army and dissident former FARC factions, so assigning responsibility before an official finding would be premature.

Associated Press

Judge proposes an outside investigation into whether officials misled her

Reyes is giving the parties an opportunity to respond. This is a proposed investigative appointment—not a contempt finding, sanctions order or final determination that anyone acted improperly.

Most Useful Development Today

A concrete federal action effective today

PUERTO RICO · GRID RELIABILITY

DOE renews emergency grid orders through February 5

The Energy Department renewed two Federal Power Act § 202(c) emergency orders authorizing Puerto Rico's utility to operate specified generation units when needed and continue vegetation and asset-management work on critical transmission facilities. The orders take effect today and run through February 5, 2027. Practical meaning: they preserve short-term reliability tools for the island's 3.2 million residents, but they do not themselves modernize the grid or guarantee against outages.

NEW ZEALAND · ELECTION RESULT

Neither bloc secures a clear majority; special ballots will decide the balance

With about 2.8 million election-night ballots counted, the center-right bloc appeared to hold 67 of 131 seats, the left 57 and the new Opportunity party seven. Roughly 400,000 special ballots remain, and final results are due November 27. The count is a concrete result and gives minor parties bargaining power, but it is too early to say who will form the next government.

ENERGY POLICY · PUERTO RICO

Emergency orders preserve dispatch and vegetation-management authority

DOE's renewed orders let PREPA operate specified generation resources under defined reliability conditions and continue work near critical transmission lines. Section 202(c) is an emergency reliability authority, not a general waiver of environmental or labor law. The renewal addresses near-term operational risk while leaving Puerto Rico's larger affordability, generation and grid-reconstruction problems unresolved.

RECENT FALLBACK · PUBLIC HEALTH · NOVEMBER 6

CDC closes turtle-linked Salmonella investigation at 24 cases in 13 states

CDC's final update reports 24 infections, seven hospitalizations among 18 people with hospitalization information and no deaths. Fifty-four percent of patients were children under five; 14 of 16 interviewed patients had contact with pet turtles. Whole-genome sequencing linked the illnesses, but investigators did not identify a single supplier. The outbreak is over, while CDC still advises against buying turtles with shells under four inches and recommends handwashing after contact.

ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE · WATER SUPPLY

Flood runoff raises Lake Powell more than 20 feet—but does not end the drought

Late-October and early-November storms lifted Lake Powell more than 20 feet from its September low and replenished some local reservoirs and aquifers. The lake remains only a little over one-quarter full, and federal Colorado River operating decisions still depend heavily on winter snowpack. The gains are measurable and useful, but officials describe them as temporary rather than a reversal of the region's long drought.

Associated Press

RECENT FALLBACK · OFFICIAL SUPREME COURT AUDIO · NOVEMBER 3

St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy oral argument

Colorado's universal preschool program permits several secular enrollment preferences but excluded Catholic preschools that require families to support church teaching on sex and gender. The schools asked the Supreme Court to hold that the program is not generally applicable and that conditioning a public benefit this way violates the Free Exercise Clause. The Court granted review on those two questions and heard argument; it has not decided the merits. The recording is useful for hearing how the justices test religious-liberty claims against equal-access rules affecting LGBTQ+ families.

RECENT FALLBACK · OFFICIAL SUPREME COURT AUDIO · NOVEMBER 2

Younge v. Fulton Judicial Circuit District Attorney's Office oral argument

Jasmine Younge says she was fired from a senior Fulton County prosecutor's-office job because she was pregnant and sued under Title VII. The office later argued that she fell within the statute's personal-staff exception even though it had not pleaded that affirmative defense in its answer. The Supreme Court granted review to decide whether a defendant may use an unpleaded affirmative defense at summary judgment without satisfying the rules for amending pleadings. The Court heard argument and has not ruled; the case may affect civil procedure well beyond employment discrimination.
