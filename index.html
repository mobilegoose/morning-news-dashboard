Ranked by public importance
  1. Iran fired missiles toward Jordan as U.S. and Saudi aircraft struck Iran-backed militias in Iraq. The Inflation Reduction Act’s statutory 6% cap on the base beneficiary premium remains, but the extra demonstration subsidy will not. CMS set the 2027 base beneficiary premium at $41.33; each plan’s actual premium will be announced in the fall. CMS, July 28

2. Most Useful Development Today

What to do with it

Medicare Part D · 2027 planning

Do not treat the $41.33 base premium as your plan’s final price

CMS’s $41.33 figure is a national statutory base used in plan calculations, not the premium every enrollee will pay. Standalone Part D plans will publish plan-specific premiums and benefits in September. When your Annual Notice of Change arrives, compare premium, deductible, formulary, pharmacy network and total annual drug cost—not premium alone—during Medicare open enrollment, October 15 through December 7. CMS fact sheet · Medicare plan-comparison guidance

Why it matters: The end of the temporary subsidy could affect the pricing strategy of plans covering millions of people, but the personal impact cannot be known until plan-level notices are released.

4. Supreme Court & Legal

Official Court check completed

Merits decision · Citizenship

Trump v. Barbara: rehearing deadline passed, leaving the June judgment final

The dispute concerned an executive order that would deny U.S. citizenship to some children born in the country when their parents were unlawfully or temporarily present. Affected parents and states challenged the order, arguing that it violated the Fourteenth Amendment and federal law. The Supreme Court granted review before judgment, then ruled 6–3 on June 30 that the order was unlawful—a merits ruling resolving the central legal question, not a temporary stay or a denial of review. The administration did not seek rehearing by Tuesday’s deadline, so the judgment remains in force and the order stays blocked. That missed deadline is procedural; the Court did not issue a new opinion today. Official docket · Official opinion · AP explainer, July 29

Late July 28 orders · Capital punishment

Court denied three last-minute petitions before Florida’s double execution

James Duckett: The former police officer, convicted of the 1987 rape and murder of 11-year-old Teresa McAbee, asked the Court to halt his execution and review two petitions centered on innocence claims and requests for further analysis of inconclusive DNA testing. The Court denied both stay applications and both certiorari petitions without noted dissent. A stay denial refused temporary emergency relief; denial of certiorari meant the Court would not review the underlying claims. Those orders allowed the execution to proceed but did not create a merits precedent or endorse the lower court’s reasoning. Official Duckett order, 26A119 · Official Duckett order, 26A129

Dominick Occhicone: Occhicone, convicted of the 1986 murders of his former girlfriend’s parents, asked for a stay and review based on his age and health and the conditions surrounding Florida’s first same-day double execution in decades. The Court denied both the stay and certiorari without noted dissent, allowing his execution to proceed while resolving no broader constitutional question. Florida executed both men later Tuesday. Official Occhicone order, 26A121 · AP, July 28

5. Policy & Benefits

Medicare, disability and detention health

Medicare Part D

CMS ended an extra premium subsidy but kept the statutory premium-growth cap

The voluntary demonstration that reduced standalone Part D plan premiums will end after 2026. The IRA’s 6% annual cap on the base beneficiary premium remains; CMS calculated a 2027 national average monthly bid of $296.05 and a base beneficiary premium of $41.33. Actual plan premiums and benefit details arrive in September. CMS, July 28 · Reuters, July 28 — link may be limited

Immigration detention · Medical care

Court-appointed monitor found California City facility failed all eight health-care measures reviewed

A monitor appointed in federal litigation reported that the ICE detention center lacked a reliable system for adequate care and was not complying with a court order. The assessment reviewed 141 medical records, interviewed 40 patients and examined thousands of documents during a three-day inspection. The filing is a monitor’s compliance report, not a final merits judgment; ICE and contractor CoreCivic said they were reviewing or disputed aspects of the findings. CalMatters, July 28 · Court-appointed monitor’s report

6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Outbreak and treatment-development updates

Michigan · Cyclosporiasis

State case count rose to 10,077, with 160 hospitalizations reported

Michigan’s health department updated the outbreak total Wednesday and said lettuce or salad greens may be the source, but investigators had not identified a specific type, grower or supplier. Cyclosporiasis can cause prolonged or relapsing diarrhea; people with symptoms should contact a clinician, particularly if they are older or immunocompromised. Michigan DHHS outbreak page, updated July 29

Osteosarcoma · Phase III company result

Ris-Rez trial met its progression-free-survival endpoint in relapsed bone cancer

GSK said Hansoh’s China-based phase III ARTEMIS-011 trial found that the investigational antibody-drug conjugate risvutatug rezetecan improved progression-free survival versus chemotherapy in people whose osteosarcoma had progressed or relapsed after at least two prior systemic treatments. Full numerical results and safety data have not yet been presented, so the announcement supports further regulatory work but does not establish approval or availability. GSK, July 28

Evidence label: Company-reported phase III topline result; peer-reviewed data unavailable.

Melanoma · FDA review

FDA reviewers questioned whether Replimune’s single-arm RP1 evidence can show benefit

Briefing materials for Thursday’s advisory committee said trial-design problems and concurrent nivolumab make it difficult to determine RP1’s contribution in advanced melanoma. The panel will advise FDA on whether the results are interpretable and clinically meaningful; this is pre-decision review, not a rejection or approval. FDA meeting materials · FDA briefing document
