1 · IRAN WAR · STRAIT OF HORMUZ

Iran reports seven tankers damaged or burning in the Strait of Hormuz

Iranian state television said seven tankers were damaged Thursday—some in attacks and others after striking mines—the largest number reported in a single day since the war began February 28. The accounts were not all independently verified, but the incidents and an Iranian Revolutionary Guard warning point to rising danger in a waterway central to global energy shipments.

Associated Press

2 · RUSSIA–UKRAINE · OVERNIGHT ATTACKS

Russia hits Kyiv with 21 ballistic missiles as both sides exchange large strikes

Ukrainian officials said three waves of Russian ballistic missiles killed at least four people and wounded dozens; Russia said it intercepted 808 Ukrainian drones, including 630 headed toward Moscow. The scale of the exchange matters because it followed Vladimir Putin's agreement in principle to resume U.S.-mediated talks, underscoring that diplomacy has not produced a ceasefire.

Associated Press

3 · SUPREME COURT · TRANSGENDER HEALTH CARE

Supreme Court refuses to pause protections for transgender federal prisoners

In Trump v. Kingdom, transgender prisoners challenged a Bureau of Prisons policy that ended gender-affirming hormones, surgery and social accommodations for people already receiving care. The administration asked the justices to stay lower-court injunctions; the Court denied that emergency request without explanation, with Justices Thomas and Alito saying they would grant it. The protections remain in force while the appeal continues; this was not a final merits ruling.

Official docket 26A416Associated Press

4 · VOTING RIGHTS · FEDERAL COURT

Judge restricts DHS door-knocking over suspected noncitizen voters in nine states

U.S. District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín found the government's data-driven operation likely relied on serious errors and risked intimidating lawful voters in violation of the Voting Rights Act. Her injunction blocks election-related field visits and related steps in nine states through Election Day, but she paused it until 5 p.m. Friday so the government can appeal. The ruling is preliminary, not a final judgment on every claim.

CBS NewsWashington Post

5 · STUDENT LOANS · PUBLIC SERVICE

Justice Department drops two appeals seeking to narrow Public Service Loan Forgiveness

DOJ withdrew appeals of June decisions that struck down a rule allowing the Education Department to exclude employers it said had a “substantial illegal purpose,” including organizations serving undocumented immigrants or providing gender-affirming care to minors. The withdrawals leave the rule vacated and preserve ordinary PSLF eligibility for qualifying government and nonprofit workers, although a separate PSLF appeal remains pending.

Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning Supreme Court check included

TRUMP v. KINGDOM · STAY DENIED

Lower-court protection for transgender prisoners remains in force

Transgender federal prisoners say the Bureau of Prisons unlawfully cut off medically necessary hormones and related accommodations; the government argues the new policy is lawful and asked the Supreme Court to suspend the injunctions while it appeals. On October 21, the Court denied that stay request without explaining its reasoning; Justices Thomas and Alito would have granted it. A stay denial preserves the status quo during litigation and does not decide the case's merits. The lower-court appeal now continues.

Official docket 26A416Associated Press case explainer

MULLIN v. FRIENDS OF THE RUIDOSA CHURCH · PENDING APPLICATION

Administration asks Supreme Court to let Big Bend border-wall work resume

The government says Congress authorized DHS to waive laws and continue barriers in the Big Bend region; conservation and local groups argue the waivers exceed that authority and threaten wildlife, land and a historic church. The administration filed an emergency stay application October 21 asking the justices to lift a lower-court injunction during the appeal. The Court has not ruled, so construction remains blocked for now; a stay decision would address interim relief, not finally resolve the legality of the project.

PUBLIC SERVICE LOAN FORGIVENESS · APPEALS WITHDRAWN

Employer-exclusion rule stays vacated after DOJ ends two appeals

Two federal judges ruled in June that the Education Department lacked authority to disqualify nonprofit employers based on the administration's “substantial illegal purpose” test. DOJ's one-sentence withdrawals leave those judgments intact, so qualifying public servants should continue using the normal PSLF process. Borrowers still need 120 qualifying payments and certified eligible employment; the action does not itself discharge an individual balance.

EMPLOYER HEALTH INSURANCE · KFF SURVEY

Workers' family-plan contributions rise 9% to an average $7,471

KFF's 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey found the total average family premium reached $28,303, up 5%, while employees' average contribution rose $621. Workers at small firms paid substantially more toward family coverage on average than workers at large firms—$9,687 versus $6,840. The survey documents employer-plan costs; it does not measure every household's actual spending or predict next year's premiums.

BLOOD SAFETY · FDA UPDATE

FDA says blood centers may stop asking donors about Uganda travel for Ebola risk

FDA updated its Ebola blood-donation information after Uganda's outbreak ended: the country's last confirmed case was reported June 21 and no active cases remain. Blood establishments may discontinue the Uganda travel-or-residence question, while existing safeguards and questions for other affected countries continue. This is operational guidance for blood centers, not a change in general travel advice.
