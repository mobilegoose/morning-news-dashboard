1 · U.S. MIDTERMS · ELECTION DAY

Voting is underway nationwide after more than 57 million early ballots

4 · SUPREME COURT · CALIFORNIA BALLOTS

Supreme Court denies the federal bid to segregate more than 20,000 California ballots

The United States asked the Court for an injunction pending appeal that would force California to set aside mail ballots from voters federal officials suspect may be noncitizens. The Court denied the emergency application without explanation or noted dissent. That leaves California’s existing counting procedures in place while lower-court litigation continues; it is not a merits judgment about any voter’s citizenship or the government’s broader claims.

5 · DOJ · ELECTION MONITORING

Justice Department deploys about 1,000 monitors and observers across all 50 states

DOJ announced its largest Election Day monitoring effort, coordinated by the Civil Rights Division and involving department personnel, U.S. attorneys’ offices and federal observers from the Office of Personnel Management. A federal judge allowed the program to proceed after the government represented that monitors would be unarmed and would not exercise arrest authority. Their legal role is observation and federal voting-rights compliance, not running polling places or deciding voter eligibility.

Supreme Court & Legal

Emergency relief explained; final same-morning check included

SUPREME COURT EMERGENCY APPLICATION · 26A624

What the California-ballot denial does—and does not decide

Federal officials argued that California should segregate ballots tied to a list of voters they considered likely noncitizens until citizenship could be checked; California opposed an emergency change to its counting rules. Justice Kagan referred the request to the full Court, which denied an injunction pending appeal on November 2 without reasons. Procedurally, California may count under existing rules while the Ninth Circuit appeal proceeds. The denial neither grants certiorari nor resolves the merits, and unexplained emergency relief carries no stated rationale. A final same-morning check found no newer merits opinion, certiorari grant or emergency disposition by the publication cutoff.

FEDERAL VOTER DATA · PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION

Judge blocks federal distribution of suspected-noncitizen voter lists to election officials

A federal judge temporarily barred DHS and DOJ from sending state and local election officials lists purporting to identify actual or potential noncitizen voters. The court found likely Privacy Act and constitutional problems and allowed ordinary individual citizenship checks through the SAVE system to continue. A preliminary injunction preserves the status quo while the lawsuit is litigated; it is not a final judgment.

NINTH CIRCUIT · ELECTION-WORKER THREATS

Full appeals court vacates panel stay in dispute over threats to election officials

Voting-rights and labor groups sued after federal officials threatened prosecution if California election workers refused to segregate ballots based on federal eligibility claims. A district judge issued a temporary restraining order; a divided Ninth Circuit panel stayed it, but the full court then granted rehearing and vacated the panel decision. That removes the panel ruling while the en banc court considers the government’s emergency request; it is not a final merits decision.

VOTING RIGHTS · DISABILITY ACCESS · MONITOR LIMITS

DOJ says monitors will watch federal-law compliance but cannot direct polling places

DOJ says its teams will monitor the Voting Rights Act, Help America Vote Act, Americans with Disabilities Act and other federal protections. The department’s release also provides a complaint channel, while the overnight court record says monitors are to be unarmed and lack arrest authority. State and local election officials still administer voting, and monitors cannot stop someone from casting a ballot on the spot.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

A final FDA guidance and current outbreak risk update

FDA FINAL GUIDANCE · CELL AND GENE THERAPY

FDA replaces its 2011 potency guidance for cell and gene therapies

FDA issued final guidance recommending a lifecycle Potency Assurance Strategy: developers should connect manufacturing controls, potency assays and clinical performance from early development through patient delivery. The guidance is not an approval of any product and is generally nonbinding, but it sets the agency’s current expectations for showing that each batch can deliver its intended biological activity.

OPENAI · BENCHMARK · NOVEMBER 2

LifeVal measures how AI handles everyday tasks, not just academic tests

OpenAI released LifeVal, an evaluation built from time-use datasets and a demographically representative survey of 3,995 people. Tasks cover caregiving, health, education, finances, household administration and access to government services, and test whether systems can complete practical workflows. OpenAI reports strong results for its current models, but the benchmark is company-created and should be independently replicated before broad claims are made.

GITHUB ACTIONS · SECURITY POLICY · NOVEMBER 2

Public repositories get two brownouts before pull_request_target is blocked by default

GitHub moved permanent enforcement of its default block on pull_request_target workflows to December 2 and scheduled brownouts for November 9 and 16. The trigger runs with the base repository’s privileges and can expose secrets if a workflow checks out untrusted fork code. Public-repository maintainers should review the evaluation warnings and explicitly allow only workflows that genuinely need the trigger.
