1 · FRANCE · MASS ARRESTS AFTER PROTESTS

France reports at least 1,800 arrests after violence and looting overtook parts of nationwide demonstrations

Authorities said about 550,000 people joined Saturday's demonstrations over living costs, wages and school conditions; organizers estimated 800,000. Most marched peacefully, but masked groups set fires, looted businesses and attacked public buildings in multiple cities. More than 500 police, gendarmes and firefighters were injured, and Limoges City Hall was nearly destroyed. President Emmanuel Macron called an emergency meeting and planned a national address Sunday evening. The arrest and injury totals are official preliminary figures and may change.

2 · GUAM · SUPER TYPHOON CHAMPI

Federal emergency aid is authorized as Guam begins assessing Champi's damage

President Trump approved Guam's emergency-declaration request after Super Typhoon Champi passed about 10 miles south of Merizo. Guam remained in Condition of Readiness 1: officials told residents to stay sheltered while tropical-storm-force winds, rain bands, flooding and dangerous seas continued. The power authority said 51% of distribution circuits were online at 9 a.m.; schools will be closed Monday for damage checks. The hospital reported uninterrupted care and three minor storm-related emergency visits. Follow Guam officials for the transition to COR 4 before going outside.

Category 2 Hurricane Trudy tracks alongside western Mexico with flooding and mudslide risks

NORTH CAROLINA · ACTION BY TUESDAY

Request a mail ballot by 5 p.m. Tuesday—or use early voting through October 31

North Carolina's deadline for a county elections office to receive a civilian absentee-ballot request is 5 p.m. Tuesday, October 20. Any registered voter may request one online or on paper; military and overseas deadlines differ. IMMIGRATION DETENTION · DUE PROCESS

Texas federal judge orders an Ecuadorian man released after six months without an individualized hearing

An Ecuadorian citizen who applied for admission in 2022, was paroled, and was re-detained by ICE at an April check-in challenged his continued civil detention while his removal appeal is pending. Judge John Kazen granted habeas relief, finding that more than six months without a hearing on flight risk or danger violated procedural due process, and ordered release under reasonable conditions by October 20. The ruling applies directly to this petitioner, is a district-court decision rather than nationwide precedent, and the government may seek appellate relief.

Health coverage and tax-credit administration

ACA PREMIUM TAX CREDIT · PROPOSED RULE

Treasury proposal details tighter Marketplace verification and the Medicaid work-requirement interaction

Treasury and IRS proposed rules to implement statutory changes to the Affordable Care Act premium tax credit. Among other provisions, a month generally would not qualify for the credit unless the Exchange verifies residence and citizenship or lawful presence; when advance credits are paid, income, family size, other coverage and prior-year reconciliation would also need verification. For tax years after 2026, someone denied Medicaid solely for failing the new community-engagement requirement would generally be treated as Medicaid-eligible and therefore unable to claim a Marketplace credit for that month. The rule is not final; comments are due November 30 and a hearing is scheduled for December 8.

FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEER-REVIEWED EXPLORATORY CROSS-SECTIONAL HUMAN STUDY

Peripheral and cerebrospinal fluid inflammatory markers in sporadic amyotrophic lateral sclerosis: an exploratory cross-sectional association study

Neurological Sciences · online October 17, 2026 · cross-sectional study of 45 people with sporadic ALS and 30 headache controls, all under age 65. Cerebrospinal-fluid TNFR-1 was higher in the ALS group (median 1,029.5 versus 846.1 pg/mL); after adjustment, each 100-pg/mL increase was associated with higher odds of ALS (OR 1.82, 95% CI 1.26–2.63), and an exploratory same-cohort ROC analysis produced an AUC of 0.78. The finding supports further work on inflammatory mechanisms and candidate biomarkers; it does not show causation, establish a diagnostic test or identify a treatment. Limitations include a small single cohort, younger controls, no disease-control group, a threshold derived and tested in the same sample, upper age limit of 65 and unblinded laboratory work.
