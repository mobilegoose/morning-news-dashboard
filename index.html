1 · U.S. ECONOMY · OCTOBER JOBS

Employers add 165,000 jobs; unemployment edges down to 4.1%

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported stronger-than-expected October hiring, with payrolls up 165,000 and the unemployment rate little changed at 4.1%. August and September gains were revised up by a combined 84,000. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% for the month and 3.1% over 12 months, while the number working part time for economic reasons increased by 339,000 to 4.8 million. The report shows a resilient labor market, but not that every worker's purchasing power or job prospects improved.

IS-linked militants kill at least 33 as patients flee an Ebola-area hospital

Local civil-society and medical officials said Allied Democratic Forces militants attacked Cantine in North Kivu overnight, killed at least 33 people, burned more than 50 homes and forced residents to flee. 3 · MEDICARE · PRESCRIPTION DRUGS

CMS finalizes the permanent framework for drug-price negotiations beginning in 2029

CMS issued a final rule codifying how it will select, negotiate and, when warranted, renegotiate maximum fair prices for high-expenditure single-source drugs after its temporary guidance authority expires. Up to 20 Part B or Part D drugs may be selected for 2029 prices; the rule also addresses fixed-combination formulations, a temporary floor for certain small-biotech drugs and formulary treatment of selected drugs. The rule takes effect December 5, but it does not announce new negotiated prices today.

4 · CALIFORNIA · CAPITAL PUNISHMENT

Newsom moves to commute all 548 death sentences to life without parole

Gov. Gavin Newsom announced clemency for everyone sentenced to death in California, replacing death sentences with life without parole. The change is immediately within the governor's authority for 166 people, but the California Constitution requires state Supreme Court approval for the 382 people with more than one felony conviction. The announcement therefore begins, rather than completes, most of the commutations.

Associated Press via ABC News

MEDICARE & MEDICAID · COMMENT OPPORTUNITY

CMS opens a consequential CARE proposal affecting hospitals, nursing homes and Medicaid transparency

The proposal would remove selected hospital emergency- and obstetric-care training and quality requirements, relax several long-term-care reporting and assessment rules, and rescind reports showing how much Medicaid institutional payment goes to direct-care and support workers. CMS also asks for input on ombudsman transfer notices, respiratory reporting, CRNA supervision, PA/NP nursing-home visits, transplant-program certification and workforce shortages. It is not final: the public-inspection document is scheduled for November 10 publication, and comments will be due 60 days after publication. Use docket CMS-2026-3631 and address one requirement at a time with evidence about burden, safety, access or accountability.

CLEMENCY · CALIFORNIA

The death-row action is clemency, not abolition by statute or judicial ruling

Newsom is using the governor's power to substitute life without parole for death sentences; the state's death-penalty law remains on the books. The California Supreme Court must approve 382 commutations because those individuals have multiple felony convictions. Approval would authorize clemency in those cases—it would not affirm or reverse the convictions themselves.

MEDICARE · DRUG NEGOTIATION FINAL RULE

CMS codifies selection, negotiation, renegotiation and program-integrity rules for 2029 and later

The final rule turns the negotiation program's first three cycles of guidance into a durable regulatory framework. It clarifies when generic or biosimilar marketing ends negotiation eligibility, preserves Part D formulary inclusion for selected drugs, and addresses formulations that could otherwise be used to avoid a maximum fair price. Drug selection for the 2029 cycle is due by February 1, 2027; today's action establishes the process rather than beneficiary prices.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

Environmental health and one new free journal article

ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH · BREAST CANCER

Higher PFOA and PFOS levels are associated with ER-negative breast cancer—not breast cancer overall

NIH researchers compared prediagnosis blood samples from 1,609 Sister Study participants who developed breast cancer with 2,269 comparison participants. Women in the highest exposure groups had 67% higher rates of ER-negative cancer for PFOA and 93% higher rates for PFOS than those in the lowest groups; researchers found no clear association with breast cancer overall. This observational study supports further investigation but cannot prove the chemicals caused cancer, and relative differences are not an individual's absolute risk.

Official NIH release

New Free Journal Articles

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · ESOPHAGEAL CANCER · NOVEMBER 4

Spectrum-Aided Vision Enhancer shows exploratory promise for esophageal cancer—but misses too much dysplasia

Journal: DEN Open. Published online November 4, 2026. Evidence: peer-reviewed, retrospective image-based exploratory feasibility study. Three endoscopists with 3, 5 and 15 years of experience blindly reviewed 1,350 still images—450 each from white-light imaging, narrow-band imaging and the SAVE reconstruction—drawn from 158 patients. SAVE's observed sensitivity and specificity for esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma were numerically similar to the comparison methods, but sensitivity for dysplasia was only 0.39–0.62 versus 0.55–0.85 with narrow-band imaging. The finding matters as a possible lower-equipment pathway for cancer visualization, including a Compassionate Allowances cancer, but it is not a validated screening test. Major limitations are the retrospective still-image design, single exploratory dataset, only three readers, lower precancer sensitivity and no prospective multicenter validation.

OFFICIAL SUPREME COURT AUDIO · NOVEMBER 4

Guerrero v. Johnson oral argument

Texas prison director Eric Guerrero asks the Court to block death-row prisoner Dexter Johnson's successive habeas petition. Johnson relies on Atkins v. Virginia's rule barring execution of intellectually disabled people; the dispute is whether that 2002 rule was “previously unavailable” to Johnson when his first federal habeas petition was litigated after Atkins but before later decisions clarified the standard. The Court granted certiorari June 15 and heard argument November 4; no merits decision has issued. The eventual ruling will determine whether Johnson may pursue that claim and could govern other successive capital habeas petitions. The recording is useful for following how statutory finality rules interact with constitutional limits on capital punishment.
