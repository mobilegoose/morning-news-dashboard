Court adds DOGE records, an immigration terrorism bar and habeas appeals to its docket

4 · MEDICAID & CHIP

Federal matching funds end today for gender-affirming medical care for minors

A new CMS rule takes effect today, withholding federal Medicaid matching funds for specified puberty blockers, hormones and surgeries for people under 18 and CHIP enrollees under 19. States may use state-only money, and existing hormone patients receive a transition period of up to six months. The rule does not bar federally supported mental-health care and is being challenged in court.

MEDICAID & CHIP · ACTIONABLE

What today's youth gender-care funding rule does—and does not—change

The rule changes federal reimbursement, not every state's legal authority to cover care: a state can pay with state-only funds. Families with an existing hormone prescription should ask the plan and clinician promptly whether the six-month transition applies and what written coverage notice or appeal rights are available. Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning court check included

CERT GRANT · DOGE RECORDS

U.S. DOGE Service v. U.S. District Court for D.C.

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington seeks DOGE records under FOIA; the government argues DOGE was a presidential advisory body, not an agency, and says a lower court's status-related discovery intruded on executive functions. The Supreme Court granted certiorari, meaning it will review the dispute on the merits rather than leaving the lower-court process as the final word. The grant does not decide DOGE's legal status or whether records must be released; briefing and argument come next.

Official docket 25-1103Associated Press explainer

CERT GRANT · IMMIGRATION

Blanche v. Ozurumba

The government asks whether a Nigerian man's forced cooking for an armed group counts as material support to a terrorist organization under immigration law; the lower court held that the captive labor did not trigger the bar. By granting certiorari, the Court agreed to decide the statutory question, not to order a deportation today. The ruling could define how coercion and ordinary services are treated in asylum and removal cases.

Official docket 25-1343Reuters report

CERT GRANT · HABEAS APPEALS

Berry v. United States

A federal prisoner asks whether a certificate of appealability can issue when binding precedent in his circuit rejects the constitutional claim but another circuit accepts it. The Court's cert grant means it will decide the appellate-access standard; it neither grants Berry habeas relief nor decides his underlying conviction. The result may affect when prisoners can obtain appellate review of legally contested constitutional claims.

FEDERAL COURT · PUBLIC LAND

Judge orders Interior officials to testify about East Potomac tree removal

After temporarily stopping additional cutting at East Potomac Golf Links, U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes ordered federal officials to testify about how the project was authorized and reviewed. The order develops yesterday's dispute but does not decide whether the tree removal violated environmental or preservation law; the evidentiary hearing will shape the preliminary-injunction decision.

Washington Post

CERT DENIAL · DOUBLE JEOPARDY STATEMENT

Herrera-Salazar v. United States

After Jesus Herrera-Salazar served a sentence for being unlawfully ‘found in’ the United States, he was required to remain in Oklahoma on supervised release and then prosecuted again under the same statute without a new reentry. He asked the Court to review whether the second prosecution violated double jeopardy. The Court denied certiorari, so the Tenth Circuit judgment remains in place without a nationwide merits ruling. Justice Sotomayor, joined by Justice Gorsuch, agreed not to take this fact-specific case but wrote that the government's approach raised serious constitutional problems and urged lower courts to avoid the issue if it recurs.

Science, Medicine & Public Health

New evidence with limitations

LONG COVID · OBSERVATIONAL COHORT

Routine laboratory tests did not track symptom patterns or improvement in a Long COVID clinic cohort

Scientific Reports · online October 13, 2026 · single-center cohort of 564 adults across 1,452 visits. Researchers found no robust age-adjusted relationship between 89 routine laboratory measures and three symptom phenotypes, and the analytes did not predict improvement better than chance. This supports caution against using ordinary labs as standalone Long COVID biomarkers; it does not show that all biological markers fail. Referral-center selection, missing data and the restriction to routine analytes limit generalizability.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEER-REVIEWED POPULATION COHORT

Disability-related disparities in diagnosis and survival of digestive-system cancers in Northern Italy

Nature Communications · online October 10, 2026 · population-based cohort of 16,964 people diagnosed in 2018–2021. People with pre-existing mental or neuromusculoskeletal impairments were more often diagnosed after emergency presentation, and mental impairment was associated with higher one-year mortality. These are adjusted associations, not proof that disability caused the outcomes. Why it matters: the study identifies concrete access and diagnostic-timing disparities. Limits include one Italian region, administrative impairment measures and possible residual confounding.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEER-REVIEWED OBSERVATIONAL COHORT

Home-based programs and cardiac rehabilitation participation among veterans with ischemic heart disease

JAMA Network Open · online October 12, 2026 · national Veterans Health Administration cohort of 405,907 patients from 2012–2022. Participation rose from 9.6% to 17.1%; in 2022, facilities offering home-based rehabilitation had 20.0% participation versus 14.8% at center-only facilities, with an adjusted odds ratio of 1.80. The association suggests home-based access may help, but the study cannot prove causation. The cohort was 96.6% male, and facility-level confounding limits generalization.
