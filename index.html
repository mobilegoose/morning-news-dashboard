1 · Indonesia earthquake

A magnitude-7.7 quake killed at least 47 people off Flores

The shallow earthquake collapsed buildings, triggered landslides and small tsunami waves, and cut off remote villages. Authorities counted 235 aftershocks, nearly 2,000 displaced residents, 157 destroyed homes and more than 100 damaged public facilities by Saturday morning.

Why it matters The toll may rise as rescuers reach isolated communities; aftershocks and damaged roads are complicating evacuation and aid.

Associated Press

2 · Hawaii emergency

Tropical Storm Lala began lashing the Big Island under a rare hurricane warning

Lala approached with 65-mph winds and was forecast to strengthen while bringing 8–12 inches of rain, with isolated totals up to 25 inches at elevation. Shelters opened and officials warned of destructive flooding, mudslides, dangerous surf and localized wildfire risk.

Why it matters A direct hurricane landfall on the Big Island would be exceptionally rare, and vulnerable rural communities may face both access and communications problems.

4 · Abortion access

A federal judge blocked Idaho from prosecuting health-preserving abortions

Judge B. Lynn Winmill ruled that Idaho cannot punish doctors for performing abortions needed to prevent serious health harm or self-harm, even when death is not imminent. The injunction applies to the state’s near-total bans; Idaho says it will appeal.

Why it matters The decision protects a broader health exception than Idaho had allowed and becomes an important post-Dobbs test of constitutional limits on state abortion bans.

5 · Immigration

A judge cleared DHS to end Temporary Protected Status for nearly 1,100 Somalis

U.S. District Judge Allison Burroughs allowed the administration’s termination to proceed after earlier temporarily blocking it. TPS had protected eligible Somalis from deportation and authorized them to work; immigrant-rights groups had challenged the decision as discriminatory and disconnected from country conditions.

Why it matters The ruling removes a major legal shield for a small but vulnerable group and follows a Supreme Court decision that narrowed review of similar TPS terminations.

New Free Journal Articles · Qualitative human study

A small UK study documented the lived impact of post-SSRI sexual dysfunction

International Journal of Qualitative Studies on Health and Well-being · online August 13, 2026. Researchers conducted semi-structured interviews with 10 people and identified themes of professional dismissal, altered identity, emotional numbness and relationship loss. The study describes experiences; it does not estimate prevalence or test treatment.

Why it matters The findings highlight functional and mental-health harms that clinicians may miss. Limitations: the very small, mostly White British, self-selected sample—recruited with patient-group support—cannot represent all affected people.

New Free Journal Articles · Retrospective cohort plus cell experiments

A molecular signal was associated with poor healing after osteoporotic femoral-neck fracture

Cytotechnology · online August 13, 2026. Among 160 patients—71 with poor healing and 89 with normal healing—higher serum miR-99b-3p was associated with poor healing and lower BMP8A; an exploratory model produced an AUC of 0.859. Cell experiments suggested a possible pathway, but not a ready clinical treatment.

Why it matters The signal could support future biomarker or mechanism work in a function-limiting injury. Limitations: single retrospective cohort, no external validation, and laboratory findings cannot establish clinical benefit or causation.
