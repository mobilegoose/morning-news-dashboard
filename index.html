1 · FEDERAL COURT · NATIONAL GUARD

Ninth Circuit keeps California National Guard under state control

A divided Ninth Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction blocking the president from deploying California National Guard members in Los Angeles and ordering their return to Governor Gavin Newsom. The panel held that the June 2025 federalization did not satisfy either statutory trigger the administration invoked and likely violated separation-of-powers and Tenth Amendment limits. This is appellate review of interim relief, not a final trial judgment; the government can seek rehearing or Supreme Court review.

2 · IMMIGRATION DETENTION · DUE PROCESS

Full Fifth Circuit rejects a constitutional right to immigration bond hearings

In consolidated habeas cases brought by three longtime U.S. residents from Mexico and Honduras, the en banc Fifth Circuit ruled 10–7 that people detained under the immigration statute at issue have no due-process right to an individualized bond hearing beyond the procedures Congress supplied. The ruling reverses release and hearing orders and governs Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi. A separate Supreme Court case will address the statute, not this constitutional holding.

Justice Department asks a court to restrict certification of Alaska's Senate result

ALVARADO / SOSNAVA RODRIGUEZ · EN BANC REVERSAL

Fifth Circuit removes a constitutional route to bond hearings in three states

The petitioners—three people detained after living in the United States for at least 14 years—argued that prolonged detention without an individualized chance to show they were neither dangerous nor a flight risk violated due process. The full court reversed the panel and district-court relief, holding that the Constitution does not require a hearing beyond the statute's process. That ends these habeas remedies in the Fifth Circuit unless further review changes the result; it does not itself decide the separate statutory question now before the Supreme Court.

Fifth Circuit decisionTexas Tribune

California challenged the president's federalization of 4,000 state Guard members to protect federal personnel and property during immigration-enforcement protests. The Ninth Circuit affirmed preliminary relief after concluding that neither claimed statutory emergency existed and that the order likely violated constitutional limits. The Guard remains under California control while the litigation proceeds; the administration may seek rehearing or Supreme Court review.

ACA MARKETPLACES · PROPOSED RULE

CMS proposes pre-verification before people receive 2028 Marketplace subsidies

The proposal would require consumers seeking advance premium tax credits or income-based cost-sharing reductions to verify specified eligibility facts before financial assistance begins. Existing enrollees would have to return annually and pass re-verification to retain automatic subsidies; otherwise coverage could renew without financial help. NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · CANCER · ONLINE OCTOBER 21

Lymph-node regression may improve post-treatment risk estimates in esophageal cancer

MedComm · peer-reviewed multicenter retrospective cohort. Researchers studied 965 surgical patients who received PD-1-based neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy at three Chinese tertiary cancer centers and evaluated 28,882 lymph nodes. A lymph-node regression grade independently tracked disease-free and overall survival and improved a model for two-year disease-free survival in external validation. Why it matters: the pathology score may refine postoperative risk estimates after modern immunotherapy. Important limits: this was an observational surgical cohort from three centers, the score is available only after resection, and prospective studies must show that using it improves care; the study demonstrates prognostic association, not treatment benefit.
