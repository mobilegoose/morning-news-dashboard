Ranked by broad public importance

1 · UKRAINE · ENERGY INFRASTRUCTURE

Russian missiles and drones hit 28 substations as more than half a million people lose essential services

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said an overnight Russian attack struck 28 substations and other critical sites, killing a 21-year-old woman and leaving more than 500,000 people without electricity, heat or water. He said Ukraine received none of the partner intelligence warnings it previously relied on and that continuing Starlink speed restrictions are hampering drone operations. The renewed campaign matters as winter approaches and after Washington waived Russia sanctions and agreed to buy Russian diesel.

Associated Press

2 · YEMEN · BALLISTIC-MISSILE ATTACK

Houthi missiles kill at least 53 soldiers at a government training camp

Houthi forces said they targeted a military training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province; local authorities reported at least 53 soldiers killed and more than 200 recruits injured. The strike is among the deadliest since fighting intensified again this summer and adds pressure around the Bab el-Mandeb shipping corridor. Casualty figures are preliminary and come from authorities in a divided conflict zone.

Associated Press

3 · CONGO · EBOLA EMERGENCY

The world's fastest-growing Ebola outbreak reaches an eighth Congolese province

Congo's Health Ministry confirmed two cases, including one death, in Nord-Ubangi province on the Central African Republic border. The Bundibugyo-virus outbreak has produced at least 9,400 cases and more than 4,500 deaths since May, according to authorities, and no approved vaccine or treatment exists for this strain. Geographic spread is continuing even where case trends have eased in some pockets, raising cross-border and access concerns.

Associated Press

4 · U.S. SUPREME COURT · INDEPENDENT AGENCIES

Justices decline Gwynne Wilcox's appeal over her removal from the NLRB

Former National Labor Relations Board member Gwynne Wilcox asked the Court to review whether President Trump could fire her despite a federal law allowing removal only for specified cause. The justices denied certiorari without explanation, so they did not decide the merits; the D.C. Circuit judgment validating her removal remains in place. The denial follows the Court's June ruling allowing removal of an FTC commissioner and leaves the NLRB with less insulation from presidential control.

Champi strengthens to Category 5 after passing Guam; Trudy weakens off Mexico

Champi reached 160 mph over the Philippine Sea and was moving away from land after passing near Guam, where federal emergency aid is now authorized and damage assessment continues. President Trump endorsed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for reelection eight days before Israel votes, ending months of uncertainty as the two leaders diverged over regional strategy. Oil remains highly volatile and security claims are developing, so the incident should be treated as an operational risk update rather than a settled attribution.

U.S. MIDTERMS · NEW REPRESENTATIVE POLL

AP-NORC survey finds AAPI adults moving away from Trump and Republicans

A representative September survey of 1,023 Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander adults found about one-quarter approved of President Trump's job performance and about two in ten approved of his handling of the economy. Democrats held advantages on the economy, health care and immigration, although nearly half viewed the Democratic Party unfavorably. This is a poll release, not an election result; sampling uncertainty and the time before November 3 matter.

Supreme Court & Legal

Final same-morning Supreme Court check included

WILCOX v. TRUMP · CERTIORARI DENIED

Wilcox's NLRB removal challenge ends without a new merits ruling

Gwynne Wilcox argued that the National Labor Relations Act protected her from being removed before her term ended without neglect or malfeasance; the administration argued the Constitution gives the president broader removal power. Today's denial of certiorari means the Supreme Court will not hear her appeal and expresses no view through an opinion. The D.C. Circuit's December 2025 ruling against her stands, making presidential control of the NLRB the immediate practical result while other independent-agency disputes continue.

Policy & Benefits

Useful recent federal actions

RECENT FALLBACK · MEDICARE ADVANTAGE & PART D · OCTOBER 16

CMS proposes verified provider directories, PBM disclosures and a $0 generic copay for some dual eligibles

CMS proposed requiring Medicare Advantage plans to verify provider directories at least every 90 days, publish accuracy scores and cap a member's bill at in-network cost sharing when an inaccurate listing causes out-of-network care. The rule would also add Part D pharmacy-benefit-manager compensation disclosures and reduce the generic-drug copay from $1 to $0 in 2028 for full-benefit Medicare-Medicaid dual eligibles at or below 100% of the federal poverty level. It is proposed, not final; the public-comment period runs 60 days after Federal Register publication.

Official CMS fact sheetOfficial CMS release

RECENT FALLBACK · EMPLOYMENT & IMMIGRATION · OCTOBER 16

Cloudera accepts a two-year PERM debarment and $2 million settlement package

DOJ and Labor resolved allegations that Cloudera excluded qualified U.S. workers from recruitment tied to permanent labor certification. The company will be barred from PERM for two years, fund $1.4 million in back pay, pay $600,000 in civil penalties and adopt monitoring and compliance measures. Cloudera denied intentional discrimination in the settlement, so the resolution imposes obligations without a trial finding on intent.

Official DOJ and Labor releaseSettlement agreement

RECENT FALLBACK · CDC BORDER HEALTH ORDER

CDC's Ebola-related entry suspension remains in force through November 10

A CDC order effective October 11 continues a temporary suspension covering certain people who were recently in countries experiencing the Ebola outbreak; lawful permanent residents are no longer categorically exempt under the governing regulation. The order runs through 4:59 p.m. Eastern November 10 unless amended or rescinded, and comments are due October 30. Exceptions and DHS-CDC public-health assessment procedures still apply, so affected travelers should use the official order rather than generalized summaries.
