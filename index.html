1 · SPAIN · TRANSPORT DISASTER

At least 68 people die in Spain's worst rail disaster in years

Two high-speed trains collided near Urda, south of Madrid, on Wednesday, killing at least 68 people and leaving 58 hospitalized. Investigators are examining why both trains were routed onto the same track; authorities have not established a cause. The crash is Europe's deadliest rail accident since 2013 and has disrupted the Madrid–Seville corridor.

Russian drone strike kills 13 at a crowded Dnipro rail station

A Russian drone hit a passenger platform in Dnipro on Thursday morning, killing 13 people and injuring at least 10, according to Ukrainian officials. The station and nearby trains were damaged. The toll makes the strike one of the deadliest recent attacks on Ukraine's rail network, a system civilians and military logistics both rely on.

4 · ISRAEL · ELECTION

Final projections still leave Israel without a governing majority

With final official results due Thursday night, projections put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc at 53 seats, the main opposition at 48 and Arab-led parties at 19—none near the 61 needed to govern. Netanyahu alleged theft without evidence, while the election committee separately requested an investigation into unspecified foreign interference. Coalition negotiations, not the initial count, will determine the next government.

5 · VOTING RIGHTS · FEDERAL COURT

Judge rejects DOJ bid to segregate up to 20,000 California ballots

U.S. District Judge David O. Carter denied the Justice Department's emergency request to halt or separate ballots linked to a federal list of people it says may be noncitizens. He found the request likely conflicted with the National Voter Registration Act's 90-day limit on systematic voter removals and could disenfranchise eligible voters. DOJ said it will appeal; the ruling does not decide the underlying voter-eligibility claims.

RURAL HOUSING · USDA LOANS

USDA will allow guaranteed loans for homes with income-producing ADUs

A Rural Housing Service final rule published today permits the Single Family Housing Guaranteed Loan Program to finance eligible homes with income-producing accessory dwelling units and clarifies eligibility for residential features used by home-based operations. The change is effective 60 days after publication. It can expand financing options for rural borrowers, but applicants still must meet program, appraisal and property requirements.

Travel screening and elder-fraud enforcement

INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL · EBOLA

CDC broadens the November 3 Ebola travel attestation to airline crews

CDC amended its order so passengers, cargo crews and deadheading crew members on U.S.-bound international flights must attest that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo during the prior 21 days. The requirement begins November 3. Travelers who have been in the DRC should expect enhanced screening and routing; CDC says the current U.S. risk remains very low.

ANIMAL & PUBLIC HEALTH · PARASITE

USDA confirms Arizona's first New World screwworm case

USDA confirmed the parasite in a dog in Cochise County and established a 20-kilometer infested zone with animal-movement controls, surveillance, trapping and sterile-fly releases. Screwworm larvae infest living tissue and can threaten pets, livestock and occasionally people; owners in the area should promptly report suspicious wounds or larvae.

New Free Journal Articles

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEDIATRIC LEUKEMIA · OCTOBER 27

Post-transplant kinase inhibitors show promise—but evidence is mostly small and observational

Cancer Chemotherapy and Pharmacology published a peer-reviewed systematic review of 16 studies covering 94 relevant patients ages 3–18. Selected children with mutation-driven acute leukemia sometimes achieved or maintained molecular remission during post-transplant tyrosine-kinase inhibitor therapy, while relapse, resistance and toxicities were also reported. The review is useful for rare pediatric cancer and transplant follow-up, but it does not establish treatment efficacy: 13 included reports were case reports or case series, only three were observational studies, dosing and duration varied, and long-term safety is unclear. This is evidence synthesis for specialist care—not a newly available treatment. Published online October 27, 2026.
