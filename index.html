Tuesday, July 28, 2026 Updated after the final Supreme Court check

Today’s edition leads with the damaging Kyushu earthquake, a new Supreme Court emergency voting dispute, White House meetings on the Iran and Ukraine wars, expanding wildfire evacuations, and the full effect of new U.S. tariffs.

1. Top 5 Things to Know Today

Ranked by public importance
  1. A magnitude-7.1 earthquake struck Japan’s Kyushu region. The quake damaged roads, buildings and utilities, partially collapsed a shopping center, injured dozens and left people missing. A tsunami advisory was lifted, but rescues and aftershock warnings continued. AP, July 28 · Reuters, July 28 — link may be limited
  2. The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to let it enforce a mail-voting executive order. The order directs USCIS and SSA to provide citizenship lists and tells USPS to deliver mail ballots only to people on those lists. A federal judge blocked it in 23 states and D.C.; the government now seeks an emergency stay while litigation continues. AP, July 28 · Official application
  3. Trump hosted Zelenskyy and Netanyahu as two wars reached pivotal diplomatic moments. Zelenskyy sought air-defense cooperation; Netanyahu’s first White House meeting with Trump since the Iran war began focused on Iran, Lebanon and regional normalization. The meetings produced no announced settlement by publication time. AP live report, July 28 · Reuters, July 28 — link may be limited
  4. France ordered 4,000 more wildfire evacuations as renewed heat threatened containment. About 220,000 people have been displaced in France’s Gironde region and roughly 330,000 across France and Spain; the French blaze has burned an area about four times the size of Paris. AP, July 28
  5. The in-transit grace period ended for new U.S. tariffs covering nearly all imports. The 10% and 12.5% duties apply to goods from 60 trading partners under Section 301, with exemptions for products including oil, gas, fertilizer and some foods. Reuters details and July 28 effective date — link may be limited

2. Most Useful Development Today

What the voting case means now

Supreme Court emergency docket

The challenged voter-list and mail-ballot rules remain blocked in the plaintiff states

In Trump v. California, the administration is asking the Supreme Court to pause a Massachusetts judge’s injunction while appeals continue. The underlying dispute is whether the president may direct federal agencies to build a citizenship-based voter list and condition postal delivery of ballots, or whether those election rules belong to states and Congress.

Procedural statusThe Court has not granted a stay or decided the order’s legality. Responses are due August 3. Unless the justices change the status quo, the injunction remains in force for the 23 plaintiff states and Washington, D.C.

Concrete developments

Japan · Disaster response

Rescuers searched a collapsed mall after the Kyushu quake

Officials reported around 90 injuries, major transport disruption, damaged roads and blackouts. Nearby nuclear plants reported no abnormality, and the tsunami advisory was lifted; the continuing risks are trapped people, heat exposure and strong aftershocks. AP, July 28

Zelenskyy put anti-ballistic defense and strategic cooperation at the top of his request. Netanyahu’s talks centered on Iran’s nuclear activity, Lebanon and the Abraham Accords amid visible differences with Trump over how to end the Iran war. AP live report, July 28

France moved another 4,000 people from Atlantic-coast tourist sites. The Gironde fire remained volatile despite a fragile hold, while Spain battled its largest recorded blaze and additional regional fires. AP, July 28

The new duties took effect July 24, but shipments already moving received a grace period through 12:01 a.m. ET today. The policy covers 99.4% of imports by value, though numerous products are exempt. Reuters — link may be limited

4. Supreme Court & Legal

Official Court check completed

Emergency application · Voting

Trump v. California: government asks Court to lift an injunction

California, 22 other states and Washington, D.C., argue that the president exceeded his power by directing USCIS and SSA to create citizenship lists and USPS to restrict mail-ballot delivery. The administration says the order is policy guidance and the challenge came too early. A district judge entered an injunction and the First Circuit declined to stay it; the Solicitor General now asks the Supreme Court for a stay, which would temporarily lift the injunction while litigation proceeds. The Court has only requested responses, due August 3, so no merits ruling or emergency relief has been granted. Official application and appendix · AP explainer

No new merits opinion or order dated July 28

At the final post-10:30 a.m. ET check, the Court’s official pages showed no new merits opinion or order dated July 28. The newest development is the government’s emergency stay application in Trump v. California, filed July 27; the justices have not ruled on it. Official slip opinions · Official orders

5. Policy & Benefits

Immigration, voting and Social Security

Immigration · Child welfare data

ICE made more than 12,000 arrests after tips from the migrant-child agency

Reuters reported that the Office of Refugee Resettlement sent more than 460,000 leads to ICE from January 2025 through mid-2026, resulting in arrests of children, sponsors and relatives. DHS says the data helps identify unsafe or unvetted sponsors; former ORR officials and advocates say using reunification data for enforcement deters families and prolongs child detention. Reuters investigation, July 28 — link may be limited

Recent useful fallback · SSDI appeals

SSA added online disability forms, hearing-status details and digital notices

Claimants can now submit Forms HA-4631, HA-4632 and HA-4633 through my Social Security, track hearing and Appeals Council milestones, and access proffer letters plus 26 additional notices. Paper notices continue. Official SSA release, July 21

Recent useful fallback · SSI administration

SSA reported wider payroll and bank-data tools for SSI accuracy

The agency’s 2026 SSI report says it fully implemented the Payroll Information Exchange and expanded Access to Financial Institution use, while increasing non-medical redeterminations. These are administration and verification changes, not a new benefit increase. Official SSA release, July 24

6. Science, Medicine & Public Health

Outbreak and litigation updates

Ebola · Uganda

Uganda declared its latest Ebola outbreak over

The outbreak infected 20 people and killed two. Fifteen cases originated in neighboring Congo. The declaration is an important containment milestone, although Reuters noted the last patient’s June 22 discharge was slightly short of the WHO’s standard 42-day interval. Reuters, July 28 — link may be limited

Ebola · Democratic Republic of Congo

Congo’s outbreak continued to grow as surveillance teams strained

Government figures released Sunday counted 3,200 infections and 1,405 deaths. WHO has said the true scale could be two to four times the reported total, making Uganda’s containment success a regional exception rather than an end to the wider emergency. Reuters field report, July 28 — link may be limited

Cancer litigation

J&J agreed to a proposed $5.5 billion talc settlement

The deal could resolve tens of thousands of ovarian-cancer claims, but it is not final: 95% of covered claimants must accept it. J&J continues to deny that its talc products caused cancer, and the settlement is not a scientific finding on causation. Reuters, July 27 — link may be limited

7. AI Today

Policy and security

Open weights · Policy

Anthropic rejected a blanket ban on open-weight models

CEO Dario Amodei said open models without dangerous capabilities are a public good and that Anthropic has never advocated banning them. He argued for targeted controls on advanced chips, large-scale model replication and frontier-risk testing instead. This is a policy position, not a product release or regulation. Anthropic’s original post, July 27

AI security · Open tools

Nvidia and partners launched the Open Secure AI Alliance

The 37-member group plans to share open security tools for testing, auditing and defending AI systems. Founders include Nvidia, Microsoft, IBM, Cisco, Adobe, the Linux Foundation and others; OpenAI, Google and Anthropic were not listed. Nvidia announcement, July 27

8. Other High-Value Recent Developments

Worth tracking

U.S. foreign assistance

Administration notified Congress of $175.8 million for undersea cables

The proposed Caribbean and Central American funding would replace aging telecommunications cables and reduce openings for Chinese infrastructure investment. It marks a partial restoration of counter-China programs paused during last year’s aid cuts. AP, July 28

Public opinion · Iran war

Only one in three Americans supported the Iran war in a new poll

The Reuters/Ipsos survey of 1,246 adults found 69% said Trump had not clearly explained U.S. objectives. Support has remained below 40% for five months; the poll’s margin of error was about three percentage points. Reuters/Ipsos, July 27 — link may be limited

U.S. economy

Consumer confidence fell as gas and food costs weighed on households

The Conference Board index slipped to 90.8 from 92.2 in June, and the present-situation measure declined for a third month. The survey captures sentiment, not a direct measure of spending or recession. AP, July 28
