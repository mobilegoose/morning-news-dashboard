1 · PUBLIC HEALTH · EBOLA

Ebola outbreak reaches four countries beyond Congo; Nigeria confirms an Abuja case

The fast-moving Bundibugyo-virus outbreak has caused about 9,500 confirmed infections and at least 4,600 deaths in Congo and has now reached four other countries, with active transmission reported in Kenya and Nigeria. Nigeria is tracing 44 contacts of a woman who arrived through Uganda and became ill in Abuja. No approved vaccine or treatment exists for this strain, making surveillance, isolation and contact tracing the main controls.

Russia frees American Stephen Hubbard as Witkoff meets Putin about restarting talks

Russia released the 74-year-old retired teacher after more than four years in custody and a closed-trial conviction for allegedly fighting for Ukraine. President Trump said no prisoner exchange was required. The release came during envoy Steve Witkoff's St. Petersburg meeting with Vladimir Putin, where the United States urged renewed three-way talks with Ukraine; no ceasefire or settlement was announced.

3 · CLIMATE · U.N. EMISSIONS GAP REPORT

U.N. says current policies point to about 2.8°C of warming this century

UNEP's 2026 Emissions Gap Report says the outlook worsened from last year and that greenhouse-gas emissions must fall 18% by 2030 and 45% by 2035 from 2025 levels to minimize the overshoot of 1.5°C. The report attributes the weaker projection partly to the U.S. withdrawal from the Paris Agreement and revised historical-emissions estimates. These are modeled pathways, not a forecast of a single certain outcome.

Official UNEP releaseAssociated Press

4 · FDA · CHRONIC HEPATITIS B

FDA approves the first antisense treatment for selected adults with chronic hepatitis B

Hibsago (bepirovirsen) is approved with existing antiviral therapy for certain adults without cirrhosis whose virus is already suppressed. Across two placebo-controlled trials, 24% of treated participants met criteria to stop their existing medicine, and 19% still had viral control with no detectable surface antigen 24 weeks after stopping all therapy. The drug carries boxed warnings for serious liver reactions and low platelets and requires laboratory monitoring.

Official FDA approval

5 · SUPREME COURT · CAPITAL PUNISHMENT

Court denies William Reaves' final stay request; Florida carries out its 18th execution of 2026

Reaves asked the justices to halt his execution and review challenges involving Florida's lethal-injection procedure and the treatment of evidence about combat-related PTSD; he had been convicted at a 1992 retrial of murdering Indian River County deputy Richard Raczkoski in 1986. The Court denied both the stay and certiorari without explanation, leaving the judgment in place and ending federal review without creating precedent. Florida executed Reaves at 6:12 p.m. Tuesday.

Official October 20 orderAssociated Press

U.K. approves the Jackdaw gas field after a court-required climate review

Energy Minister Miatta Fahnbulleh approved the project east of Aberdeen after an earlier approval was ruled unlawful for failing to assess emissions from burning the gas. LIBYA–TURKEY · MEDITERRANEAN BOUNDARIES

Eastern Libya's parliament reverses course and ratifies the 2019 maritime agreement with Turkey

The House of Representatives unanimously approved the boundary memorandum and repealed its 2020 rejection and break in political ties. The agreement asserts continental-shelf and exclusive-economic-zone boundaries in the energy-rich eastern Mediterranean. Greece says the deal has no legal effect, while Egypt, Cyprus and Israel have also objected, so the vote revives a regional sovereignty dispute rather than settling it.

REAVES v. FLORIDA · STAY AND CERTIORARI DENIED

Unexplained denial leaves Reaves' sentence in force; Florida executes him hours later

William Reaves asked the Supreme Court to stay his execution and hear his challenge after Florida courts rejected claims involving the state's three-drug protocol, newly presented evidence and the consideration of his Vietnam-related PTSD. The justices denied emergency relief and certiorari without explanation. A stay denial allows the execution to proceed; a certiorari denial ends this request for review but does not endorse the lower courts' reasoning. Florida carried out the sentence Tuesday evening, so there is no further proceeding for Reaves; the order sets no binding rule for other cases.

DISABILITY DISCRIMINATION · EEOC LAWSUIT

EEOC sues Solera over treatment and firing of a blind employee

The agency alleges Solera transferred a longtime remote employee with degenerative eye disease into sales, limited him to one product, withheld leads and excluded him from meetings and training before citing performance to fire him. EEOC says the conduct violated the Americans with Disabilities Act and filed in federal court after conciliation failed. These are allegations, but the case squarely tests whether equal access to ordinary job resources is part of the employer's ADA duty.

Official EEOC release

SOCIAL SECURITY · DATA INTEGRITY

SSA reports a large cleanup of death and citizenship fields in its SSN database

SSA says it added 17 million previously missing dates of death, filled 25 million records with missing citizenship information using DHS data and is correcting 8.5 million more records with inaccurate citizenship status. Because federal and state agencies use these records for identity and program checks, the changes could affect eligibility verification and fraud controls. The release describes an agency data project, not a change to benefit formulas or a notice that any individual record changed.

Official SSA release

PRESCRIPTION-DRUG SOFTWARE · FINAL FDA GUIDANCE

FDA finalizes when medicine companion apps require agency review or label disclosure

The guidance says software tied to a device—such as an inhaler app—generally should appear on the drug label. Standalone trackers, reminders or coaching apps should appear there when essential for safe and effective use or when they deliver a meaningful clinical benefit; other sponsor-distributed content is generally treated as promotional labeling. Guidance states the agency's current approach and does not itself approve an app.

Official FDA announcement

FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · PEDIATRIC CANCER · OCTOBER 20

Dynamic Proton arc therapy for paediatric parameningeal rhabdomyosarcoma

Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics · Peer-reviewed treatment-planning study · Online October 20, 2026. Researchers generated VMAT, intensity-modulated proton and proton-arc plans for 13 children. Both proton approaches spared organs at risk better than photon VMAT; arc therapy and standard proton therapy were similar, while LET optimization increased target LET without materially changing dose. Why it matters: it tests ways to reduce treatment injury around sensitive head structures in a serious childhood cancer. Limitation: this was a small retrospective simulation, not a clinical trial, so it shows dosimetric potential—not better survival, function or side-effect outcomes.

FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · MULTIPLE SYSTEM ATROPHY · OCTOBER 19

Automated Magnetic Resonance Volumetry to Measure Multiple System Atrophy Disease Progression in Clinical Trials

Movement Disorders · Peer-reviewed longitudinal secondary cohort analysis · Online October 19, 2026. The study analyzed one-year MRI data from 129 people with multiple system atrophy in the placebo arm of M-STAR (76 cerebellar-type, 53 parkinsonian-type) and compared them with 184 healthy controls. A combined cerebellum–pons–putamen measure changed more sensitively than clinical scales and was estimated to require 55 participants per arm to detect a 30% treatment effect. Why it matters: MSA is on SSA's Compassionate Allowances list, and a stronger trial endpoint could make therapy studies more efficient. Limitation: this is a research biomarker from a selected cohort, not a treatment, and it is not validated here for individual diagnosis or routine care.
