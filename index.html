1 · IRAN WAR · U.S. FORCES IN BAHRAIN

Iran launches a third recent attack on U.S. targets in Bahrain

Iran fired drones at a U.S. radar facility in Bahrain today, ending a roughly two-month lull with a third attack on American military targets there in recent days. Bahrain said its defenses intercepted the drones; the U.S. military reported no injuries or equipment damage. The attack matters because Bahrain hosts the U.S. Navy's 5th Fleet and the renewed strikes could further complicate stalled diplomacy and already-constrained Gulf shipping.

2 · UKRAINE · KERCH BRIDGE

Ukraine says its drones and missiles severed the road link across the Kerch Bridge

Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia with occupied Crimea after a weeks-long operation against surrounding defenses. Ukraine's General Staff said a road arch and span collapsed, while Russia acknowledged damage and halted road traffic; rail traffic later resumed. The Associated Press could not independently verify Ukraine's imagery. The bridge is a major military-supply and civilian route, so losing the road span is strategically and symbolically significant even if repairs follow.

Associated Press

3 · IMMIGRATION · FINAL FEDERAL JUDGMENT

Federal judge vacates DHS's fixed-term rule for international students and exchange visitors

U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV granted the challengers summary judgment and vacated DHS's rule replacing the long-standing duration-of-status system for F-1 students, J-1 exchange visitors and foreign-media representatives with fixed admission periods. The court held the rule arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act. This is a final merits judgment—not the earlier temporary injunction—so the prior framework remains controlling unless the ruling is stayed or reversed on appeal.

4 · MEDICARE & MEDICAID · PROPOSED RULE

CMS proposes sweeping fraud-control changes across federal health programs

CMS issued its CRUSH proposed rule, estimating nearly $90 billion in ten-year savings. The proposal would expand provider-ownership disclosures, enrollment denial and revocation authority, Medicare Advantage and Part D fraud reporting, Medicaid overpayment recovery, and Marketplace broker restrictions. These provisions are not yet final; public comments are due January 19, 2027.

5 · CANCER TREATMENT · FDA APPROVAL

FDA approves giredestrant plus everolimus for a defined metastatic breast-cancer group

FDA approved giredestrant with everolimus for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer after specified endocrine therapy, and authorized a companion liquid-biopsy test. In the 207-patient ESR1-mutated subgroup of the randomized evERA trial, median progression-free survival was 10 months versus 5.5 months with physician-chosen endocrine therapy plus everolimus; overall-survival data remain immature.

FINAL JUDGMENT · INTERNATIONAL STUDENTS

Presidents' Alliance v. DHS ends the fixed-period visa rule at the district-court level

A coalition of higher-education and labor groups challenged DHS's rule that would have replaced duration-of-status admission with fixed periods and new extension requirements. Judge Saylor granted the plaintiffs summary judgment and vacated the entire rule under 5 U.S.C. § 706, finding DHS inadequately considered existing enforcement tools and imposed restrictions outside its reasoned justification. The government may appeal and seek a stay, but an appeal alone would not revive the rule.

Medicare, Medicaid, housing and healthcare access

PROPOSED RULE · MEDICARE, MEDICAID & MARKETPLACE

CRUSH would expand screening, reporting, recovery and broker-enforcement tools

Among the proposal's practical provisions: CMS could deny or revoke Medicare enrollment when owners cannot be verified or create undue fraud risk; Medicare Advantage and Part D plans would report suspected or substantiated fraud; states would strengthen Medicaid provider data and recovery processes; and CMS could suspend Marketplace brokers over credible allegations of consumer harm. CMS's $90 billion figure is an estimate for a proposal, not realized savings.

A cancer approval and one new free journal article

FDA APPROVAL · ESR1-MUTATED BREAST CANCER

Giredestrant combination nearly doubles median progression-free survival in the approval population

The approval covers a biomarker-defined group after recurrence or progression on endocrine-based therapy. In the randomized open-label evERA trial, the ESR1-mutated subgroup had a progression-free-survival hazard ratio of 0.38; objective response was 27% versus 14%. Common adverse reactions included stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, pneumonitis and nausea. Longer follow-up is needed because overall survival was not mature.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · MEASLES IN PREGNANCY · NOVEMBER 16

Preliminary U.S. surveillance report finds serious maternal and infant outcomes with measles

NEJM Evidence published a preliminary descriptive public-health surveillance analysis of 112 confirmed measles cases in pregnant women across 16 U.S. jurisdictions from January 2025 through September 28, 2026. Among 21 pregnancies with outcomes within 14 days of rash onset, 19 ended in live births, eight were preterm and eight infants required intensive care; two pregnancies ended in loss, and 13 congenital-measles cases were reported. Ninety-eight percent of the women were unvaccinated or had unknown vaccination status. Why it matters: the report documents preventable maternal, obstetric and neonatal risk during the current resurgence, but it does not establish comparative risk or causation. Major limitations include preliminary and incomplete outcome reporting, missing longer follow-up, a small outcome subset and no uninfected comparison group. Published online November 16, 2026.
