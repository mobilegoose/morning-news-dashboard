New Free Journal Articles · Human observational study

Merkel cell carcinoma progression in immunosuppressed patients

Acta Dermato-Venereologica · online August 12, 2026. This peer-reviewed, retrospective single-center cohort identified 400 people with Merkel cell carcinoma; 241 had complete follow-up and 81 (33.6%) progressed. Immunosuppression was associated with roughly twice the progression risk in unadjusted analysis, but the overall adjusted estimate was uncertain and did not meet the conventional significance threshold (odds ratio 2.22; p=.06). The adjusted association with soft-tissue metastasis was stronger (OR 3.55; p=.004).

Why it matters Merkel cell carcinoma is aggressive and appears on the Compassionate Allowances list; the findings support closer risk assessment for immunosuppressed patients. Limitation: this was a retrospective single-center association, not proof that immunosuppression causes progression; 159 of 400 identified patients lacked complete follow-up, and some clinical variables were subjective.
