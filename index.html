1 · ELECTION LAW · ALASKA

Federal judge dismisses DOJ bid to conditionally block Alaska Senate certification

U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed the Justice Department’s late-filed case seeking to stop certification if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses and votes for another candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan could affect the result. She found no workable way to investigate voter intent while preserving ballot secrecy and held that the federal government lacked standing. DOJ filed notice of appeal, so the ruling ends the district-court request but not the appellate fight.

2 · IRAN WAR · DIPLOMACY

Iran offers a two-day route to reopen Hormuz and resume U.S. nuclear talks

Iran said it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks within two days if Washington lifts its blockade of Iranian ports and oil sanctions and agrees to a ceasefire covering Lebanon. Ships would use a route agreed with Oman under Iranian supervision. The proposal is concrete but conditional: the United States has not accepted it, so it is not a ceasefire or reopened shipping lane.

Associated Press

3 · UKRAINE AND RUSSIA · CYBERSECURITY

Emergency-alert app hacks send false nuclear and chemical warnings

Authorities in both countries said hackers compromised emergency-alert apps late Sunday. Ukraine’s app sent false radiation, chemical-threat and street-fighting messages; Russia said false alerts went to 33 regions. The apps were suspended or repaired, while other warning channels remained available. Attribution is disputed, but the compromise itself shows how public-safety systems can be used to spread panic during war.

Associated PressReadable AP companion

4 · YEMEN · HUMANITARIAN CRISIS

More than 300,000 people have fled renewed fighting in Yemen

New International Organization for Migration data put displacement above 300,000 people, almost all in the past two months, as fighting between Houthi and government forces intensifies. Aden alone has received more than 56,000 displaced people. IOM says families urgently need shelter, food, clean water and health care, making this a major new humanitarian threshold rather than another battlefield forecast.

5 · HUNTINGTON’S DISEASE · FDA REVIEW

FDA accepts the first Huntington’s gene-therapy application for review

uniQure said FDA accepted its biologics-license application for ifezuntirgene inilparvovec, also called AMT-130, and set a May 1, 2027 decision date. Acceptance means the agency will review the submission; it is not approval and does not establish that the investigational one-time gene therapy is safe or effective. The application follows early-phase studies in people with early Huntington’s disease.

Final same-morning Supreme Court check included

ELECTION ADMINISTRATION · ALASKA

Dismissal protects ballot secrecy while DOJ appeals

The government argued that a namesake candidate could confuse voters and asked for a conditional certification block and investigation. Judge Gleason ruled the requested remedy infeasible after voting had begun, found no plan that preserved secret ballots and cited states’ constitutional election authority. A dismissal is a trial-court judgment, not a nationwide merits ruling; the Ninth Circuit can review the standing and remedy questions on appeal.

Associated Press

NAACP asks court to bar federal law-enforcement personnel from poll-monitor roles

In an overnight filing, the NAACP sought emergency limits on staff from the Bureau of Prisons, DEA and ATF serving as Election Day monitors, arguing their presence could intimidate voters and workers. DOJ says monitors will be unarmed, will not exercise arrest authority and are trained not to interfere. The request is pending; it is not an order against monitors.

CIVIL RIGHTS · ELECTION MONITORS

Court asked to decide whether federal enforcement personnel may observe polling places

The NAACP’s emergency motion targets law-enforcement employees assigned as civilian monitors, not the traditional use of Civil Rights Division lawyers to observe compliance with voting-rights law. DOJ’s own declaration says FBI and ICE personnel will not serve and that participating monitors cannot carry firearms or act as agents. New Free Journal Articles

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · BLADDER CANCER · NOVEMBER 1

Nectin-4 and IRF6 expression track less aggressive bladder-cancer features, but add no independent prognostic power

Cancer Diagnosis & Prognosis published a peer-reviewed retrospective tissue study of 163 people who underwent radical cystectomy for muscle-invasive bladder cancer. Higher tumor Nectin-4 and cytoplasmic IRF6 expression were associated with lower stage and longer progression-free survival in unadjusted analyses, but neither independently predicted progression or cancer-specific survival after adjustment for stage. The markers may help frame research hypotheses, not guide treatment yet. Single-center design, archival tissue, observational associations and limited independent prognostic value are major limitations. Published online November 1, 2026.

NEW FREE JOURNAL ARTICLE · CERVICAL ESOPHAGEAL CANCER · NOVEMBER 1

Small retrospective series reports durable survival for some patients after definitive chemoradiotherapy

Cancer Diagnosis & Prognosis published a peer-reviewed single-institution study of 33 people with previously untreated cervical esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma who received curative-intent radiotherapy with platinum chemotherapy. Median overall survival was 58.2 months, while median progression-free survival was 12.1 months; 21 patients progressed, most with locoregional-only recurrence. The findings are useful for a rare cancer but cannot establish treatment superiority. The very small cohort, retrospective design, long treatment era and lack of a comparison group limit generalizability. Published online November 1, 2026.
